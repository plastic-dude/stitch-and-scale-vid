## Campaign decision

This is a new vertical-first campaign, not a crop of `spreadsheet-11pm`. The nearest prior concept is `spreadsheet-11pm`, which dramatized unpaid spreadsheet labor at 11 PM and used a Spec Sheet Lab proof surface. This concept changes all six required dimensions: **pain** changes from night-time spreadsheet labor to fear of losing or surrendering work; **hook** changes from “the math is not finished” to “your pattern should not need my server to stay yours”; **visual metaphor** changes from the late-night spreadsheet to a project held inside a local device; **proof surface** changes from a grading specification table to the visible `Local only` state, local-storage notice, and no-account workflow; **emotional turn** changes from invisible labor being respected to ownership becoming a deliberate product behavior with an honest warning; **final line** changes to “Keep the work on your machine. Then help us test what comes next.”

The campaign is deliberately conservative. It does not claim that cloud sync exists, that recovery is automatic, that data loss is impossible, or that the application has a complete backup system. The video must show the warning as part of the product truth rather than hide it.

## Pain and promise

**Pain:** A designer wants to begin a pattern without handing the project to a remote service, but local ownership also creates a responsibility: browser data can be cleared and the project can disappear.

**Private fear or cost:** Months of pattern decisions may be trapped in a browser session, or the designer may avoid a useful tool because the ownership model is unclear.

**Promise:** The viewer will understand exactly what the current public product surface says: the app starts locally, no account is required to begin, and the visible warning explains that clearing browser data deletes local projects. This is a truth check, not a promise of recovery.

**Emotional turn:** The warning is not treated as a footnote. It becomes evidence that the product is willing to show the cost of its local-first choice.

**Final image and line:** The clean `Local only` / `Saved` header remains visible while the narrator says, “Keep the work on your machine. Then help us test what comes next.”

## Production bible

**Visual grammar:** One idea per shot. Start with dark quiet space and a real product state, then move from the broad landing surface to close warning proof, then to the New Project setup. Use slow, deliberate push-ins or editorial crops on the supplied captures. Avoid kinetic UI animation that could imply behavior not shown. Keep the central proof unobstructed.

**Settings/arrangements:** Minimal black-green field matching the product’s dark interface. Product captures occupy the center 70% of the vertical canvas. Leave the lower caption band and the right-side platform interface margin empty. No physical set is required.

**Narrator:** One warm, close, unhurried narrator family; first-person founder voice. No alternating models, no doubled takes, no synthetic room-tone changes. Record the complete narration as one continuous voice treatment.

**Music/audio:** Prefer near-silence with a very low, rights-cleared sustained room tone. No beat under the warning or CTA. No sound effect may imitate a deletion, upload, or save action that is not shown.

**Editorial text:** Captions only, plus at most one final non-caption CTA card in a blank safe region. No decorative headlines over UI proof. No generated text pretending to be part of the product.
