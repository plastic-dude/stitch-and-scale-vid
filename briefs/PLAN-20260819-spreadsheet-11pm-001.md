## One-sentence concept

At 11:47 PM, the designer’s creative work is still alive but the grading math has taken over the room; a deliberately restrained transition reveals a current Stitch & Scale Spec Sheet surface where gauge, tolerance, yarn bill, and multi-size points of measure can be inspected without pretending a blocked quote-readiness state is finished.

## Pain and stakes

**Primary pain:** Unpaid spreadsheet labor and unstructured grading decisions consume the designer’s night.

**Private fear or cost:** A designer may lose the thread of the creative work, carry a stale or unexplained number into a pattern, or mistake a table full of sizes for a verified publication-ready result.

**Why this angle now:** Current craft sources describe grading as a barrier to publishing and show workflows built from size charts, body measurements, ease, gauge, finished measurements, and stitch/row calculations. They also describe the intimidation of a blank spreadsheet and the value of clearer visual relationships and fresh eyes. See `research/2026-08-19-first-director-research.md`.

**Truthful promise:** The video lets a designer see one current, inspectable Stitch & Scale proof surface and invites them to test the math. It does not promise perfect grading, universal automation, complete quote readiness, or a replacement for expert review.

**Final beat:** The creator’s honest outsider status becomes an invitation to craft experts: “I wrote the math. You tell me where it fails.”

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Decision |
