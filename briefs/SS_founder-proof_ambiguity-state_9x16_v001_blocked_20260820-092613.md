## Dramatic engine before camera

**Subject:** A fictional instruction review card containing one short ambiguous line.  
**Desire:** Let the maker identify the intended action without inventing the designer’s missing context.  
**Obstacle:** The same source line supports two plausible readings, and a neat “clear” check would hide the divergence.  
**Stakes:** A maker may proceed under a different interpretation; the video must not claim this happened to a Stitch & Scale user.  
**Change:** Move from `CLEAR: YES/NO` to `READING A / READING B / MISSING CONTEXT / DECISION UNRESOLVED`.  
**Contradiction:** The most honest state is not a defect badge; it is a preserved ambiguity awaiting the right human review.  
**Escalation:** The original line appears; two readings split; context fields appear; the expected result remains open; reviewer and next check remain unassigned.  
**Final image:** `ORIGINAL TEXT / READING A / READING B / MISSING CONTEXT / EXPECTED RESULT / EVIDENCE / REVIEWER / NEXT CHECK` with `STATUS: AMBIGUITY REPORTED / DECISION UNRESOLVED` and `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Treatment

Open on a clean desk with a paper card titled `INSTRUCTION REVIEW`. The narrator says, “If two makers read the same line two ways, the line is not finished.” A fictional line appears under `ORIGINAL TEXT` without a real pattern quote or technical instruction. It splits into `READING A` and `READING B`. A lower strip reveals `MISSING CONTEXT`, `EXPECTED RESULT`, and `EVIDENCE`. The final fields read `REVIEWER: UNASSIGNED`, `NEXT CHECK: TECHNICAL REVIEW`, and `STATUS: AMBIGUITY REPORTED / DECISION UNRESOLVED`. The narrator explains that an ambiguity note is not an automatic defect verdict and does not replace technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, or test knitting. The founder turns the paper away and says he will not pretend it is the app. The final card invites founding testers to identify what the current product can actually preserve about an unclear instruction.
