## Concept, distinctness, and research boundary

A file can be kept while its future return remains uncertain. The nearest prior angles are `retention-state` and `backup-state`, which concern storage/disposal duration and recoverable copies with restore testing. `archival-state` asks whether a retained record has a deliberate archive status, access path, metadata, integrity/fixity evidence, change history, format, search/retrieval, restore check, review, and decision. Hook: “Kept is not retrievable.” Fictional proof surface: `ITEM / ARCHIVE STATUS / ACCESS PATH / METADATA / FIXITY / CHANGE LOG / FORMAT / SEARCHABLE / RESTORE CHECK / REVIEWED / DECISION`. Emotional turn: “We kept the file” becomes “We can find and understand it later.” Final line: “Name what the archive can return.” Distinct from `deprecation-state`, `withdrawal-state`, `provenance-chain`, `commit-state`, `reproducibility-state`, and `portability-state`.

NARA describes digital preservation as preserving content while maintaining access and continued usability, with fixities, tracked actions, metadata, manifests/logs, validation, audits, monitoring, and restoration tests. W3C separates metadata, provenance, quality, versioning, identifiers, formats, access, and preservation. These sources support workflow education only; they do not prove that Stitch & Scale currently exposes archival state, retrieval, integrity, or long-term access.
