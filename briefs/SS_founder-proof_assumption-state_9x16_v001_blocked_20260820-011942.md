## Concept and claim boundary

> “An unstated assumption is an unreviewed input.”

A fictional result card appears without explaining what it assumes. An assumption card attaches to the result and names an input, value, basis, impacted output, check method, owner, review status, and disposition. The emotional turn is not that the result is wrong; it is that the result is **not yet responsibly interpretable** while the assumption is hidden or unchecked. The story is about a declared assumption, not product assumption tracking, data-quality certification, fitness-for-purpose assessment, provenance, yarn-weight interpretation, gauge, fit, or correctness.

NIST AI RMF 1.0 identifies assumptions and related limitations about system purposes, uses, and risks as matters to document across a product lifecycle [912]. W3C Data Quality Vocabulary provides a framework for describing data quality so users can make their own fitness-for-purpose judgment and includes measurements, annotations, policies, certificates, and provenance of quality metadata [913]. Craft Yarn Council labels yarn-weight gauge ranges as guidelines and says to follow the gauge stated in the pattern, supporting the distinction between a convenient category assumption and a checked project-specific condition [914]. These sources motivate the fictional assumption card and production review only; none proves that Stitch & Scale implements the AI RMF, DQV, assumption tracking, quality metrics, provenance, or craft calculation.

All inputs, values, bases, impacted outputs, check methods, owners, reviewers, statuses, and dispositions are fictional. Generated text is not UI truth. Exact product labels, values, warnings, and controls require a current approved product capture or proof-registry entry. David is a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not described as a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, technical editor, or tester.
