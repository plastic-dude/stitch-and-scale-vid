## Concept, distinctness, and research boundary

A review marker can exist without stating what was attested. The nearest prior angle is `review-expiry` or related review-state work, which asks whether review exists or remains current. `attestation-state` asks which claim was attested by whom or what, under which role, evidence, scope, date/expiry, policy, and verification result. Hook: “Reviewed is not attested.” Fictional proof surface: `CLAIM / ATTESTER / ROLE / EVIDENCE / SCOPE / ISSUED / EXPIRES / POLICY / VERIFIED`. Emotional turn: “Someone looked at it” becomes “What exactly was attested, by whom, and under which rule?” Final line: “Name what the review actually attests.” Distinct from `acknowledgement-state`, `authority-state`, `evidence-type`, `evidence-coverage`, `identity-state`, `schema-state`, and `consent-state`.

W3C VC 2.0 distinguishes a credential’s verifiability from the truth of its claims and requires verifiers to evaluate issuer, proof, subject, and claims against policy before relying on them. NIST’s identity-guideline landing page separates identity proofing, authentication, and federation, and notes that the linked revision is superseded. These sources support bounded assurance education only; they do not prove that Stitch & Scale currently exposes attestation, reviewer identity, cryptographic verification, or credential semantics.
