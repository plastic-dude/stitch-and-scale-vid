## Core creative distinction

The nearest prior is pass 254 `dependency-disclosure-state`. Pass 254 asks what an external pattern resource is for and how its source, scope, version, access, and review relationship should be named. Pass 255 asks whether the planned use is actually permitted after the source has been named.

| Dimension | Pass 254 `dependency-disclosure-state` | Pass 255 `attribution-boundary-state` |
|---|---|---|
| Changed pain | A support file can be attached without purpose, scope, version, access, or review context. | A source can be named and linked while permission, license, permitted use, channel, term, or modification remains unresolved. |
| Hook | “A file can be attached and still not tell you what it is for.” | “A credit line can be correct and still not give you permission to use the work.” |
| Visual metaphor | A dependency card naming a resource relationship. | A split `CREDIT` / `USE CHECK` card where acknowledgement fills first and rights remain unresolved. |
| Proof surface | Pattern version, resource, purpose, source, scope, last check, link label, access, alternate version, human dependency review, status. | Work, rightsholder/requested credit, source link, license or permission, allowed use, channel/term, modification, human rights review, status. |
| Emotional turn | Attachment-looking-complete becomes an explicit dependency boundary. | A tidy source line becomes visibly insufficient for a particular reuse decision. |
| Final line | “Name what the pattern depends on before you trust the link.” | “Credit the source. Clear the use. Keep the human gate.” |

The concept is not a noun-swapped duplicate of `credit-line`, `source-note`, `quote-permission`, `provenance-chain`, `permission-line`, `consent-state`, `privacy-by-default`, `dependency-state`, `support-boundary`, `cross-reference-state`, `locale-alignment-state`, `version-delta-state`, `false-completion`, or `release-gate-roster`. It makes the specific distinction between **acknowledging a work** and **being authorized to use it in a defined way**. It does not claim that Stitch & Scale currently provides rights management.

## Dramatic engine before camera

**Subject:** A fictional split paper card titled `USE CHECK` for one external pattern resource.
**Desire:** Let a designer or reviewer distinguish “who made this?” from “may we use it this way?” before publishing or sharing.
**Obstacle:** A credit line and source URL can exist while the license, permission, channel, term, modification, or rights owner remains unclear.
**Stakes:** A maker or publisher could mistakenly redistribute, modify, translate, display, or place external work in a campaign without confirmed scope. The film must not claim that this happened to a Stitch & Scale user.
**Change:** Move from a tidy source acknowledgement to an explicit human rights boundary.
**Contradiction:** “Credit given” can be true while “use cleared” is still unresolved.
**Escalation:** Work is named; creator is named; source link is named; license/permission remains blank; allowed use and channel/term remain blank; modification is separated; human review stops the card.
**Final image:** `CREDIT: RECORDED` beside `USE: UNRESOLVED`, followed by `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI` and the final line.

## Treatment

Open on a quiet desk. A cream paper card titled `USE CHECK` sits beneath a neutral pencil and blank folder. The left column is `CREDIT`; the right column is `USE`. The narrator says, “A credit line can be correct and still not give you permission to use the work.” The hand writes `WORK: SUPPORT PHOTO`, `RIGHTSHOLDER: SOURCE OWNER`, and `SOURCE LINK: ORIGINAL LOCATION` on the left. The right column stays visibly empty. A second card adds `LICENSE OR PERMISSION: NOT RECORDED`, `ALLOWED USE: UNRESOLVED`, and `CHANNEL / TERM: UNRESOLVED`. The narrator refuses to turn the blank into a green badge: “I don't want a tidy source note doing the job of a rights decision.” The card adds `MODIFICATION: HUMAN REVIEW` and `STATUS: HOLD`. The paper turns toward camera with `CREDIT ≠ CLEARANCE` and the fixed footer `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. The founder invitation asks testers to identify what evidence a product should preserve, not to trust a fictional rights feature.

## Visual grammar and production bible

Use one narrator family, one quiet tabletop, one cream/charcoal/ink-blue paper system, and a strict type hierarchy. The only readable words are the exact illustrative labels in this brief plus the fixed footer `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Do not render real pattern text, chart symbols, schematic measurements, photographs, URLs, creator names, license badges, legal notices, platform logos, customer records, tester identities, or product controls. Generated text is not UI truth.

Use one locked overhead camera and one 45-degree macro angle. Keep the split card centered but leave a declared caption-safe band above it. Film one field family per close shot rather than a dense rights table. Use practical hand placement and paper movement as the visual rhythm. No fake software interface, automatic rights scanner, copyright verdict, legal shield, competitor screen, or green clearance badge may appear.

The dramatic object must be legible at mobile size. Capture a clean plate, a credit-only state, a permission-unresolved state, a scope-unresolved state, and the final hold state. The proof card must be readable without captions. Caption-on and caption-free exports require separate review.
