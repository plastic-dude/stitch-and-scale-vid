## Concept, distinctness, and research boundary

A review stamp can exist without proving what was actually examined. The nearest prior angles are `review-expiry` and `attestation-state`, which concern review freshness and what a named attester attests. `audit-state` asks whether a defined audit procedure bounded by scope and criteria produced evidence, findings, exceptions, disposition, auditor/date, and review. Hook: “Reviewed is not audited.” Fictional proof surface: `AUDIT OBJECT / SCOPE / CRITERIA / PROCEDURE / EVIDENCE / FINDINGS / EXCEPTIONS / DISPOSITION / AUDITOR / DATE / REVIEWED`. Emotional turn: “Someone looked at it” becomes “We can show what was actually checked.” Final line: “Name what the audit actually examined.” Distinct from `acceptance-criteria`, `evidence-coverage`, `archival-state`, `provenance-chain`, `verification-state`, and `closure-state`.

NIST distinguishes functionality from assurance and warns against treating mappings as one-to-one equivalence without scope/intended-use analysis. Craft Yarn Council guidance emphasizes standards checks, technical editing as a second set of eyes, and tester feedback on clarity, symbol/chart alignment, materials, and finished result. These sources support workflow education only; they do not prove that Stitch & Scale currently exposes audits, compliance, certification, or independent review.
