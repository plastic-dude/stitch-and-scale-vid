## Research-informed distinction

Pass 196 asked whether an observed effect is fit for a named intended use. Pass 197 begins after that evidence packet appears complete and asks whether the resulting acceptance is authorized, bounded, attributable, and revisitable. NIST’s current Risk Management Framework separates assessment, authorization, and monitoring; GOV.UK’s check-answers pattern separates review from final confirmation; and Craft Yarn Council guidance separates project evidence, submission requirements, and recipient-specific acceptance. These sources inform the educational framing only. [1] [2] [3] [4]

> **Changed is not the same as fit for use; fit for use is not the same as authorized for this scope.**

## Founder and product truth

David is a developer whose late mother knitted. He does not knit and must not be described as a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, technical editor, or tester. He may explain why an evidence packet should name who can make a decision, what the decision covers, what remains limited, and when it must be revisited. Calculation, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, and test knitting remain distinct human evidence gates.

No current product-proof registry entry or approved, non-private, rights-cleared capture verifies a current authorization surface with an accountable role, decision scope, limitations, review trigger, or durable decision record. Every card is an original prop labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Do not invent current labels, controls, role permissions, audit history, approval workflows, release authority, suitability, usability, project-level classification, accuracy, outcomes, adoption, or capabilities.

| Hook | “Changed is not the same as fit for use.” | “Fit for use is not the same as authorized for this scope.” |

## Treatment and beats

The film opens on a neutral tabletop. An original card reads `EVIDENCE PACKET COMPLETE — ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`; beneath it sit separate neutral cards for `CALCULATION CHECK`, `TECHNICAL EDIT`, `PRINT REVIEW`, `CHART READABILITY`, `SCHEMATIC SCALE`, and `TEST KNIT`, each marked `HUMAN GATE — STATUS NOT SUPPLIED`. A hand reaches toward a large `AUTHORIZED` stamp and stops.

A second card asks `AUTHORIZED FOR WHAT?` The evidence packet is narrowed to an illustrative scope: `FOLLOW CHART 3 FOR SIZE M`. A third card asks `AUTHORIZED BY WHICH ROLE?`; it remains `UNCONFIRMED`. A fourth card exposes `LIMITATIONS` and `REVIEW_TRIGGER`; both remain incomplete. The final card records `DECISION_RECORD: NOT RECORDED` and `DISPOSITION: HOLD`. The film never shows a real product surface, customer record, approval, or outcome.

## Visual grammar and coverage

Use overhead and hands-only side-angle coverage, neutral paper, high contrast, sentence-case labels, and dedicated readable inserts for the scope, role, limitations, review trigger, record, and disposition cards. Capture a wide master showing evidence beside authorization, a macro insert for each dense card, a close shot of the human-gate boundary, a clean hold-state card, a caption-free proof pass, and a final disclaimer frame. Captions may not cover hands, cards, labels, numbers, logos, controls, menus, or the primary action.

Primary location is a quiet tabletop with neutral light and no private or branded material. Alternatives are a wall-mounted decision board, a no-hand version, or text-only original cards. Do not substitute a screenshot, copyrighted pattern, customer record, private message, logo, or current UI capture without approved proof and rights.

## Sound motif and audio plan

Use one approved narrator family only, restrained card movement, one stop sound when the `AUTHORIZED` stamp halts, and a neutral hold tone when role, review trigger, or decision record remains unknown. No unlicensed music. Record room tone before and after each setup. Reject mixed voices, doubled words, overlaps, room-tone jumps, clipped consonants, stale UI, unsupported claims, and effects that conceal a defect. Deliver caption-on and caption-free review versions. Human-review captions; automatic captions are only a draft.

## Risk register and acceptance gates

| Risk | Mitigation | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| The authorization card is mistaken for a current product control | Persistent illustrative disclaimer and no product styling | Product/truth gate |
| “Authorized” is read as universal approval | Narrow scope card and visible hold state | Claims/scope gate |
| A role card is mistaken for actual role-based access or a named customer approver | Use `UNCONFIRMED`; show no person, identity, or permission control | Founder/claims/privacy gate |
| Human gates are collapsed into a single approval | Keep calculation, technical editing, print, chart, schematic, and test-knit cards separate | Human-evidence gate |
| Review trigger looks like a product reminder feature | Use a blank illustrative calendar card and no UI chrome | Product/truth gate |
| Dense record becomes unreadable on mobile | Dedicated close inserts and phone review at reduced brightness | Visual/platform gate |
| Captions obscure evidence cards or hands | Caption-on and caption-free reviews with movable safe area | Caption gate |
| Decision record appears complete without rights, audio, or platform confirmation | Keep package blocked and rerun all pre-generation gates | Director gate |
