## Research decision

CYC advises confirming that a chosen yarn will be available for at least the next year, checking yarn/color and dye lot, keeping notes, and discussing a yarn or color problem with the manufacturer or editor.[268] CYC also frames its materials as base standards that do not replace company-specific requirements and notes that project submissions can involve company or magazine timelines and labeling.[269] Stitch & Scale’s contracts keep material trace, substitution, care, gauge, source/date, business records, technical editing, print/chart review, and test knitting separate.[270] [271] These sources support a time-stamped availability checklist, but do not prove Stitch & Scale monitors supply.

## Difference from nearest prior angle

The nearest prior angle is `material-trace`, which focuses on the identity of the yarn actually used in a project. `availability-status` changes the pain to **whether a material recommendation is being mistaken for a current supply fact**. The hook changes to **“Recommended is not available.”** The visual metaphor changes from material identity fields to **`RECOMMENDED YARN / AVAILABILITY BASIS / CURRENT AS-OF / DISCONTINUED OR SEASONAL / SUBSTITUTE STATUS / SOURCE / CONFIDENCE / REVIEW STATUS`**. The proof surface changes from a material-trace record to a current availability-context surface. The emotional turn changes from tracing what was used to time-stamping what is known about supply without a live-inventory or replacement promise. The final line changes to **“Date the availability before you name the yarn.”**

It is distinct from `substitution-basis` because it does not compare technical properties of a substitute; from `material-trace` because it does not record the lot of material actually used; from `before-download` because it does not provide a marketplace context; from `price-basis` because it does not classify a cost; and from imported `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster` because it is not completion or evidence-roster accountability. Neither imported concept is reopened, renamed, overwritten, or approved.
