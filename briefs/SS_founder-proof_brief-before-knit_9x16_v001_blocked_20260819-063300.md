## Research decision

A practitioner guide from A Bee In The Bonnet identifies onerous processes, inflexible yarn requirements, unclear or short timelines, and asking for the sample after knitting as test-knit red flags. It also notes that indie designers often do not pay testers while emphasizing support for the tester experience. A related guide distinguishes technical editing from test knitting and says tester groups can bring varied experience and language perspectives. These are practitioner sources, not universal labor standards, legal rules, compensation benchmarks, or proof of Stitch & Scale features.

The research changed the concept from a generic recruitment or tester-roster story to **expectation clarity before participation**. The film must not claim fair pay, paid work, tester success, a complete recruitment system, consent obtained, or an acceptable exchange.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect one verified tester brief or participation-record surface; it does not promise a tester marketplace, participant coverage, fair compensation, or completed samples.

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `feedback-loop`, which concerns what happens after an observation is received and whether a response or recheck path is visible. `brief-before-knit` changes the pain to **a maker being asked for work before scope and expectations are visible**; the hook to **“Before the first stitch, make the ask clear.”**; the visual metaphor from response states to a fictional tester brief with `SCOPE / TIME / YARN / SAMPLE / FEEDBACK`, followed by `ASK — CONSENT — RESPOND`; the proof surface from feedback/issue response to a current tester-brief or participation-record surface; the emotional turn from response accountability to informed choice before labor begins; and the final line to **“Make the ask clear before the first stitch.”**

It is distinct from `feedback-loop` because it is pre-participation scope, not post-observation response; from `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster` because it is not a release decision or roster; from `permission-line` because it is an operational tester brief rather than a rights boundary; from `sample-chain` because it is not physical custody; and from `event-window`, `material-notes`, `gauge-reality`, `reader-handoff`, `chart-parity`, `equivalent-meaning`, `readability-under-load`, `measurement-moment`, `decision-ledger`, `recovery-proof`, `evidence-over-output`, `inherited-thread`, `offline-continuity`, `correction-trace`, and `locale-drift` because the conflict is clarity before participation. It does not reopen, overwrite, rename, or consume the imported reservations.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on a fictional tester invitation card labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. It contains only neutral placeholders. Five blank tabs appear: `SCOPE`, `TIME`, `YARN`, `SAMPLE`, `FEEDBACK`. They fill with non-prescriptive prompts, not terms: `WHAT IS NEEDED?`, `HOW LONG?`, `FLEXIBILITY?`, `RETURN?`, `HOW TO RESPOND?`. A final card reads `ASK — CONSENT — RESPOND`, not `ACCEPTED`, `PAID`, or `APPROVED`.

The product insert is optional and blocked until a current, versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact tester-brief, participant, consent, scope, timeline, or participation-record labels. If absent, retain `TESTER-BRIEF PROOF REQUIRED — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Any real tester words, image, compensation detail, sample request, or consent record requires explicit permission and appropriate review. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Use one narrator family. Captions must not cover brief cards, hands, proof, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.
