## Concept, distinctness, and research boundary

A recorded number can be correct-looking while its reference, conditions, instrument, date, repeat, and review remain unknown. The nearest prior angle is `input-state`, which asks whether an entered value reflects intent. `calibration-state` instead asks what makes a measurement interpretable. The hook is “Measured is not calibrated.” The fictional proof surface is `MEASUREMENT / REFERENCE / CONDITIONS / INSTRUMENT / UNIT / DATE / REPEAT / REVIEWED`. The emotional turn moves from “I entered the number” to “I can explain how this number was obtained.” The final line is “Name what made the number meaningful.” It is distinct from `unit-state`, `tolerance-state`, `reproducibility-state`, `comparison-basis`, `absence-state`, and `evidence-type`.

NIST and BIPM define metrological traceability through documented reference and calibration chains, while NIST warns that traceability alone does not guarantee fitness for purpose. Craft Yarn Council describes gauge as stitches and rows per unit and recommends making a swatch and comparing it with the pattern gauge, with adjustments when the result differs. These sources support workflow education only; they do not prove that Stitch & Scale currently offers calibration, traceability, measurement accuracy, or any named surface. Do not imply ordinary knitwear work requires laboratory calibration or certification.
