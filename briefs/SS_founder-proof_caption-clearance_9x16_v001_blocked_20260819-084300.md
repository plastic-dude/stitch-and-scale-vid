## Research decision

W3C’s current WCAG 2.2 Understanding guidance says prerecorded synchronized media should provide captions and that captions include dialogue, speaker identification, and meaningful sound effects. Section508.gov distinguishes captions from subtitles and transcripts, warns that auto-captioning can omit speaker changes, punctuation, synchronization, and meaningful sound information, and recommends planning for captions rather than relying solely on auto-captioning. These sources do not prove Stitch & Scale conformance, product caption controls, platform safe zones, or automatic caption quality.

The research changed the concept from generic accessibility compliance into **caption placement as a proof-preservation decision**. The film must not claim WCAG conformance, universal accessibility, platform-safe zones, or automatic caption quality. It must require human caption review, one narrator family, caption-on and caption-free versions, and a clean proof frame before any generation request.

## Campaign assumption

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `equivalent-meaning`, which concerns preserving pattern meaning across screen readers, large text, spoken description, and non-visual access. `caption-clearance` changes the pain to **caption text covering visual proof in a demonstration video**; the hook to **“If the caption covers the stitch, you lost the proof.”**; the visual metaphor from alternate pattern semantics to a caption box moving away from hands and proof into a reserved lane; the proof surface from an accessible product/chart surface to one current product proof frame plus caption-on/caption-free review; the emotional turn from inclusive access through semantic alternatives to disciplined audiovisual composition; and the final line to **“Keep the words clear. Keep the proof visible.”**

It is distinct from `readability-under-load` because it is not mobile document density; from `paper-proof` because it is not screen-to-paper output; from `equivalent-meaning` because it is caption placement in video, not alternate pattern semantics; from `notation-key` and `sequence-map` because it is not pattern language; from `chart-parity` because it is not chart-versus-text equivalence; from `data-minimum`, `permission-line`, `support-boundary`, `feedback-loop`, `brief-before-knit`, `sample-chain`, `material-notes`, `care-context`, `design-intent`, `count-check`, `recovery-proof`, `false-completion`, `release-gate-roster`, `as-of`, `source-note`, `size-boundary`, `measurement-moment`, `gauge-reality`, `technique-profile`, `event-window`, `decision-ledger`, `evidence-over-output`, `inherited-thread`, `offline-continuity`, `correction-trace`, `locale-drift`, and `reader-handoff` because the conflict is caption clearance around audiovisual proof. It does not reopen, overwrite, rename, or consume the imported reservations.

The caption moves into a reserved lane, then the caption-free version appears beside the caption-on version. A green accessibility or conformance badge is rejected.

The product insert is optional and blocked until a current, versioned, non-private product proof frame is supplied with exact labels and a completed caption-clearance review. If absent, retain `PROOF FRAME REQUIRED — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Any real pattern page, chart, table, product UI, person, or founder voice/image requires source and rights review. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Use one narrator family and a deliberate speaker map if more than one person is ever authorized. Captions must not cover faces, hands, charts, tables, numbers, logos, buttons, menus, or primary actions. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.
