## Research decision

The Craft Yarn Council explains that care symbols communicate washing, bleaching, drying, ironing, and professional-care instructions, including temperatures and hand-washing indicators, while noting that source systems may express temperature in Celsius or Fahrenheit. The Woolmark Company gives material- and care-claim-specific washing, rinsing, and flat-drying guidance for wool garments. Neither source proves a universal care outcome, applies to every fiber or construction, or proves Stitch & Scale has care fields or recommendations.

The research changed the concept from generic garment-care advice into **care context plus human recheck**. The film must not claim wash safety, no-shrink performance, durability, care automation, or universal care guidance.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect one verified care/material/sample-record surface; it does not promise a care system, garment outcome, or automated instruction.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | A care symbol sits beside a sample whose fiber and source context are missing. |
| Desire | To know what the care instruction refers to before passing it to a maker or recipient. |
| Obstacle | A symbol can be detached from fiber, construction, source, substitution, or sample history. |
| Stakes | A care instruction without context can be misunderstood or overgeneralized. |

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `material-notes`, which concerns the material assumptions behind a yarn substitution. `care-context` changes the pain to **a care symbol or washing instruction detached from the fiber, sample, source, or substitution that gives it meaning**; the hook to **“A care symbol without its material context is only half a promise.”**; the visual metaphor from substitution cards to an unbranded care card, swatch, and sample note joined by `FIBER / SOURCE / CARE / RECHECK`; the proof surface from materials/gauge/substitution to a current care/material/sample-record surface; the emotional turn from substitution rechecking to responsible care communication; and the final line to **“Keep the care with the context.”**

It is distinct from `material-notes` because it is about downstream care communication rather than selecting or substituting yarn; from `gauge-reality` because it is not gauge or fabric behavior; from `sample-chain` because it is not physical custody; from `brief-before-knit` and `feedback-loop` because it is not tester participation or response; and from `false-completion`, `release-gate-roster`, `event-window`, `permission-line`, `recovery-proof`, `measurement-moment`, `decision-ledger`, `evidence-over-output`, `inherited-thread`, `offline-continuity`, `correction-trace`, `locale-drift`, `reader-handoff`, `chart-parity`, `equivalent-meaning`, and `readability-under-load` because the conflict is care meaning detached from material context. It does not reopen, overwrite, rename, or consume the imported reservations.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on an original unbranded care-card silhouette with abstract symbols and persistent `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. No ISO, ASTM, GINETEX, Woolmark, yarn-company, or garment-care logo is used. Beside it are a fictional swatch and cards reading `FIBER`, `SOURCE`, `SAMPLE`, `CARE`, and `RECHECK MATERIAL`. The card is not a care recommendation and does not depict a real garment.

The product insert is optional and blocked until a current, versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact care, material, source, blocking, washing, drying, or sample-record labels. If absent, retain `CARE PROOF REQUIRED — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Any real care instruction, fiber claim, care symbol set, garment, or brand mark requires rights and source review. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Use one narrator family. Captions must not cover symbols, cards, swatch, proof, hands, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.
