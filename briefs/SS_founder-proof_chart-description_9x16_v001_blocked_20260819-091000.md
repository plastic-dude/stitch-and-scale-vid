## Research decision

W3C says complex non-text content such as charts and diagrams may need short and long text alternatives that convey equivalent information through another modality. GOV.UK says the same point made by an image or chart can be placed in document text, or alt text can be supplied to the person converting/uploading the document. ONS guidance says essential information should be available in an accessible alternative because PDF-only content can be difficult for assistive technology. These sources do not prove Stitch & Scale has chart descriptions, tagged PDFs, or accessible exports.

## Difference from nearest prior angle

The nearest prior angle is `chart-parity`, which focuses on whether chart counts and written instructions match. `chart-description` changes the pain to **whether the visual chart’s meaning exists in an equivalent text or structured form for someone who cannot see the grid**. The hook changes to **“A chart is not explained by its title.”** The visual metaphor changes from two numeric representations matching to a visual grid beside **`WHAT THE GRID MEANS / HOW TO READ IT / TEXT ALTERNATIVE`**. The proof surface changes from chart parity to a current chart-description field, alt-text/long-description surface, accessible table/structured chart alternative, schematic text summary, or tagged-export proof. The emotional turn changes from confidence in numerical agreement to access to meaning without requiring sight. The final line changes to **“Describe the meaning, not just the picture.”**

It is distinct from `caption-clearance` because it is not caption placement; from `readability-under-load` because it is not visual density; from `paper-proof` because it is not physical print review; from `chart-parity` because it is not numerical agreement; from `artifact-status` because it is not copy identity; from `reader-handoff` because it is the semantic alternative itself; and from imported `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster` because it is not completion or roster accountability.
