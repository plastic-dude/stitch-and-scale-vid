## Research decision

Fresh research on Stitchmastery chart-writing guidance and Brooklyn Tweed chart-reading guidance changed the campaign toward **chart parity**. The sources support explaining chart-to-text relationships, repeat boundaries, legends, partial repeats, special stitches, shaping, no-stitch placeholders, and reading direction. They do not prove that Stitch & Scale currently compares chart and written instructions or guarantees semantic equivalence.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect the relationship between a chart and written instruction; it does not promise automated equivalence or replace technical editing and test knitting.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | One pattern repeat represented twice: once as a chart, once as written instruction. |
| Desire | To let readers choose chart or text without forcing them to reconcile hidden differences. |
| Obstacle | Repeat boundaries, partial repeats, special stitches, shaping, no-stitch cells, and start/stop directions can diverge between representations. |
| Stakes | Both versions can look polished while sending the reader through different work. |
| Change | The viewer moves from “we included both” to “do both actually say the same thing?” |
| Contradiction | Redundant-looking representations can create more trust only when they are genuinely equivalent. |
| Escalation | Chart → written line → boundary marker → special stitch/shape → current proof or honest blocker. |
| Final image | One current verified chart/text pair aligned at a readable repeat boundary, or an original illustrative pair labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. |

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `readability-under-load`, which focuses on legibility and cognitive load. `chart-parity` changes the pain to **semantic divergence between two representations of the same pattern**; the hook to **“Two ways to read it. One pattern still has to agree.”**; the visual metaphor from a busy page becoming clear to a chart and text line snapping into alignment; the proof surface from accessible-PDF or contrast behavior to a current chart/written comparison, repeat boundary, special-stitch, or export-preflight state; the emotional turn from relief through legibility to trust through equivalence; and the final line to **“Show me where the chart and the words agree.”** It is distinct from locale-drift, gauge-reality, correction-trace, reader-handoff, false-completion, and release-gate-roster.

## Treatment and visual grammar

Open with one original illustrative chart fragment and one written instruction line. A hand traces the repeat boundary on the chart, then the same boundary in text. A special stitch or shaping moment introduces tension; the frame aligns both only if proof is verified. Props must be original and marked `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. If a current approved product capture exists, show one exact chart/text pair at readable scale with version/date and visible labels in the manifest. Do not invent equivalence badges or automated comparison results.

Use symmetrical compositions, restrained cuts, and one narrator family. Captions must not cover chart cells, legend, written line, counts, hands, face, controls, or CTA. The visual climax is the boundary aligning, not a green “passed” badge.
