## Research decision

W3C’s current WCAG 2.2 guidance explains minimum contrast for text and images of text and why contrast matters for people with low vision or impaired contrast perception. Craft Yarn Council says stitch charts may supplement or replace words and warns that symbol variations require consulting the pattern key. These sources support contrast, key, resize, print, and non-color review, but they do not prove Stitch & Scale performs contrast analysis, non-color validation, accessibility certification, tagged-PDF export, or automatic chart-readability checks.

## Concept and dramatic engine

| Engine element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | A chart distinction is carried by a color that may disappear or weaken in print, grayscale, resize, or a changed screen. |
| Desire | To preserve the meaning of a chart symbol or line distinction across viewing conditions. |
| Obstacle | Color alone can hide a difference; symbols and keys can vary; image text can scale poorly. |
| Stakes | A reader may not perceive the intended difference; no actual user outcome is claimed. |

## Difference from nearest prior angle

The nearest prior angle is `chart-parity`, which focuses on whether chart and written instructions agree. `contrast-proof` changes the pain to **whether a distinction remains perceivable when color, print, screen, resize, or viewing condition changes**. The hook changes to **“If color disappears, does the stitch still exist?”** The visual metaphor changes from chart-versus-text comparison to **`COLOR / SHAPE / LINE / KEY / GRAYSCALE`**. The proof surface changes from parity evidence to a contrast check, non-color chart distinction, symbol/line-style legend, grayscale/print preview, accessible chart export, or chart-readability review record. The emotional turn changes from semantic agreement to visual meaning surviving a changed viewing condition. The final line changes to **“Make the distinction survive the color.”**

It is distinct from `chart-description` because it is perceivability and contrast rather than an equivalent text alternative; from `readability-under-load` because it is visual distinction across conditions rather than dense reading load; from `notation-key` because it tests whether symbol/line meaning survives rather than merely defining notation; and from imported `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster` because it is not completion or roster accountability. Neither imported concept is reopened, renamed, overwritten, or approved.

## Treatment and production bible

Open on a neutral tabletop with an original abstract chart card marked `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Two stitch areas are distinguished by color only. The card desaturates; the distinction weakens. New cards enter: `SHAPE`, `LINE`, `KEY`, and `GRAYSCALE`. The final card reads `DISTINCTION SURVIVES — REVIEW REQUIRED`.

A real Stitch & Scale chart, export, contrast record, or accessibility surface may replace the fictional card only if a current, approved, non-private capture or proof-registry entry verifies the exact surface, labels, product version/date, and rights to show it. If proof is absent, retain the blocker card and disclaimer. No real chart, pattern page, screenshot, or customer project may appear without scoped permission. Do not imply automated contrast analysis, non-color validation, accessibility certification, tagged-PDF export, or automatic chart-readability checks.

Use one narrator family. David may appear only with authorization and remains described as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Captions must not cover chart marks, proof cards, tables, numbers, buttons, menus, logos, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free versions. Generated text remains illustrative and cannot substitute for current product proof.
