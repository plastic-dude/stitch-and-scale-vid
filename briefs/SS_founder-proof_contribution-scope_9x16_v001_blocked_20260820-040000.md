## Research decision

U.S. Copyright Office text distinguishes copyright in each contribution to a collective work from copyright in the collective work and separately describes joint-work authorship; this is legal context, not a determination of any person’s status.[419] Craft Yarn Council describes an industry context with companies, publishers, editors/design directors, submissions, and accepted projects, but does not verify an individual’s role or credentials.[420] Internal contracts preserve contributor provenance, rights/privacy boundaries, export identity, and separate human evidence gates.[425] Current platform and caption guidance supports a short vertical master with human-reviewed captions.[421] [422] [423] [424] None of this proves current Stitch & Scale contribution/credit behavior.

## Concept and dramatic engine

| Engine element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | One person’s actual contribution separated from role, credit scope, consent, and publication status. |
| Desire | To credit help accurately without assigning a title, authorship, endorsement, or expertise that was not evidenced. |
| Obstacle | A short credit line can flatten different work into one implied claim. |
| Stakes | A person may be misrepresented or a real contribution may disappear; no real outcome is claimed. |
| Change | The fictional record adds contribution, role, credit text/scope, consent, publication status, source/date, owner, and notes. |
| Contradiction | A smaller credit can be more truthful than a grander title. |
| Escalation | Person → contribution → role → credit text/scope → consent → publication status → source/date → owner. |
| Final image | `CREDIT SCOPE VISIBLE — CONTRIBUTION NOT INFERRED`. |

## Difference from nearest prior angle

The nearest prior angle is `credit-line`, which focuses on whether a person or source appears in a credit line and how the credit is formatted. `contribution-scope` changes the pain to **a credit or role label implying more, less, or different work than the person actually contributed**. The hook changes to **“A credit is not a contribution.”** The visual metaphor changes from a credit line beside a source to a contribution card that separates person, actual contribution, role, credit text, credit scope, consent, and publication status. The proof surface changes from credit presence/format to contribution-boundary evidence. The emotional turn changes from giving credit correctly to avoiding accidental credential, authorship, endorsement, or labor claims. The final line changes to **“Name the contribution before you name the credit.”**

It is distinct from `permission-line` because it is not actor access; from `quote-permission` because it is not quote reuse; from `test-knit-coverage` because it is not size coverage; from `technical-profile` or `technique-profile` because it is not ability; and from `outsider-founder` because it is not founder identity. It does not reopen or reuse `false-completion` or `release-gate-roster`.

## Treatment and production bible

Open on original abstract cards marked `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`: `PERSON: A. / CONTRIBUTION: TESTED SIZE 4 SAMPLE`. A second card reads `ROLE: TEST KNITTER`, followed by `CREDIT TEXT: SIZE 4 TEST KNIT`, `CREDIT SCOPE: THIS PROJECT ONLY`, `CONSENT: NOT RECORDED`, and `PUBLICATION: HOLD`. The narrator refuses to expand a scoped contribution into “co-designer,” “author,” “editor,” “endorser,” or “expert.” A final card reads `CREDIT SCOPE VISIBLE — CONTRIBUTION NOT INFERRED`.

A current Stitch & Scale contribution capture may replace fictional cards only if a current, approved, non-private proof registry entry verifies person, project/artifact, contribution, role, credit text/scope, consent, publication status, source/date, owner, and rights to show the capture. A screenshot cannot prove authorship, ownership, credentials, endorsement, or consent; product/data and rights evidence are required. If proof is absent, retain the blocker card and disclaimer.

Use one narrator family. David may appear only with authorization and remains described as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Captions must not cover contribution cards, names, roles, consent, proof, hands, menus, logos, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free versions. Generated text remains illustrative and cannot substitute for current product labels or contributor evidence.
