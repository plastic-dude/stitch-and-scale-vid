## Research decision

The fresh wide research examined current pattern-errata practice and a 2024 human-computer interaction paper on knitting-pattern layout and validation. It changed the concept toward **correction traceability after first release**. The story is not false completion, tester ownership, reader interpretation, or broad publication labor. It is what happens when a known change must remain visible and trustworthy to the person using or maintaining the pattern.

The research does not prove that Stitch & Scale currently has public errata, version history, automatic validation, or update delivery. The film remains blocked until a current approved proof surface is supplied.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect correction traceability and identify where a reader or maintainer could still be looking at the wrong version. It does not promise error-free work, automatic correction, guaranteed delivery, or perfect history.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | One pattern correction moving from a discovered issue to a clearly identified next version or correction record. |
| Desire | To ensure a known change reaches the right reader without ambiguity. |
| Obstacle | A correction can live in a note, an old PDF, a message, or a memory while the reader still holds an outdated artifact. |
| Stakes | A reader may follow a superseded instruction or miss a correction; the film must not claim the product prevents this without proof. |
| Change | The viewer moves from “we fixed it somewhere” to “show which version carries the fix and what changed.” |
| Contradiction | A correction that exists but cannot be found is operationally close to a correction that was never made. |
| Escalation | Marked-up page → ambiguous old/new files → one explicit correction record or version proof → human review remains visible. |
| Final image | A current, readable, version/defect-history or publication-preflight surface with one change clearly identified. If absent, use an original neutral paper prop labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. |

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior concept is `reader-handoff`, which focuses on the reader’s first interpretation of a size, ease, schematic, chart key, or instruction. Imported `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster` focus on unresolved human gates and tester evidence ownership. `correction-trace` changes the pain to **post-release change visibility**; the hook to **“A correction nobody can find is still waiting to happen.”**; the visual metaphor from a handoff across a desk to a marked page traveling through versions; the proof surface from reader interpretation to a current version, defect-history, errata, or publication-preflight state; the emotional turn from empathy for a reader arriving cold to relief that responsibility has a visible trail; and the final line to **“Show me what changed, and which copy carries it.”** It is a new angle, not a renamed reader-handoff.

## Treatment and visual grammar

Open with a marked-up paper page and two files whose names are intentionally generic props: `old copy` and `new copy`. The prop must be clearly illustrative and must not resemble current Stitch & Scale UI. A pencil line crosses from the old page to the new page, but the correction remains visually incomplete until a current approved product capture is supplied. David’s voice frames the contradiction as a developer and honest outsider; he does not perform knitting or claim craft authority.

If product proof exists, show one version/defect-history or preflight surface at a time, with exact current labels, dates, identifiers, and status values. Do not invent a changelog, errata table, automatic update, or release badge. Do not show dense tables as the only proof. Captions must not cover pages, version text, dates, controls, hands, face, or CTA.

| Beat | Approx. time | Action | Proof purpose | Coverage |

| Trace forward | 38–44s | The marked page lands beside the current copy; CTA asks what changed and which copy carries it. | End with accountable next action. | Clear end card; no fake button or urgency. |

## Sound and audio plan

Use one original, rights-cleared pencil mark, paper turn, and a restrained page slide. One continuous narrator family with stable room tone. No music under the correction sentence. Produce caption-on and caption-free review versions. Human-review all captions against the final recording; automatic captions may be an editable starting point only.
