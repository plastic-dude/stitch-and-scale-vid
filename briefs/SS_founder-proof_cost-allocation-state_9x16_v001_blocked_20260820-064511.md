## Research-informed premise

The UCSF Controller’s Office cost-allocation guide says that when a non-payroll cost benefits two or more projects, the benefit and allocation methodology should be documented. It identifies the percentage charged, method or reasoning, supporting documentation, and approvals as required components, and recommends determining the basis before or concurrently with incurring the cost and reviewing it regularly. [1] This is a sponsored-research policy guide, not craft-business policy or Stitch & Scale proof.

IRS Topic No. 509 describes direct business expenses and allocation of indirect home expenses by a business-use proportion under its specific United States home-use rules. [2] The film must not discuss deductions, rates, eligibility, compliance, or tax outcomes. Craft Yarn Council’s submission guidance separates project labels, sample size, work notes, gauge, instructions, schematics, invoices, and return shipping around a design project, giving the story a craft workflow context without establishing a product ledger. [3]

QuickBooks documents assigning existing expense line items to projects, while Xero documents project identifiers, categories, receipts, tracking categories, and reconciliation. [4] [5] These public materials support only a narrow category contrast: assigning a cost to a project is not the same as preserving the original transaction, the allocation basis, the share, the evidence, and the review state for a shared cost. No superiority claim is permitted.

## Visual grammar and production bible

Use one quiet neutral tabletop, soft side light, restrained camera movement, and high-contrast original cards. The receipt must be fictional, without a real vendor name, invoice number, address, tax ID, bank detail, currency total that could be mistaken for a real transaction, or recognizable accounting software chrome. Use abstract values such as `AMOUNT: EXAMPLE` rather than real money figures. Every record card must display `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

Keep the source transaction card physically separate from the derived allocation cards. Use one field family per close shot: source transaction, beneficiaries, basis, share/evidence, reviewer/status. Do not show a dense full-screen table. Do not use a calculator animation, success chime, profit chart, tax form, deduction stamp, margin badge, or automatic percentage. Generated text is not UI truth.

The production bible locks the same receipt orientation, card order, hand direction, desk surface, wardrobe, narrator family, room tone, and light temperature across all takes. David may appear in a neutral developer frame or remain voice-only; he must not perform knitting or be framed as a craft practitioner. Physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, technical editing, and test knitting remain distinct human evidence gates and cannot be replaced by an allocation card.

Use one approved narrator family with a close, dry voice and consistent room tone. The sound motif is a pencil underline on `ORIGINAL_TRANSACTION`, a restrained card separation on `ALLOCATION_BASIS`, and a short paper hold when `STATUS: OPEN` appears. No music is required. If a paper cue is retained, caption it only when meaningful, for example `[paper stop]`, and keep it below narration priority.
