## Research decision

Craft Yarn Council’s current designer guidance says both stitch and row counts should be included and explains that pattern instructions include electronic submission in its publication context. Its pattern-reading guidance explains cast-on counts, rows, repeats, brackets, and parentheses. A Snickerdoodle Knits practitioner guide describes technical editing as checking accuracy, consistency, and clarity, including stitch-count numbers, mathematical relationships, dimensions against gauge and stitch/row counts, yarn quantities, photos/schematics, and written-versus-charted instructions. These sources do not prove a current Stitch & Scale count surface, arithmetic validator, grading checker, or technical-editing service.

The research changed the concept from generic math correctness into **human-facing count checkpoints**. The film must not claim arithmetic validation, grading correctness, error-free output, or automatic checking.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect one verified count/checkpoint/arithmetic/review surface; it does not promise that the product verifies numbers or pattern correctness.

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `sequence-map`, which concerns prerequisite, repeat scope, branch conditions, and what must happen before the next action. `count-check` changes the pain to **numbers being present without a meaningful pause-and-compare point**; the hook to **“A number becomes useful when you know when to check it.”**; the visual metaphor from `BEFORE / DO / REPEAT / THEN` to `START / WORK / COUNT / PAUSE` with a pencil checkpoint; the proof surface from sequence/dependency context to count/checkpoint/arithmetic/review context; the emotional turn from procedural orientation to deliberate verification ownership; and the final line to **“Give the number a checkpoint.”**

It is distinct from `sequence-map` because it is not action order; from `gauge-reality` because it is not gauge/sample correspondence; from `correction-trace` because it is not change history; from `decision-ledger` because it is not record type; from `measurement-moment` because it is not input context; from `chart-parity` because it is not chart-versus-text equivalence; from `paper-proof` because it is not screen-to-paper preservation; and from `false-completion`, `release-gate-roster`, `as-of`, `source-note`, `notation-key`, `equivalent-meaning`, `readability-under-load`, `size-boundary`, `material-notes`, `care-context`, `sample-chain`, `technique-profile`, `support-boundary`, `feedback-loop`, `brief-before-knit`, `permission-line`, `recovery-proof`, `design-intent`, `data-minimum`, `event-window`, `evidence-over-output`, `inherited-thread`, `offline-continuity`, `locale-drift`, and `reader-handoff` because the conflict is human checkpoint placement around counts. It does not reopen, overwrite, rename, or consume the imported reservations.
