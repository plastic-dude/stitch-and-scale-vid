## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the refreshed repository. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment through the free demo and early-access cohort remains the campaign objective. The film is a year-round pre-publication story, not a seasonal event announcement. It invites designers to inspect one current contributor-terms, asset-rights, attribution, or publication-credit surface if such proof is supplied; it does not promise that Stitch & Scale manages test knits, permissions, rewards, or credits.

## Research decision

Creative Commons recommends explicit attribution components and respect for a licensor’s requested identity or no-attribution preference. UK Government guidance separates permission, licences, limited-use terms, moral rights, and performer rights. Yarnpond demonstrates that test-call workflows can contain role, size, deadline, material, milestones, and reward fields, while Edie Eckman’s Q&A distinguishes test knitters, sample stitchers, and technical editors and notes that compensation practices vary. YouTube provides editable caption workflows; TikTok’s current page was dynamic and was not treated as detailed evidence. None of this proves Stitch & Scale has the corresponding product surfaces.

## Difference from nearest prior angle

The nearest prior angle is `brief-before-knit`, which focuses on scope, time, yarn flexibility, sample expectations, feedback path, and exchange before labor begins. `credit-line` changes the pain to **the publication boundary where a contributor’s work, image, name, and exchange can be conflated**. The hook changes to **“A credit line is not a permission slip.”** The visual metaphor changes from a pre-work brief to a contributor card splitting into **`ROLE / ASSET USE / CREDIT / REWARD OR PAYMENT — CONFIRM`**. The proof surface changes from a tester-brief or participation record to a current contributor-terms, asset-rights, attribution, or publication-credit surface. The emotional turn changes from informed choice before labor to respectful recognition and clear boundaries before publication. The final line changes to **“Name the permission. Honor the credit. Keep the terms.”**

It is distinct from `feedback-loop` because it is not about what happens after a note is received; from `test-knit-coverage` and imported `release-gate-roster` because it is not size coverage or roster accountability; from `sample-chain` because it is not physical location or custody; from `permission-line` because it focuses on contributor role, credit preference, asset use, and exchange in one pre-publication record rather than a generic share permission; from `data-minimum` because it is not person-level data restraint; from `support-boundary` because it is not issue ownership; and from imported `false-completion` because it is not a human gate that appears complete. Neither imported concept is reopened, renamed, overwritten, or approved.

## Treatment and production bible

Open on a neutral tabletop with a fictional paper-and-phone terms card labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. A line reading `TESTER WANTED` is too vague. It splits into four readable cards: `ROLE: TEST KNIT / SAMPLE / REVIEW`, `ASSET USE: ASK FIRST`, `CREDIT: PREFERRED NAME OR NONE`, and `REWARD / PAYMENT: CONFIRM`. A fifth line reads `TECHNICAL EDITING ≠ TEST KNITTING` to preserve the human-gate distinction.

A real Stitch & Scale screen may replace the fictional card only if a current, approved, non-private capture or proof-registry entry verifies the exact surface, labels, product version/date, and rights to show it. If proof is absent, retain the blocker card and its disclaimer. No contributor name, face, voice, sample, project photo, pattern page, private message, or tester data may appear without explicit permission and a recorded use scope. Do not treat a public credit, free pattern, discount, payment, or reward as universal practice or endorsement.

Use one narrator family. David may appear only with authorization and remains described as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Captions must not cover hands, proof cards, names, role labels, numbers, buttons, menus, logos, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free versions. Generated text remains illustrative and cannot substitute for current product proof.
