## Research-informed premise

The Craft Yarn Council’s current standards page describes shared guidelines and symbols for yarn, needle, hook, and pattern labeling, including weight, charts, abbreviations, project levels, body sizing, and care symbols. It asks users of its symbols in publications to provide a credit line, but it does not establish that every destination uses the same vocabulary or that software maps standards automatically. [1]

CYC’s current project checklist requires yarn symbols, needle/hook sizes in millimeters and U.S. sizes, unusual abbreviation definitions, measurements by size, charts, schematics, and destination-specific instructions, while saying companies may require additional or different instructions. [2] Knitty’s current June 17, 2026 guidance requires its latest style guide, a text-only pattern document, standard abbreviations, CYC sizing, imperial and metric measurements, individual image and chart files, a schematic, and an originality/rights statement. It says its work is edited to its style and that test knitting, technical editing, and publication remain separate stages. [3]

The W3C Publishing Accessibility Metadata Crosswalk provides a direct analogy: it maps terms across EPUB Accessibility, Schema.org, ONIX, and MARC 21; says the resource is guidance rather than imposed architecture; and includes terms with no direct equivalent or non-wholly-equivalent mappings. [4]

## Dramatic engine

| Engine element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | Fictional source and target vocabulary cards. |
| Desire | Move a pattern or project between standards without changing meaning silently. |
| Obstacle | Similar-looking labels hide different scope, units, size systems, symbols, or destination rules. |
| Stakes | A plausible mapping may be overread as exact equivalence, accessibility conformance, technical correctness, or acceptance. No real failure is claimed. |
| Change | A generic arrow becomes typed `EXACT`, `BROADER`, `NARROWER`, `CONDITIONAL`, `NO DIRECT EQUIVALENT`, or `UNMAPPED`. |
| Contradiction | A crosswalk can describe a mapping without certifying the underlying content or destination decision. |
| Escalation | Similar labels → generic arrow → mapping types → unmapped row → human review boundary. |
| Final image | `SOURCE / TARGET / MAPPING TYPE / EQUIVALENCE LIMIT / HUMAN REVIEW REQUIRED`. |

## Treatment, beats, and visual grammar

Use one unbranded tabletop, one source column, one target column, six fictional mapping chips, and one reviewer card. All cards are original illustrative props. Open on two labels that look like a match. A generic arrow is challenged; mapping types then reveal exact, broader, narrower, conditional, no-direct-equivalent, and unmapped states.

| Time | Beat | Image and action | Spoken idea | Evidence status |
