## Research-informed premise

Craft Yarn Council’s industry guidance says most magazines and book publishers work six months in advance, asks designers to reconfirm material availability and sample size before beginning, tells them to keep notes, and ends with a direction to complete the project on time. It also separates the actual project, gauge swatch, electronic instructions, schematic, invoice, and return shipping as distinct submission materials. [1]

Craft Yarn Council’s designer pitfalls list warns about missing records, incomplete instructions, missing charts or schematics, sending a garment before writing the pattern, and late submissions. It also identifies missing measurements, incorrect math, missing stitch counts, gauge mismatch, missing quantities, and unscaled schematics. [2]

Atlassian’s general acceptance-criteria guidance describes predefined, clear, concise, testable requirements or conditions that must be met for a task to be complete and accepted; it distinguishes completion conditions from purpose and emphasizes clarity, measurability, independence, testability, and review. It is not a Stitch & Scale product claim or a knitwear publication standard. [3]

> **A date is not a status.**

## Treatment and beats

The film opens on a fictional work card marked `ON TIME`. The camera pulls back to reveal request, commitment, dependency, block, completion, acceptance, and open-condition lanes. The stamp changes to `DATE BASIS: REVIEW REQUIRED`; distinct human gates remain visible. The final line names the date’s basis, dependency, and acceptance need without claiming that the product tracks or verifies them.
