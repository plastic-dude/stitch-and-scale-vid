## Research-informed premise

Craft Yarn Council’s current submission checklist tells designers to reconfirm yarn and color selection, check dye lots, reconfirm sample size, keep notes, and not rely on memory. It separates yarn labels, gauge swatches, instructions, schematics, invoices, return shipping, and recorded amounts. [1] CYC’s idea guidance distinguishes an idea, swatch/sketch/sample, completed project, acceptance, contract, and review, while noting that organizations have their own requirements. [2] Knitty’s current June 17, 2026 guidance separates technical editing, test knitting, charts, schematics, photographs, rights, and individual submission files. [3]

Atlassian’s DACI template provides a category example of recording decision roles—Driver, Approver, Contributors, and Informed—but roles do not prove a decision is correct. [4] CYC standards provide craft terminology and sizing context but are not a decision validator. [5]

These sources support a bounded record-design distinction: **an explanation is not a decision basis until observation, source, derivation, assumption, roles, and open questions are visible**. The film must not claim that a rationale is correct, that a decision is approved, or that the product evaluates evidence.

## Founder and product truth

David is a developer whose late mother knitted. He does not knit and must not be described as a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, technical editor, or tester. If he appears, he speaks as a developer explaining why decisions should expose their basis; he does not claim accounting, legal, publishing, translation, or craft-practitioner authority.

No current product-proof registry entry or approved, non-private, rights-cleared capture verifies that Stitch & Scale exposes a decision-basis surface. Every proposed card is a fictional prop and must display `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

Do not claim that Stitch & Scale evaluates evidence, calculates correctness, recommends decisions, records approvals, assigns DACI roles, replaces technical editing, proves test-knit success, gives financial or tax treatment, or establishes publication readiness. Preserve technical editing, calculation, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, care review, test knitting, and publication approval as separate human evidence gates.

The decision card must be abstract and contain no real pattern title, test-knit result, customer record, publication decision, invoice, private message, logo, or current UI. Every card must display `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

Keep `DECISION_PURPOSE`, `OBSERVATION`, `SOURCE_OR_REFERENCE`, `DERIVATION_OR_FORMULA`, `ASSUMPTION`, `DECISION_OWNER`, `APPROVER_OR_GATE`, `CONTRIBUTORS`, `INFORMED`, `OPEN_QUESTION`, `DECIDED_AT`, `STATUS`, and `LIMITATIONS` as separate field families. Do not show a green `CORRECT`, `APPROVED`, `PUBLISHED`, `TAX READY`, or `DONE` badge. Do not imply that a role record proves the decision or that a source link proves the conclusion. Generated text is not UI truth.

Lock card orientation, typography, tabletop, hand direction, narrator family, room tone, and light temperature across takes. David may appear in a neutral developer frame or remain voice-only; he must not perform knitting or be framed as a craft practitioner. Technical editing, calculation, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, care review, test knitting, and publication approval remain distinct human evidence gates.

Record room tone before and after each setup.
