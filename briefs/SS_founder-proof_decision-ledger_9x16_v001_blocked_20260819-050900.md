## Research decision

Fresh research on IRS recordkeeping and SBA small-business management guidance changed the concept from a generic “price your work” story to a **truth-state decision story**. Good records help a business monitor progress, identify income and expenses, and support later statements; that does not mean a projected break-even or rollup is a settled transaction. The campaign therefore dramatizes the separation of `recorded`, `derived`, `estimated`, `planned`, and `unverified` states without using invented numbers, tax advice, revenue claims, profitability claims, or final pricing.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect a record-state workflow; it does not promise accounting, tax compliance, automatic reconciliation, profitability, or a pricing recommendation.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | A maker pauses before committing to the next sample or release decision and separates what was recorded from what is calculated or planned. |
| Desire | To make the next decision with an honest view of the work already documented. |
| Obstacle | A single total can visually collapse a real expense, a derived rollup, a projection, and an assumption into one misleading number. |
| Stakes | A designer can act on a projection as if it were a fact, lose the source event, or forget what still needs human judgment. |
| Change | The viewer moves from “show me the total” to “show me what kind of value this is and what source event supports it.” |
| Contradiction | A trustworthy business record is useful because it refuses to pretend an estimate is a receipt. |
| Escalation | Next decision approaches → source event appears → derived value is separated → estimate is labeled → human chooses whether to proceed. |
| Final image | One current approved record/ledger/export surface beside a physical note reading `NEXT DECISION`, or an original illustrative prop labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. |

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `offline-continuity`, which concerns work remaining usable and honestly labeled when network access disappears. `decision-ledger` changes the pain to **epistemic classification before a business decision**; the hook to **“Before you price the next step, know what kind of number you’re looking at.”**; the visual metaphor from connected/local/pending device state to separate record cards moving from source event to derived rollup to labeled estimate; the proof surface from offline/freshness/synchronization to a current design-ledger, expense, sales-rollup, estimate, or export surface; the emotional turn from calm resilience under signal loss to calm agency through explicit truth states; and the final line to **“Show me what I know before I decide.”**

It is distinct from `correction-trace` because it is not about post-release changes; from `gauge-reality` because it is not about material behavior; from `readability-under-load` and `chart-parity` because it is not about representation legibility or semantic equivalence; from `locale-drift` because language is not the conflict; from `reader-handoff` because the decision is the designer’s record interpretation; and from imported `false-completion` / `release-gate-roster` because it is not a generic human release gate or roster.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

The opening is a quiet worktable. A hand approaches one undifferentiated total on an original neutral prop. The narrator asks what the number actually represents. The prop divides into four cards: `RECORDED`, `DERIVED`, `ESTIMATED`, and `PLANNED`. Those labels are illustrative and must never be styled as current product UI. The camera stays close, with one information family per shot and no decorative graphs, green approval badges, or celebratory profit language.

Use one narrator family: a single calm off-camera voice, with David appearing only if his likeness and exact founder disclosure are authorized. If David appears, say only that he is a developer whose late mother knitted; never call him a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. The product insert is optional and blocked until a current, versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact labels. Captions must not cover the record surface, numbers, cards, hands, face, primary action, or CTA. Review both caption-on and caption-free cuts.

The production bible requires a clean device fixture with no private data, original or licensed table/hand/sample assets, an asset manifest, narrator release, music and room-tone provenance, current product version/date, exact UI-label transcription, and a rights ledger. No external archive, upload, or generation is authorized while blocked.

## Exact script

**0–7s — Narrator:** “Before the next decision, pause on the number.”

**7–15s — Narrator:** “What was actually recorded—and what did we calculate from it?”

**15–25s — Narrator:** “Recorded is not derived. Derived is not estimated. Planned is not paid.”

**25–33s — Narrator:** “A useful record does not make the choice for you. It makes the state of the choice harder to confuse.”

**33–41s — Narrator:** “If Stitch & Scale can show that clearly, show the current surface. If it cannot, keep the claim open.”

**41–46s — Narrator:** “Show me what I know before I decide.”
