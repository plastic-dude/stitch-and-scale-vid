## Concept, distinctness, and research boundary

An item can be discouraged without being visibly gone. The nearest prior angle is the occupied `supersession-state`, which asks for an explicit predecessor/current replacement relationship. `deprecation-state` asks how a workflow communicates that an item is no longer recommended for new use: reason, effective date, affected scope, replacement, migration, historical link, review, and decision. Hook: “Discouraged is not gone.” Fictional proof surface: `ITEM / STATUS / DEPRECATED-AT / REASON / AFFECTED SCOPE / REPLACEMENT / MIGRATION / HISTORICAL LINK / REVIEWED / DECISION`. Emotional turn: “It is discouraged” becomes “The workflow knows what not to start next.” Final line: “Name what should not start again.” Distinct from `version-lock`, `identity-state`, `withdrawal-state`, `review-expiry`, `closure-state`, `publication-readiness`, and `portability-state`.

W3C version-management guidance distinguishes current/latest/canonical and dated historical versions and lists obsolete, superseded, rescinded, retired, and outdated states. Craft Yarn Council describes pattern-quality concerns and review across production from designer through editing/printing, while noting company-specific requirements. These sources support workflow education only; they do not prove that Stitch & Scale currently exposes deprecation, retirement, or migration controls.
