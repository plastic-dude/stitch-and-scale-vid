## Dramatic engine before camera

**Subject:** A fictional `DEVICE EXIT` card, blank removable-drive-shaped prop, and neutral device silhouette.
**Desire:** Leave a device while retaining evidence that the record remains owned, readable, and inspectable.
**Obstacle:** A local copy, export button, or file presence does not by itself prove format readability, fixity, destination, backup, or restoration.
**Stakes:** A designer may believe the work is safe because it is present on one device. The film must not claim this happened to a Stitch & Scale user.
**Change:** Move from possession to a named exit checklist and human confirmation.
**Contradiction:** Local-first ownership increases agency but also makes responsibility visible.
**Escalation:** Local copy; export; format/version; checksum/fixity; readability test; backup state; restore test remains separate; human confirmation.
**Final image:** `LOCAL COPY → EXPORT → READABILITY TEST → HUMAN CONFIRMATION`, with `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Treatment

Open on a quiet cream desk. A paper card titled `DEVICE EXIT` sits beside an unlabeled removable-drive-shaped prop and a neutral device silhouette with no screen. The narrator says, “Having the file is not the same as knowing you can leave the device.” The hand writes `LOCAL COPY: PRESENT` and places a small marker beside it. The card advances to `EXPORT: CREATED`, then `FORMAT: NAMED`, `VERSION: RECORDED`, and `CHECKSUM / FIXITY: CHECKED`. A second strip asks `CAN YOU OPEN IT?` and the hand writes `READABILITY TEST: HUMAN CHECK`. The final strip reads `BACKUP STATE: SEPARATE`, `RESTORE TEST: SEPARATE`, and `HUMAN CONFIRMATION: PENDING`. The narrator refuses a green safety badge: “Own the copy. Test the exit.” The final frame reads `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. The founder invitation asks founding testers what evidence they would need before leaving a device; it does not claim a finished export, backup, or recovery feature.
