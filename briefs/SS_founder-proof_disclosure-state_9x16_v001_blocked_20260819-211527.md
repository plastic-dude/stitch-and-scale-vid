## Concept, distinctness, and research boundary

A record can be visible without being cleared to leave the room. The nearest prior angle is `consent-state`, which asks whether permission exists or is valid. `disclosure-state` asks what is actually shown, to whom, for what purpose, through which channel, and for how long. Hook: “Visible is not shareable.” Fictional proof surface: `AUDIENCE / PURPOSE / FIELDS SHOWN / FIELDS REDACTED / RECIPIENT / CHANNEL / EXPIRY / REVIEWED`. Emotional turn: “I can see it” becomes “I know who may receive it.” Final line: “Name what left the room.” Distinct from `withdrawal-state`, `exclusion-state`, `authority-state`, `identity-state`, `context-state`, and `accessibility-state`.

NIST frames privacy as a risk-management question for protecting individuals; W3C DPV models purposes, processing operations, context, data source, status, rights, risks, and technical/organizational measures. These sources support workflow education only. They do not prove that Stitch & Scale currently offers disclosure controls, consent, redaction, sharing safeguards, expiry, privacy compliance, or safe screenshots/exports.
