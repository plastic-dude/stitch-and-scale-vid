## Founder and product truth

David is a developer whose late mother knitted. He does not knit and must not be described as a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, technical editor, or tester. He may explain why a trustworthy workflow should name the next action clearly. Calculation, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, and test knitting remain distinct human evidence gates.

No current product-proof registry entry or approved, non-private, rights-cleared capture verifies a task list, guided next action, glossary, persistent location, status semantics, mobile navigation surface, or user research outcome. Every card is an original prop labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. No current Stitch & Scale labels, controls, numbers, warnings, menus, or capabilities may be invented.

## Dramatic engine

| Engine element | Design |
|---|---|
| Subject | A craft-production task that exists somewhere in a crowded set of valid tasks. |
| Desire | Find the next action and understand what its status means without remembering the whole workflow. |
| Obstacle | The needed task is present but hidden among vague labels, mixed statuses, or a status treatment that looks clickable. |
| Stakes | Time and attention are spent hunting; the film must not claim a specific lost deadline, error, or outcome. |
| Change | The task becomes findable through a purpose label, order rule, honest status, hint, location, and explicit next action. |
| Contradiction | A complete list can still fail to orient a person. |
| Escalation | Crowded cards → misleading status → search/hunt → one card surfaced with a clear action contract. |
| Final image | One unobstructed card reading `NEXT: REVIEW CHART KEY — STATUS: INCOMPLETE — OPEN HERE`, with the illustrative disclaimer visible on a separate frame. |

## Treatment

The film opens on an overhead table covered with six neutral cards: `CALCULATE`, `TECHNICAL EDIT`, `PRINT REVIEW`, `CHART READABILITY`, `SCHEMATIC SCALE`, and `TEST KNIT`. A hand searches for the next action. The cards are all present, but one is buried beneath a status chip that resembles a button. A second card has a vague label, `CHECK IT`, with no purpose. The narrator says that existence is not findability.

The table is reset. Each card receives one short purpose label and a truthful status. A narrow guide strip states `ORDER: FLEXIBLE — VERIFY WITH WORKFLOW OWNER`; a contrasting example states `ORDER: FIXED — FOLLOW SEQUENCE`. One card gains a short hint and an explicit location: `CHART KEY — BEFORE ROW 1`. The final shot shows the next action where the work is, without green completion theater or a claim that software created the evidence.

## Visual grammar and coverage

Use overhead and hands-only side-angle coverage, neutral paper, high contrast, short labels, and long enough holds for mobile reading. Capture a wide table master, macro inserts for each dense card, a status-only insert, a caption-free proof pass, and a clean final card. Never show a fabricated app screen. Captions may not cover hands, cards, labels, numbers, buttons, menus, or the primary action. If the proof card occupies the lower safe area, move captions to an empty upper area or use a separate reaction shot.

## Locations, arrangements, and alternatives

Primary location is a quiet tabletop with neutral light and no private or branded material. Alternative is a plain wall-mounted card arrangement if the table becomes visually dense. If rights or privacy conditions change, use text-only original cards. Do not substitute a screenshot, customer record, copyrighted pattern, or current UI capture without approved proof and rights.
