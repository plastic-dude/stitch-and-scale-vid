## Concept, distinctness, and research boundary

Two outputs can each look finished while drifting apart from the same source. The nearest prior angle is `consistency-state`, which concerns internal agreement across one record’s fields and derived views. `divergence-state` asks whether two derived outputs preserve or depart from their shared source across version/view/export context, and whether that difference is reviewed and given a disposition. Hook: “Derived is not identical.” Fictional proof surface: `SOURCE / DERIVATION / VERSION / VIEW / OUTPUT A / OUTPUT B / DIFFERENCE / REVIEWED / DISPOSITION`. Emotional turn: “Both look finished” becomes “They no longer agree with the same source.” Final line: “Name what changed between the outputs.” Distinct from `reconciliation-state`, `dependency-state`, `version-lock`, `provenance-chain`, `portability-state`, `schema-state`, and `scenario-state`.

W3C PROV-DM distinguishes entities, activities, generation, usage, derivation, revision, and invalidation. NIST SP 800-128 provides bounded configuration-management context for managing and monitoring configurations, baselines, and change history. The Craft Yarn Council official result describes schematics as representing size and shape and indicating relevant measurements. These sources support bounded provenance, change-review, and craft-context education only; they do not prove that Stitch & Scale currently compares, synchronizes, or reviews derived outputs.
