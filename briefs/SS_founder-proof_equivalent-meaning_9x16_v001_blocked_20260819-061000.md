## Research decision

W3C WCAG 2.2 says non-text content needs a text alternative that serves an equivalent purpose and identifies related guidance for captions, audio description, contrast, text resizing, reflow, keyboard access, and more. A CHI study of disabled knitters interviewed 16 experienced disabled knitters and analyzed 20 forum threads; its abstract reports continuing accessibility concerns and argues for tools that support pattern generation/modification and progress tracking. The ACM page was captcha-blocked, so only the extracted abstract was used. Neither source proves Stitch & Scale conformance or any current accessibility feature.

The research changed the direction from a generic accessibility promise to **equivalent meaning plus disabled-maker review**. The film must not claim WCAG compliance, screen-reader support, universal accessibility, or that one text alternative replaces a chart.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites makers to inspect one verified accessible chart, description, or semantic pattern surface and asks for disabled-maker review; it does not promise that the product serves every access need.

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `chart-parity`, which concerns whether a visual chart and written instructions stay semantically aligned. `equivalent-meaning` changes the pain to **pattern meaning being unavailable across different access modes**; the hook to **“If the chart changes, the meaning must not disappear.”**; the visual metaphor from two pattern representations to one chart tile transforming into large text, spoken description, and a focused keyboard path; the proof surface from chart/written parity to one current accessible chart, description, or semantic pattern surface; the emotional turn from parity confidence to inclusive participation through specific review; and the final line to **“Keep the meaning within reach.”**

It is distinct from `readability-under-load` because it is not density or mobile scanning; from `chart-parity` because it centers access mode and equivalent purpose rather than representation equality; from `measurement-moment` because it is not numeric capture; and from `false-completion` / `release-gate-roster` because it is not a generic human release gate. It does not reopen, overwrite, rename, or consume the imported reservations.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on a fictional chart tile with a persistent `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI` label. The tile contains abstract stitch symbols, not a usable pattern, private data, or copyrighted chart. The visual tile then becomes three illustrative possibilities: large text, a concise spoken-description line, and a keyboard-focus path. These must not be presented as a complete accessibility solution or current product controls. A question card reads `EQUIVALENT PURPOSE?` rather than `ACCESSIBLE` or `WCAG PASS`.

The product insert is optional and blocked until a current, versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact text alternatives, semantic labels, keyboard/focus behavior, or accessible chart/description surface. If absent, retain `ACCESSIBILITY PROOF REQUIRED — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Any product claim requires review by disabled makers who use the relevant mode; do not speak for every disabled maker. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Use one narrator family. Captions must not cover chart symbols, text, focus path, proof, hands, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.

## Rights, captions, audio, and platform plan

Use original abstract chart tile, paper cards, neutral device fixture, hands, and table. No real pattern symbols, copyrighted charts, private data, third-party accessibility badges, WCAG logos, assistive technology product marks, or identifiable makers without permission. Any disabled-maker reviewer voice/image requires informed consent and a clear scope; the safest blocked treatment uses a written `REVIEW WITH MAKERS` card. David’s voice/image requires authorization. Audio must be original or licensed.

Use one narrator family. Sound motif: one soft paper/grid movement, a restrained voice, and room tone; no triumph chime, accessibility-as-inspiration cue, or simulated screen-reader voice presented as a person’s experience. Human-review captions sentence by sentence and provide caption-on and caption-free versions. Captions may not cover chart symbols, text, focus path, proof, hands, or CTA. Platform requirements remain validation gates.
