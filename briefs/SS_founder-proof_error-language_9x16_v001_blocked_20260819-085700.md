## Research decision

The current GOV.UK Design System error-message guidance says an error message should explain what went wrong and how to fix it, preserve the user’s entered information, associate the message with the relevant field, use clear and concise plain language, and avoid vague or blame-oriented wording. The Home Office User-Centred Design Manual similarly recommends clear descriptive text, cause and location, corrective guidance, text rather than color alone, and review/correct/confirm flows. These sources do not prove Stitch & Scale validation, error detection, error summaries, or a universal craft QA message standard.

The research changed the concept from generic error handling into **constructive inspection language around uncertain pattern QA**. The film must not claim automated detection, correctness, validation, error-free output, or a resolved defect.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect one verified warning, QA status, validation summary, or corrective-guidance surface; it does not promise that the product has detected an error or knows who is responsible.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | A warning says something is wrong but does not say what to inspect next. |
| Desire | To understand the issue, keep the original context, and choose a responsible next action. |
| Obstacle | Generic `ERROR` language can hide location, cause, scope, and uncertainty. |
| Stakes | A designer may repeat work, blame the wrong source, or move forward without a human review; no product failure or outcome is claimed. |
| Change | The viewer moves from a red label to an inspection path: `INSPECT / COMPARE / DECIDE`. |
| Contradiction | A warning can be specific and useful without proving the underlying pattern is wrong. |
| Escalation | Generic `ERROR` → context returns → field/source named → inspection prompts → human-review boundary → current proof or blocker. |
| Final image | A fictional warning card reads `INSPECT / COMPARE / DECIDE`, with `HUMAN REVIEW` below and no resolved badge. |

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `count-check`, which concerns placing meaningful stitch and row checkpoints around numbers. `error-language` changes the pain to **a warning that says something is wrong without saying what to inspect next**; the hook to **“A warning should tell you what to inspect, not who to blame.”**; the visual metaphor from `START / WORK / COUNT / PAUSE` to a generic `ERROR` card transforming into `INSPECT / COMPARE / DECIDE` while preserving the source context; the proof surface from count/checkpoint/arithmetic/review to warning, QA status, validation summary, error-summary, or corrective-guidance surface; the emotional turn from deliberate checkpoint ownership to constructive, non-accusatory inspection; and the final line to **“Name the next inspection.”**

It is distinct from `count-check` because it is warning language and ownership, not count placement; from `correction-trace` because it is not history of a change; from `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster` because it does not close a release gate; from `feedback-loop` because it does not track tester responses; from `support-boundary` because it does not route a question; from `sequence-map` because it does not describe instruction dependency; from `caption-clearance` because it is not video caption placement; and from `hands-busy`, `measurement-moment`, `gauge-reality`, `material-notes`, `care-context`, `sample-chain`, `technique-profile`, `equivalent-meaning`, `readability-under-load`, `paper-proof`, `chart-parity`, `notation-key`, `as-of`, `source-note`, `design-intent`, `data-minimum`, `size-boundary`, `event-window`, `permission-line`, `recovery-proof`, `decision-ledger`, `evidence-over-output`, `inherited-thread`, `offline-continuity`, `locale-drift`, `reader-handoff`, and `brief-before-knit` because the conflict is the wording and ownership boundary of an uncertain warning. It does not reopen, overwrite, rename, or consume the imported reservations.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on an original fictional warning card labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`: a red `ERROR` word sits beside an unlabeled `Something is wrong` message. The card retains the source context as `ROW 4 / SIZE M / CHART A` but uses fictional identifiers. The red word dissolves into three plain-language cards: `INSPECT: ROW 4`, `COMPARE: CHART A`, and `DECIDE: HUMAN REVIEW`. A green `VALIDATED` or `FIXED` badge is rejected.

The product insert is optional and blocked until a current, versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact warning, QA, validation-summary, error-summary, or corrective-guidance labels. If absent, retain `WARNING PROOF REQUIRED — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Any real product UI, pattern page, chart, count table, person, or founder voice/image requires source and rights review. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Use one narrator family. Captions must not cover warning text, source context, proof, buttons, menus, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.

## Exact script

**7–15s — Narrator:** “Name the place. Keep the context. Say what is still unknown.”

**15–25s — Narrator:** “Inspect the row. Compare the source. Decide who needs human review.”

**25–31s — Narrator:** “Specific language can guide a check. It cannot settle the pattern.”
