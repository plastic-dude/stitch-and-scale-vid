## Research decision

Fresh research on Craft Industry Alliance’s current resource index and Modern Daily Knitting’s 2026 community-event page changed the concept from generic launch urgency to **event-window discipline**. Craft businesses plan across business, events, marketing, legal, productivity, and production concerns. The MDK example names a start date and a goal while explicitly softening the finish deadline. Neither source proves that an event creates demand, that Stitch & Scale has event scheduling, or that a deadline authorizes skipping human evidence.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The event is contextual rather than a promised campaign or sales driver. The brief uses an illustrative date card unless a current approved product or event surface is supplied.

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | A designer sees a fixed event date and must decide what can honestly be ready without collapsing human evidence gates. |

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `decision-ledger`, which concerns distinguishing recorded, derived, estimated, and planned values before a business decision. `event-window` changes the pain to **a fixed event date pressuring a slower evidence workflow**; the hook to **“The date can be fixed. The proof cannot be rushed.”**; the visual metaphor from truth-state record cards to a calendar page beside distinct human evidence cards; the proof surface from a business-record state to one current planning, submission, or release-context surface; the emotional turn from calm agency about numbers to calm discipline under a real calendar; and the final line to **“Keep the date. Keep the evidence.”**

It is distinct from imported `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster`: those concepts concern generic or rostered human release gates and remain blocked with occupied filenames; this concept concerns event context and deadline pressure, not a roster. It is also distinct from `correction-trace`, `reader-handoff`, `locale-drift`, `gauge-reality`, `readability-under-load`, `chart-parity`, `offline-continuity`, and `inherited-thread` because the conflict is seasonal timing versus evidence, not version history, audience handoff, language, fabric, legibility, representation parity, connectivity, or founder provenance.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on an original neutral calendar card with an illustrative event date; its label must read `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. A hand places it beside five separate cards: `TECHNICAL EDIT`, `PHYSICAL PRINT REVIEW`, `CHART READABILITY`, `SCHEMATIC SCALE`, and `TEST KNIT`. These are human evidence gates, not current product controls. Do not turn them into a green checklist, roster, or automatic release status. The camera gives each dense card a readable close-up and never allows captions to cover labels, tables, numbers, hands, or primary actions.

Use one narrator family: a single calm voice. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. A current product insert is optional and blocked until a versioned, non-private proof capture verifies the exact planning/release surface. If absent, retain the blocker card. No event organizer logos, copyrighted pattern pages, private submission details, or unlicensed festival footage may appear.
