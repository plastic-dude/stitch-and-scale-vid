## Research-informed distinction

Pass 207 qualified a source against intended use, relevance, timeliness, validity, completeness, accuracy basis, integrity, sample coverage, and method. Pass 208 adds the applicability step: the record should identify the exact claim or decision, required context, audience or user, environment, scope, and assumptions, then show whether the qualified evidence actually bears on that claim. NIST defines validation as objective evidence that requirements for a specific intended use or application have been fulfilled and describes predefined tests and results. GOV.UK says quality requirements vary by purpose and user. W3C WCAG-EM 2 bounds scope, user agents, sample, evaluation context, and reporting. Craft Yarn Council keeps gauge materials and instructions tied to the question tested. [1] [2] [3] [4]

> **Qualified evidence is not automatically evidence for this claim.**

These sources inform general workflow education only. They do not establish that Stitch & Scale currently maps evidence to claims, validates context, supports applicability judgments, or blocks misapplied evidence.

He may explain why evidence must be checked against the claim and operating context before it supports a decision. Calculation, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, and test knitting remain distinct human evidence gates.

## Treatment and beats

The film opens on a neutral tabletop. A qualified evidence card arrives with a visible source-quality tag, then stops at a second gate labeled `APPLIES TO THIS CLAIM?`. The card does not enter the decision lane merely because it passed an earlier qualification step.

Target claim, required context, user, environment, assumptions, scope, sample, and version cards enter one at a time. The applicability test remains `NOT RUN`, and the decision stays `HOLD` until current evidence and accountable review establish the match.
