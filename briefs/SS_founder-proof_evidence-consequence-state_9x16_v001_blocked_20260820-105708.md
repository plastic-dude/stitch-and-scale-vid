## Core creative distinction

The nearest prior is `evidence-applicability-state`, with adjacent `evidence-type`, `evidence-status-state`, `evidence-coverage`, `evidence-lineage-state`, `evidence-over-output`, `test-knit-coverage`, `observed-knit-state`, `observation-state`, `acceptance-criteria`, and `acceptability-state`. Those concepts concern whether evidence applies, what type it is, its status, its coverage, its lineage, evidence versus output, test-knit coverage, observation, or acceptance criteria. Pass 260 asks what decision one piece of evidence supports, what decision it does not support, and who must make the next call.

| Dimension | Nearest prior territory | Pass 260 `evidence-consequence-state` |
|---|---|---|
| Changed pain | Evidence lacks type, status, coverage, lineage, or acceptance context. | A valid observation is silently stretched into a larger claim than its question and scope support. |
| Hook | Identify or cover the evidence. | “Proof is only as wide as the question it answered.” |
| Visual metaphor | Evidence chain, coverage grid, test-knit roster, or observation card. | One evidence card split into `QUESTION SUPPORTED` and `QUESTION NOT SUPPORTED`; a pencil stops at a thick boundary. |
| Proof surface | Evidence type, status, coverage, lineage, or acceptance fields. | `EVIDENCE TYPE / OBSERVED-CALCULATED-REVIEWED / SCOPE / SAMPLE / QUESTION SUPPORTED / QUESTION NOT SUPPORTED / DECISION OWNER / STATUS`. |
| Emotional turn | Evidence gains traceability or coverage context. | Evidence accumulation becomes evidence humility and a bounded human decision. |
| Final line | Context-specific prior line. | “Name what the proof can answer before you let it decide.” |

It is distinct from pass 259 `meaning-preservation-state`, which concerns reader-facing meaning surviving a medium change; pass 260 concerns the consequence and limit of evidence regardless of medium. It is also separate from imported `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster`; no tester roster, assignment queue, release staffing, automatic routing, false-closure verdict, release decision, or generic human-gate completion surface appears.

## Dramatic engine before camera

**Subject:** A fictional `EVIDENCE CONSEQUENCE` card with supported and not-supported columns.
**Desire:** Make a responsible next decision without overstating what was checked.
**Obstacle:** A calculation, technical edit, reader observation, test knit, accessibility evaluation, or capture can be valid while still limited by scope, sample, question, method, and conditions.
**Stakes:** One small observation may be mistaken for universal pattern accuracy, accessibility, publication readiness, or product proof. The film must not claim this happened to a Stitch & Scale user.
**Change:** Move from one evidence label to explicit supported-question, not-supported-question, and decision-owner fields.
**Contradiction:** More evidence can still produce less honesty if its consequence is left undefined.
**Escalation:** Evidence type; observed/calculated/reviewed; scope; sample; supported question; unsupported question; owner; status.
**Final image:** `EVIDENCE → QUESTION → SCOPE → HUMAN DECISION`, with `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Treatment

Open on a quiet cream desk. A paper card titled `EVIDENCE CONSEQUENCE` sits beside a blank observation strip and a blank calculation strip. The narrator says, “Proof is only as wide as the question it answered.” The hand writes `EVIDENCE TYPE: OBSERVED` and `SCOPE: ONE SIZE / ONE ROUTE`. A pencil tries to drag the evidence across a thick vertical line. The left column reads `QUESTION SUPPORTED: WHAT HAPPENED HERE?`; the right column reads `QUESTION NOT SUPPORTED: DOES EVERY SIZE WORK?`. The hand stops the pencil and adds `DECISION OWNER: HUMAN` and `STATUS: REVIEW`. The narrator says, “A test knit, a calculation, a technical edit, and a reader report answer different questions.” The final frame reads `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. The founder invitation asks founding testers what decision they need evidence to support; it does not claim a finished evidence registry.
