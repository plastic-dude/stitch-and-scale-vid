## Research decision

Fresh research on The Knitting Guild Association’s current AI-in-knitting resource and the FTC’s current artificial-intelligence enforcement index changed the concept from a generic anti-AI warning to a **verification boundary story**. The research supports caution around generated pattern language, unsupported accuracy or outcome claims, and the need to keep human evidence distinct. It does not prove that Stitch & Scale uses AI, generates text, validates patterns, or offers any AI surface.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect one verified product surface and keep the evidence boundary visible; it does not market an AI feature, condemn AI generally, or claim human review guarantees correctness.

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `false-completion`, but that imported concept is a blocked broad human-gate story and its filename remains occupied. `evidence-over-output` changes the pain to **fluent or generated-looking language being mistaken for craft evidence**; the hook to **“A generated line can sound finished. It cannot knit the proof.”**; the visual metaphor from a generic completion state to a polished sentence stopping before five distinct evidence cards; the proof surface from a human-gate completion concept to one current approved product surface that shows exactly what the tool does; the emotional turn from fear of false closure to calm verification literacy; and the final line to **“Show me the evidence behind the sentence.”**

It is distinct from `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster` because it specifically concerns output-versus-evidence semantics, not a generic or rostered human release gate. It is distinct from `decision-ledger` because it is not about financial truth states; from `chart-parity` or `readability-under-load` because it is not about two representations or visual load; from `inherited-thread` because it is not founder provenance; and from `recovery-proof`, `event-window`, `offline-continuity`, `correction-trace`, `locale-drift`, `gauge-reality`, and `reader-handoff` because the conflict is generated-language plausibility versus human craft evidence.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on an original neutral paper card with one polished illustrative sentence. The card must carry `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI` and must not contain a real pattern, private data, or generated product text. The sentence is deliberately generic and not presented as a usable knitting instruction. As the camera moves closer, five separate human-evidence cards interrupt: `TECHNICAL EDIT`, `PHYSICAL PRINT REVIEW`, `CHART READABILITY`, `SCHEMATIC SCALE`, and `TEST KNIT`. These are workflow props, not current product controls, and must not become a green checklist or roster.

Do not claim the product uses AI, generates text, checks patterns, or validates any human gate. A current product insert is optional and blocked until a versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact visible labels. If no proof is supplied, keep the blocker card `PRODUCT PROOF REQUIRED — NOT PRODUCT UI`. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Use one narrator family. Captions must not cover the sentence, evidence cards, hands, face, proof, controls, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.
