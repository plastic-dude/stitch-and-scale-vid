## Concept and claim boundary

> “Allowed once is not governed.”

A fictional workflow departs from a named rule. The exception card begins as a single amber permission, then reveals rule, scope, reason, owner, start, expiry or review, affected gates, compensating action, status, and disposition. The story is about a **bounded deviation lifecycle**, not a product exception workflow, waiver, risk acceptance, approval, automated expiry, compliance, or accessibility claim.

NIST describes its Risk Management Framework as a comprehensive, flexible, repeatable, and measurable process for managing information-security and privacy risk [897]. GOV.UK architecture-decision guidance recommends recording context, decisions, consequences, authorship/time, and lifecycle states in version control [898]. W3C WCAG guidance informs the need for textually explicit state changes and review when non-text content changes [899]. These sources motivate the fictional proof surface and production review only; none proves that Stitch & Scale implements exception controls, ADRs, risk acceptance, or WCAG conformance.

All rules, scopes, reasons, names, dates, affected gates, compensating actions, statuses, and dispositions are fictional. Generated text is not UI truth. Exact product labels, warnings, controls, and lifecycle states require a current approved product capture or proof-registry entry. David is a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not described as a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, technical editor, or tester.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |

| Desire | Move a necessary deviation forward without hiding what it changes |

| Change | The exception names rule, scope, reason, owner, lifecycle boundary, affected gates, compensating action, status, and disposition |

| Escalation | `EXPIRES_OR_REVIEWED_AT: UNDEFINED` and `AFFECTED_GATES: UNKNOWN` turn amber/red |
| Final image | Exception remains held with a visible review boundary; no permanent approval |

## Visual grammar and coverage plan

Use original abstract rule and exception cards, a neutral evidence board, and a visible lifecycle boundary. Amber marks undefined or unverified exception fields. Red marks only `EXCEPTION UNBOUNDED`. There is no green approved, compliant, safe, or complete state. Captions occupy a fixed lane away from `EXCEPTION`, `RULE`, `SCOPE`, `REASON`, `OWNER`, `STARTED_AT`, `EXPIRES_OR_REVIEWED_AT`, `AFFECTED_GATES`, `COMPENSATING_ACTION`, `STATUS`, `REVIEWED`, `DISPOSITION`, tables, numbers, labels, buttons, menus, hands, and primary actions.

Coverage: locked overhead master; rule-departure insert; scope/reason hold; owner/start hold; expiry/review boundary hold; affected-gates and compensating-action hold; status/disposition hold; caption-free clean master; caption-on review master; mobile crop review; accessibility review frame. Alternative coverage is a static temporary-exception lifecycle card with start, review/expiry, affected gates, and disposition centered if motion weakens legibility. Reject stale UI, caption collision, fake approval, invented risk acceptance, mixed voices, doubled words, overlaps, room-tone jumps, and unreadable fields.
