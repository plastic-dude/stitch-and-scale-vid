## Dramatic engine

**Subject:** A large `TOTAL` card breaking into eight source-carrying fields.
**Desire:** Let a designer inspect what an expense represents before it becomes a summary.
**Obstacle:** A total looks authoritative while event source, date, category, project, and status remain hidden.
**Stakes:** A designer may misread a derived rollup or estimate as a recorded transaction or attach it to the wrong design. This is workflow-risk framing, not a claim of user harm.
**Change:** Move from summary-first bookkeeping to source-carrying record discipline.
**Contradiction:** A simple record can be more trustworthy when it preserves context instead of displaying more financial analysis.
**Escalation:** `TOTAL`; `EVENT`; `DATE`; `AMOUNT`; `CURRENCY`; `CATEGORY`; `PROJECT`; `SOURCE`; `STATUS`; `RECORDED / DERIVED / ESTIMATE`.
**Final image:** `KEEP THE SOURCE BESIDE THE EXPENSE.` with `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Visual grammar and production bible

Use one locked overhead camera, one 45-degree macro angle, one cream/charcoal/ink-blue paper system, one pencil, one blank receipt-shaped rectangle with no real data, and one neutral desk. No real receipt, invoice, bank statement, tax form, accounting screenshot, financial account, personal identifier, third-party logo, customer record, family material, or unlicensed sound. The only readable labels are the exact labels in this brief plus `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

Keep one field per close shot. The cards must remain legible with captions disabled and audio muted. The visual contrast is not “bad accounting versus good accounting”; it is **summary versus source**. ## Rights and risks

Use original paper cards and neutral props only. No real financial record, tax form, bank statement, receipt, invoice, account number, merchant data, customer file, or third-party logo is cleared. Future product captures require version/date, exact labels, privacy, destination, rights, and classification of event, source, rollup, estimate, or tax-related field.

Primary risks are a total being mistaken for a recorded event, a derived rollup being treated as a transaction, an estimate being treated as fact, a receipt prop exposing private data, an internal field being mistaken for tax advice, captions covering numbers, and a fictional card being mistaken for product UI. Mitigate with source fields, status labels, a blank source prop, fixed illustrative footer, one narrator, caption-on/off review, muted comprehension, rights review, and owner sign-off.
