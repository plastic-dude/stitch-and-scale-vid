## Research-informed premise

NIST explains that a hash maps an arbitrary-length message to a fixed-length digest and describes the security properties of approved hash functions. [1] Adobe documents validating PDF signatures to check signer authenticity and signed-content integrity, including certificate and timestamp details. [2] GitHub documents artifact attestations that link software artifacts to repositories, workflows, commits, events, and digests, while warning that an attestation is not a guarantee that the artifact is secure. [5] [6]

These sources support a bounded creative distinction: **identity evidence can help answer “is this the same artifact?” but does not answer “is the pattern mathematically correct, technically edited, physically print-reviewed, chart-readable, schematically scaled, or test-knit?”** No source proves that Stitch & Scale exposes this behavior.

Craft Yarn Council’s submission guidance separates labels, yarn bands, gauge swatches, electronic and printed instructions, schematics, charts, invoices, and return shipping. [3] Knitty’s June 17, 2026 submission guide likewise specifies individual files, text-only pattern documents, separate photos/charts/schematics, current style requirements, and a Winter 2026 deadline of September 15, 2026. [4] This supports a package-identity story without claiming a product workflow or creating urgency.

## Founder and product truth

David is a developer whose late mother knitted. He does not knit and must not be described as a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, technical editor, or tester. If he appears, he speaks as a developer explaining why a released artifact needs an honest identity trail; he does not claim technical, accounting, legal, or publishing authority.

No current product-proof registry entry or approved, non-private, rights-cleared capture verifies that Stitch & Scale exposes a file identity, hash, signature, export comparison, commit link, or reviewed-versus-released state. Every proposed card is a fictional prop and must display `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI` in the production bible and on any visible frame.

Do not claim that Stitch & Scale hashes files, signs PDFs, verifies certificates, links exports to commits, compares reviewed and released artifacts, guarantees integrity, proves correctness, ensures accessibility, or replaces technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, or test knitting. A matching hash or signature is not a correctness, fit, readability, or publication-approval claim.

## Treatment and beats

The narrator names the evidence needed to establish identity—file ID, version, digest or signed-content reference, capture time, source relationship, reviewer, and release status—then clearly separates identity from technical review. The end state remains blocked and unverified.

## Visual grammar and production bible

Use one quiet neutral tabletop, soft side light, restrained camera movement, and high-contrast original cards. The fictional filename must be visibly generic and non-operational. Do not use a real PDF, real hash, real commit SHA, real signature badge, actual publication submission, customer data, vendor logo, or recognizable software chrome. Every card must display `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

Keep `REVIEWED_ARTIFACT` and `RELEASED_FILE` physically separate until the identity fields are introduced. Use one field family per close shot: review identity, release identity, identity evidence, source relationship, human gates, and status. Do not show a dense full-screen table. Do not use a success chime, green “verified” badge, lock icon that implies security, signature guarantee, or “correct” stamp. Generated text is not UI truth.

The production bible locks the same card orientation, filename typography, desk surface, hand direction, wardrobe, narrator family, room tone, and light temperature across all takes. David may appear in a neutral developer frame or remain voice-only; he must not perform knitting or be framed as a craft practitioner. Technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, and test knitting remain distinct human evidence gates and cannot be replaced by a hash, signature, or identity card.
