## Research decision

Craft Yarn Council distinguishes finishing from blocking and notes that care and blocking methods vary by yarn; damp or steamed garments can be warped or misshapen until dry. An established knitwear schematic guide describes the schematic as a multi-measurement blueprint and says a single finished body/bust measurement is not enough. These sources support state-aware comparison, but they do not prove Stitch & Scale tracks blocking, washing, drying, care conditions, or final garment outcomes.

## Difference from nearest prior angle

The nearest prior angle is `fit-intent`, which focuses on the meaning of body, finished garment, ease, and fit measurements. `finish-state` changes the pain to **whether a measurement or appearance comparison is being made before or after finishing, blocking, drying, or a care condition**. The hook changes to **“A measurement needs a state.”** The visual metaphor changes from body/garment/ease cards to **`UNFINISHED / BLOCKED / DRY / CARE-CONDITIONED`**. The proof surface changes from sizing/fit guidance to a current blocking-state label, finished/unfinished state, care-condition note, post-block measurement surface, or state-aware schematic/measurement record. The emotional turn changes from choosing with context to honest comparison through state context. The final line changes to **“Name the state before you compare the number.”**

It is distinct from `fit-intent` because it is object condition rather than body/garment/ease meaning; from `paper-proof` because it is not printed-page inspection; from `schematic-scale` because it is not physical legibility; from `material-notes` because it is the measurement state rather than material identity; from `measurement-moment` because it is the object condition rather than capture timing; and from imported `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster` because it is not completion or roster accountability. Neither imported concept is reopened, renamed, overwritten, or approved.
