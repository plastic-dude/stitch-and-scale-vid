## Research-informed distinction

Pass 203 separated a change event, affected surface, observed result, evidence, and decision. Pass 204 adds the criteria-fit boundary: an observed result needs explicit criteria, a named intended use, and a defined test before anyone can say whether it qualifies. NIST defines validation as objective evidence that requirements for a specific intended use have been fulfilled and as acceptability according to predefined tests and results. GOV.UK guidance recommends recording acceptance criteria and supporting evidence. W3C says Easy Checks provide a rough first review and are not a complete accessibility evaluation. Craft Yarn Council keeps gauge, instructions, reading, and actual human craft checks distinct. [1] [2] [3] [4]

> **A result can be real and still not answer the question you need answered.**

These sources inform general workflow education only. They do not establish that Stitch & Scale currently evaluates fit, runs acceptance tests, verifies requirements, or certifies accessibility.

## Treatment and beats

The film opens on a neutral tabletop. A card reads `OBSERVED_RESULT: EXAMPLE ONLY`. Beside it sits a large neutral `PASS` stamp crossed out. An intended-use card enters: `FOLLOW CHART 3 — SIZE M`. Criteria and test cards remain blank until the use is named.

The criteria card reads `STITCH + ROW COUNT`; the test reads `ONE GAUGE CHECK`; supporting evidence remains `NOT SUPPLIED`. A limitations card keeps other sizes, print, chart, schematic, and test knitting visibly outside the check. The final fit status stays pending.
