## Concept, distinctness, and research boundary

A record can exist without being suitable for the decision someone wants to make. The nearest prior angle is `schema-state`, which asks whether the record has a defined structure. `fitness-state` asks whether a measurement or output is fit for a stated craft purpose under constraints and a bounded acceptance test. Hook: “Present is not fit for purpose.” Fictional proof surface: `INTENDED USE / INPUTS / CONSTRAINTS / OUTPUT / ACCEPTANCE TEST / LIMITS / REVIEWED / DECISION`. Emotional turn: “The record exists” becomes “The record is suitable for this decision.” Final line: “Name the purpose before you trust the result.” Distinct from `calibration-state`, `context-state`, `acceptance-criteria`, `applicability-scope`, `comparison-basis`, `publication-readiness`, and `test-knit-coverage`.

NIST defines fit for purpose as capable of meeting objectives or service levels with suitable design, implementation, control, and maintenance. Craft Yarn Council links measurements, sizing, schematics, ease, and project-pattern context while warning that fit and length charts are guidelines and individual differences matter. These sources support workflow education only; they do not prove that Stitch & Scale currently exposes fitness semantics, sizing accuracy, or fit outcomes.
