## Research decision

Fresh research on the Craft Yarn Council’s Standard Yarn Weight System examined the gap between yarn-category labels and the actual gauge and fabric a knitter produces. The source explicitly calls gauge ranges guidelines only and tells readers to follow the gauge stated in the pattern. This changed the campaign away from locale, version, reader-interpretation, and human-gate stories toward **gauge reality**: the fabric is the evidence, not the label.

The research does not prove that Stitch & Scale currently tracks swatches, yarn substitutions, fiber behavior, or gauge validation. The film remains blocked until current approved proof is supplied.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect the relationship between yarn category, stated gauge, swatch, and finished fabric. It does not promise that the product determines safe substitutions or guarantees fit.

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `locale-drift`, which focuses on meaning changing across locale terminology and keys. `gauge-reality` changes the pain to **material behavior being mistaken for a category label**; the hook to **“Same weight is not the same fabric.”**; the visual metaphor from aligned locale pages to label-to-swatch transformation; the proof surface from locale/export comparison to current gauge, swatch, substitution, yarn, or preflight evidence; the emotional turn from inclusive language confidence to tactile confidence in the material decision; and the final line to **“Show me the fabric the label cannot.”** It is distinct from `correction-trace`, `reader-handoff`, `false-completion`, and `release-gate-roster`.

## Treatment and visual grammar

Open with an original yarn label prop, an unlabeled needle, and a small swatch. The label may show only generic illustrative category text and must be marked `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI` in production records. A simple split shows category label on one side and fabric on the other. The tension is that a category range cannot stand in for the target gauge. If a current approved product capture is supplied, show one exact gauge or substitution surface at readable scale with current labels, values, and version/date. Do not invent a swatch tracker or “safe substitution” badge.

Use tactile macro shots, restrained cuts, and one narrator family. Captions must not cover the swatch, label, counts, needle size, face, hands, controls, or CTA. Avoid claiming that one gauge is correct outside the actual pattern and fabric context.
