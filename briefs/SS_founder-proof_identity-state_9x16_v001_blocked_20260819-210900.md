## Concept, distinctness, and research boundary

Two records can look alike while representing different entities or states. The nearest prior angle is `version-lock`, which asks whether an item is the same version. `identity-state` asks which record an item became after reopen, copy, revision, or export. Hook: “Named is not the same record.” Fictional proof surface: `RECORD ID / DISPLAY NAME / PARENT / REVISION OR COPY / CREATED / DERIVED FROM / OWNER / REVIEWED`. Emotional turn: duplicate anxiety becomes traceable record identity. Final line: “Name which record this became.” Distinct from `supersession-state`, `conversion-trace`, `provenance-chain`, `commit-state`, `schema-state`, and `authority-state`.

RFC 9562 describes persistent unique identifiers intended to provide uniqueness across space and time; W3C PROV-DM distinguishes entities and activities, and models generation, usage, derivation, and attribution. These sources support workflow education only. They do not prove that Stitch & Scale currently exposes stable IDs, copy/revision relationships, derived-from links, ownership, or identity review.
