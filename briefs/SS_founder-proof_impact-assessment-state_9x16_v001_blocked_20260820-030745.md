## Research-informed distinction

Pass 202 named a review trigger and paused a prior decision. Pass 203 adds the impact boundary: a trigger is not an impact finding. NIST defines impact analysis as determining the extent to which a change affected or may have affected a system’s state or posture. GOV.UK guidance recommends recording user needs, acceptance criteria, supporting evidence, and rationale. Craft Yarn Council guidance keeps gauge, instructions, reading, and human craft checks distinct. [1] [2] [3]

> **A change is an event. Its impact is a question. The decision needs evidence.**

These sources inform general workflow education only. They do not establish that Stitch & Scale currently exposes impact analysis, affected-surface links, automatic consequence detection, acceptance-criteria records, or decision automation.

## Treatment and beats

The film opens on a neutral tabletop. A card reads `CHANGE_EVENT: MATERIAL OR DYE LOT CHANGED`. It forks into three neutral lanes: `AFFECTED_SURFACE`, `OBSERVED_RESULT`, and `SUPPORTING_EVIDENCE`. A fourth lane, `DECISION`, remains separated from the event.

The affected surface is a neutral example, `GAUGE + COLOR`; the observed result remains `NOT OBSERVED`; acceptance criteria and supporting evidence are not supplied. The decision owner remains unconfirmed, and the impact status stays pending. The film refuses to turn the event into a product finding.
