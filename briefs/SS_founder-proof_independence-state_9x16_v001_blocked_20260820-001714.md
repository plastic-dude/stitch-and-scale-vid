## Concept and claim boundary

> “Assigned is not independent.”

A fictional release card names an author and reviewer. The first card looks complete until `SAME_PERSON` appears. A second card records recusal, an independence basis, the finding, and disposition without turning a same-person review into an approval. The story is about **inspectable reviewer relationship**, not a product release-gate, independent-audit, compliance, approval, or role-enforcement claim.

NIST SP 800-53 is used only as governance vocabulary; its official page describes a broad catalog and warns that framework mappings are not equivalence [880]. GOV.UK and Defra service-assessment guidance support the conceptual value of peer review, independent assessment, user needs, and testing risky assumptions [881] [882]. Craft context remains separate: calculation, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, and test knitting are distinct human evidence gates [883] [884]. None of these sources proves that Stitch & Scale exposes author/reviewer roles, recusal, independence, or release gating.

All labels, identities, dates, findings, and dispositions are fictional. Generated text is not UI truth. Exact product labels, role behavior, warnings, and release state require a current approved product capture or proof-registry entry. David is a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not described as a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, technical editor, or tester.
