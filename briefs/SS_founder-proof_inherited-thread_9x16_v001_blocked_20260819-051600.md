## Research decision

Fresh research on the FTC’s current endorsement guidance and a 2026 Journal of Brand Management study on storytelling in SMEs changed the concept from a generic founder-origin montage to a **boundary-of-voice story**. A truthful founder story may explain why a company exists, but it cannot use family provenance as proof of craft expertise, product performance, customer outcomes, or a testimonial. The customer/designer remains the subject; the founder can state what he knows and what he does not claim.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film is an invitation to inspect one verified product surface, not a claim that the founder understands knitting practice or that the product delivers any specific result.

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `decision-ledger`, which concerns distinguishing recorded, derived, estimated, and planned values before a business decision. `inherited-thread` changes the pain to **borrowed authority in founder storytelling**; the hook to **“I inherited the reason, not the craft.”**; the visual metaphor from separate record-state cards to a thread passing from an abstract inherited memory toward a proof surface and stopping at the founder’s boundary; the proof surface from a business-record state to one current, approved product surface that shows what the tool actually does; the emotional turn from calm agency about numbers to respectful credibility through a stated limitation; and the final line to **“Let the work show what the story cannot.”**

It is distinct from `offline-continuity`, `correction-trace`, `gauge-reality`, `readability-under-load`, `chart-parity`, `locale-drift`, and `reader-handoff` because the conflict is neither technical state, publication correction, material behavior, legibility, representation equivalence, locale meaning, nor reader interpretation. It does not reopen or consume imported `false-completion` or `release-gate-roster`, which remain blocked with occupied filenames.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on an abstract, rights-cleared thread, yarn, or neutral hand-to-object gesture. Do not depict an invented mother, invented workshop, heirloom, family photograph, or private family record. If a real family asset is proposed, record provenance and written consent before use; otherwise use a neutral abstract prop labeled as an illustrative metaphor in the production record. David appears only with authorization and speaks as a developer. His exact identity boundary is the story’s turn.

The camera never fakes a craft demonstration. No needles in David’s hands, no tutorial gesture, no swatch authority, and no voiceover claiming that he knits. The product insert is optional and blocked until a current, versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact labels. If absent, use an original blocker card labeled `PRODUCT PROOF REQUIRED — NOT PRODUCT UI`. One narrator family only; David’s spoken line may be the sole voice or an authorized narrator may read all lines. Captions must not cover the face, thread, proof, controls, logos, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.

## Exact script

**7–16s — David or narrator:** “My late mother knitted. I’m a developer. I didn’t inherit the craft—I inherited the reason to care about the problem.”

**16–24s — Narrator:** “That story is not proof. The work has to show what it actually does.”

**24–35s — Narrator:** “So here is the honest invitation: inspect the current surface, ask what it proves, and leave the rest open.”
