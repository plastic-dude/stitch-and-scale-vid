## Core creative distinction

The nearest prior is pass 245 `revisitability-state`. Pass 245 asked whether a recorded decision remains understandable and challengeable after time, revision, or handoff. Pass 246 asks which human work categories are hidden behind a finished-looking pattern output.

| Dimension | Pass 245 `revisitability-state` | Pass 246 `labor-visibility-state` |
|---|---|---|
| Changed pain | Context debt: rationale and reopen conditions disappear. | Visibility gap: real work categories disappear behind a finished-looking file. |
| Hook | “If future-you cannot tell why, the decision is not finished.” | “A pattern file can look finished while the work is still invisible.” |
| Visual metaphor | Decision card lifted to reveal context fields. | Finished-file card unfolding into a human-labor receipt. |
| Proof surface | Decision, why, source, alternatives, assumption, scope, confidence, reopen-if, status. | Output, work category, evidence, owner, status, cost unknown, value not calculated. |
| Emotional turn | Relief through future-readable context. | Recognition that names the work without assigning a price. |
| Final line | “Leave future-you a reason, not just a result.” | “Name the work before you name the value.” |

The angle is not a rename of `business-math`, `price-basis`, `cost-model`, `record-context`, `revisitability-state`, `restore-boundary-state`, `review-question-state`, or any prior evidence-state concept. It is a category-visibility question, not pricing advice, financial analysis, or a capability claim.

## Dramatic engine before camera

**Subject:** A fictional finished-pattern card and a paper work-category receipt.  
**Desire:** See and name the labor that made a pattern usable before deciding what the work means commercially.  
**Obstacle:** The final file is clean while notes, grading, charts, schematics, technical editing, test knitting, layout, photography, accessibility, admin, and support are not visible in the file itself.  
**Stakes:** The designer may make a business decision without seeing the actual work chain; the video must not claim this has happened to a Stitch & Scale user.  
**Change:** Move from “the file is ready” to “the work categories are named.”  
**Contradiction:** A clean deliverable can hide the messy human sequence that makes it trustworthy.  
**Escalation:** The result card looks complete; one line unfolds into work categories; the narrator refuses to attach a price or call the list a product ledger; the invitation asks founding testers what the current product can actually record.  
**Final image:** `OUTPUT: PATTERN FILE` above named work categories, with `COST UNKNOWN / VALUE NOT CALCULATED` and `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Treatment

Open on a clean desk with a bold card reading `PATTERN FILE: READY`. The narrator says, “A pattern file can look finished while the work is still invisible.” A paper strip slides from behind it and reveals `DESIGN NOTES`, `GRADING`, `CHARTS + SCHEMATIC`, `TECHNICAL EDITING`, `TEST KNITTING`, `LAYOUT`, `PHOTOS`, `ACCESSIBILITY`, and `ADMIN + SUPPORT`. The card distinguishes work categories from value: `COST UNKNOWN` and `VALUE NOT CALCULATED`. It then names human gates that remain separate from any record: technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, and test knitting. The founder turns the paper away and says he will not pretend it is the app. The final card asks founding testers to show what the current product can actually make visible.

## Visual grammar and production bible

Use one narrator family, one tabletop, one paper system, one charcoal/cream/ink-blue palette, and a strict type hierarchy. The only readable words are the fictional labels listed in this brief, with the fixed footer `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Do not render real pattern numbers, chart symbols, schematic measurements, customer records, invoices, rates, prices, or UI controls. Generated text is not UI truth. Any future product number, label, warning, or control must come from a current approved capture.

The primary location is a quiet desk with no private documents. Use one locked overhead camera and one 45-degree macro angle. Keep all cards within a high-contrast central area. Use paper unfolding and pencil placement as the visual rhythm. No digital glitch, fake loading, fake save, fake database, or effect may imply product behavior. The arrangement is output card at left, work-category strip through center, and blocked product-proof card at right.
