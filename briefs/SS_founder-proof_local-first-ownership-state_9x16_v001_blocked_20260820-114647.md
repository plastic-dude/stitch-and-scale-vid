## Dramatic engine

**Subject:** An original `WHO HOLDS THE COPY?` card and six responsibility cards.
**Desire:** Keep a designer’s work under visible control without pretending local storage solves every loss or security problem.
**Obstacle:** “Local-first” sounds like one complete promise while browser storage, sync, export, backup, and security have different states.
**Stakes:** A designer may trust an unverified permanence, recovery, sync, or privacy claim and fail to create an export or backup. This is a risk framing, not a claim that a Stitch & Scale user experienced loss.
**Change:** Move from slogan-level reassurance to a named copy/backup/security responsibility map.
**Contradiction:** Local primary data can improve agency while still requiring backup, portability, and honest storage-status disclosure.
**Escalation:** `LOCAL = SAFE?`; `PRIMARY WORKING COPY`; `SYNC COPY`; `EXPORT`; `BACKUP`; `SECURITY`; `STATUS`.
**Final image:** `KNOW WHERE THE COPY LIVES.` with `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Visual grammar and production bible

Use one locked overhead camera, one 45-degree macro angle, one cream/charcoal/ink-blue paper system, one pencil, one blank envelope, and one neutral device silhouette. No real pattern, private record, browser screenshot, customer file, family material, cloud-provider logo, security badge, third-party UI, or generated dashboard. The only readable labels are the exact labels in this brief plus the fixed footer. Do not render a backup success tick, sync animation, lock icon, browser permission prompt, cloud status, encryption label, or recovery control.

Keep all cards in the central 9:16 safe region. The role of each card must remain readable with captions disabled and audio muted. Use negative space to show that a local copy, a sync copy, an export, and a backup are different objects. The blank reverse sides are intentional: they show unknown product proof rather than fabricated detail.

## Rights and risks

Use original paper assets only. No real pattern, private record, browser/device screenshot, customer file, family material, cloud logo, security badge, competitor UI, or unlicensed sound is cleared. If a current capture is supplied, verify version/date, exact labels, browser/device context, privacy, destination, ownership, rights, and whether it demonstrates storage, sync, export, backup, persistence, or merely a visual state.

Primary risks are local-first being mistaken for backup, browser storage being presented as permanent, security being implied by visual polish, sync being implied by a dashed line, export being treated as recovery, legal ownership being inferred from practical control, captions obscuring the proof cards, and current product labels being fabricated. Mitigate with blank reverse cards, explicit `STATUS: VERIFY`, fixed illustrative footer, a single narrator, caption-on/off review, muted comprehension, rights review, and owner sign-off.
