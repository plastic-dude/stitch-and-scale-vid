## Core creative distinction

The nearest prior is pass 252 `version-delta-state`. Pass 252 asked how a later change should disclose its delta to people using an earlier state. Pass 253 asks how a localized version should preserve technical relationships across languages.

| Dimension | Pass 252 `version-delta-state` | Pass 253 `locale-alignment-state` |
|---|---|---|

## Dramatic engine before camera

**Subject:** A fictional `LOCALE PARITY` card comparing source and target pattern fields.  
**Desire:** Let a designer or reviewer see whether a translated technical relationship still lines up.  
**Obstacle:** A fluent target phrase may disagree with the source abbreviation, unit, chart, schematic, size, or identified language part.  
**Stakes:** A small language seam can alter a maker’s interpretation; the video must not claim this happened to a Stitch & Scale user.  
**Change:** Move from a polished translation to a visible parity record with a human review boundary.  
**Contradiction:** Both columns can read smoothly while the technical relationship between them remains unreviewed.  
**Escalation:** Source and target languages appear; the term pair appears; abbreviation and definition appear; unit and chart reference appear; affected size and human review status remain visible.  
**Final image:** `SOURCE / TARGET / TERM / ABBREVIATION / DEFINITION / UNIT / CHART REF / SIZE / LANGUAGE IDENTIFIED / HUMAN PARITY REVIEW / STATUS` with `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Treatment

Open on a clean desk with a paper card titled `LOCALE PARITY`. The narrator says, “Two languages can agree on the sentence and still disagree on the pattern.” The card splits into `SOURCE: EN` and `TARGET: FR`. One field family appears at a time: `TERM`, `ABBREVIATION`, `DEFINITION`, `UNIT`, `CHART REF`, `SIZE`, and `LANGUAGE IDENTIFIED`. A bottom strip reads `HUMAN PARITY REVIEW / STATUS: UNRESOLVED`. The narrator explains that translated is not the same as proven aligned, and that language identification is not the same as accessibility conformance. The founder turns the paper away and says he will not pretend it is the app. The final card invites founding testers to identify what the current product can actually preserve across locales.
