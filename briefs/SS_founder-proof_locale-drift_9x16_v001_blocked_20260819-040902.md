## Research decision

Fresh research on Craft Yarn Council pattern-reading guidance, knitting abbreviations, and locale terminology changed the concept toward **locale drift**. The sources show that knitting shorthand is a learned convention, special abbreviations require definitions, and even closely related locales can use different terms such as bind off/cast off and gauge/tension. The sources do not prove that Stitch & Scale currently performs translation QA or cross-locale semantic validation.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect a multi-locale pattern handoff, not to promise automatic translation or identical meaning across languages.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | One canonical instruction passing through locale versions while its count, abbreviation, measurement, or key must remain intentional. |
| Desire | To make a pattern travel without making the reader decode an accidental new meaning. |
| Obstacle | Translation and locale differences can change words, abbreviations, measurement labels, or convention keys while the stitch count appears unchanged. |
| Stakes | A reader can follow the wrong convention even when the line looks professionally translated; the film must not claim the product prevents this without proof. |
| Change | The viewer moves from “the words changed” to “the meaning contract must remain visible.” |
| Contradiction | Identical counts do not guarantee identical instructions when terms or keys drift. |
| Escalation | One line → locale labels → abbreviation/key mismatch → current locale/export proof or an honest blocker. |
| Final image | Two locale pages aligned on one verified instruction/key, or an illustrative prop marked `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI` if proof is unavailable. |

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `reader-handoff`, which focuses on a reader interpreting a single published instruction after authorship. `correction-trace` focuses on a known post-release change and which copy carries it. Imported `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster` focus on human-gate status and tester evidence ownership. `locale-drift` changes the pain to **meaning changing across locales even when counts remain stable**; the hook to **“Same stitches. Different words. One meaning still has to survive.”**; the visual metaphor from a desk handoff or marked old/new page to aligned locale pages with one line traveling across them; the proof surface from reader or version state to current locale/export comparison, labels, keys, or translated pattern output; the emotional turn from individual uncertainty or maintenance responsibility to inclusive confidence that every intended reader receives the same instruction; and the final line to **“Show me what survived the translation.”**

## Treatment and visual grammar

Open on one original paper instruction and two locale cards, not fabricated product UI. Use a single line such as an illustrative “bind off / cast off” contrast, with all props labeled illustrative in production records. A transparent overlay shows the count staying constant while the term changes. The tension is whether the definition/key travels with the term. If a current approved locale/export capture is supplied, show one exact instruction or label family at readable scale with version/date and locale recorded in the manifest. Do not claim translation quality, semantic equivalence, or automatic QA from the prop.

Keep one narrator family, restrained cuts, clear labels, and no flags or cultural shorthand. Do not turn language difference into a joke or imply that one locale is more correct. Captions remain within safe zones and never cover locale labels, keys, counts, hands, face, or CTA.
