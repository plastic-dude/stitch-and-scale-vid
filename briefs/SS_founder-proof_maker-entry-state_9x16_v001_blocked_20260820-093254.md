## Dramatic engine before camera

**Subject:** A fictional `BEFORE YOU START` card with seven entry fields.  
**Desire:** Let a maker know what they are starting with before committing yarn, time, or a size choice.  
**Obstacle:** Identity, materials, gauge basis, notes, size context, support files, or scope can be scattered, missing, or assumed.  
**Stakes:** The maker may begin from the wrong basis; the video must not claim this happened to a Stitch & Scale user.  
**Change:** Move from a false green light to a visible entry state with explicit unknowns and human choice.  
**Contradiction:** A pattern can be technically present while the maker’s starting context is still incomplete.  
**Escalation:** The card reveals identity, then materials, gauge, notes, size, support files, and scope; the final field refuses to convert entry review into fit or publication approval.  
**Final image:** `PATTERN ID / VERSION / MATERIALS / GAUGE BASIS / NOTES / SIZE-MEASUREMENTS / SUPPORT FILES / SCOPE-LIMIT` with `ENTRY REVIEWED / HUMAN CHOICE REMAINS` and `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Treatment

Open on a clean desk with a paper card titled `BEFORE YOU START`. The narrator says, “Before you start, what exactly do you have?” The first card reveals `PATTERN ID / VERSION`, then unfolds into `MATERIALS`, `GAUGE BASIS`, `NOTES`, `SIZE / MEASUREMENTS`, `SUPPORT FILES`, and `SCOPE / LIMIT`. The narrator explains that the entry card is not a green-light score: size choice remains the maker’s decision, gauge still needs the right swatch and conditions, and technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, and test knitting remain distinct human gates. The founder turns the paper away and says he will not pretend it is the app. The final card invites founding testers to identify what the current product can actually preserve before a project begins.

## Visual grammar and production bible

Use one narrator family, one tabletop, one paper system, one charcoal/cream/ink-blue palette, and a strict type hierarchy. The only readable words are the fictional labels listed in this brief, with the fixed footer `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Do not render real pattern lines, stitch counts, row counts, abbreviations, chart symbols, schematic measurements, tester identities, customer records, or UI controls. Generated text is not UI truth. Any future product number, label, warning, or control must come from a current approved capture.

The primary location is a quiet desk with no private documents. Use one locked overhead camera and one 45-degree macro angle. Keep the entry card within a high-contrast central area. Use one field family per close shot rather than a dense checklist. The visual rhythm is identity, materials, gauge, notes, size, support, scope, then the human-choice boundary. No digital loading, fake save, fake score, fake database, or effect may imply product behavior.
