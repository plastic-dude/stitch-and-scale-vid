## Research decision

Kate Atherley’s current technical-editor/designer guidance explains that yarn-weight names are categories rather than precise measures, that stockinette gauge can help identify yarn thickness, that pattern-stitch gauge helps identify needle needs, and that patterns should include clear yarn and gauge information. Purl Soho’s current substitution guide was discovered but blocked by a Cloudflare challenge, so no substantive claims were taken from it. The local craft-business workflow requires material/source notes and keeps test knitting and other human evidence separate.

The research changed the concept from a generic yarn-substitution tip into **material provenance plus a human recheck**. No source proves Stitch & Scale recommends substitutes, predicts behavior, determines fit, or offers a yarn database.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect one verified materials/gauge/substitution surface; it does not promise a match, substitution safety, fit outcome, or automatic recheck.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | A sample’s yarn is replaced while the original material assumptions sit beside the pattern. |
| Desire | To know what made the sample behave as it did before choosing a substitute. |
| Obstacle | A substitute can retain a broad weight label while changing fiber, construction, drape, elasticity, or gauge behavior. |
| Stakes | A designer or maker may treat a label match as proof of equivalence and skip the recheck. |
| Change | The viewer moves from “find a similar yarn” to “record the assumptions and recheck the work.” |
| Contradiction | A useful material note does not promise a match; it makes uncertainty visible. |
| Escalation | Yarn band appears → label seems to match → fiber/gauge/stitch/source cards diverge → `SUBSTITUTION — RECHECK` appears → current proof or blocker → human recheck remains. |
| Final image | Original yarn note, substitute note, gauge, and recheck boundary stay together beside the swatch. |

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `gauge-reality`, which concerns whether mathematical gauge assumptions survive physical fabric behavior. `material-notes` changes the pain to **a yarn substitution losing the material assumptions that made the sample interpretable**; the hook to **“A substitute can match the label and still change the work.”**; the visual metaphor from gauge math versus fabric to a yarn band, swatch, and pattern card linked by `FIBER / GAUGE / STITCH / RECHECK`; the proof surface from a gauge/reality surface to a current materials, source, gauge, or substitution surface; the emotional turn from material humility to traceable rechecking; and the final line to **“Keep the note. Recheck the work.”**

It is distinct from `sample-chain` because it concerns material assumptions rather than physical custody; from `decision-ledger` because it is not financial classification; from `measurement-moment` because it is not body/garment input; from `recovery-proof` because it is not file restoration; and from `chart-parity`, `equivalent-meaning`, `readability-under-load`, `evidence-over-output`, `permission-line`, `event-window`, `inherited-thread`, `offline-continuity`, `correction-trace`, `locale-drift`, `reader-handoff`, `false-completion`, and `release-gate-roster` because the conflict is substitution assumptions versus human recheck. It does not reopen, overwrite, rename, or consume the imported reservations.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on an original neutral yarn band, fictional swatch, and pattern card with `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. The fictional material note includes `FIBER`, `GAUGE`, `STITCH`, and `SOURCE`; no real brand, colorway, supplier, price, or copyrighted pattern is used. A second card reads `SUBSTITUTION — RECHECK`. The visual must not imply that the second yarn is equivalent or approved.

The product insert is optional and blocked until a current, versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact material, source, gauge, substitution, or recheck labels. If absent, retain `MATERIAL PROOF REQUIRED — NOT PRODUCT UI`. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Use one narrator family. Captions must not cover yarn bands, swatch, notes, proof, hands, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.
