## Research decision

Fresh research on Craft Yarn Council sizing guidance and Apple Human Interface Guidelines for text fields changed the concept from generic mobile input to **context-preserving measurement capture**. CYC distinguishes body measurements from finished-garment measurements and says sizing information and schematics should be reviewed before beginning. Apple’s guidance emphasizes appropriate keyboards, numeric formatting, persistent labels because placeholders disappear, logical focus, and validation. Neither source proves Stitch & Scale’s measurement UI, grading behavior, fit outcome, or accessibility compliance.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect one verified measurement or grading surface; it does not promise that the product determines correct fit, performs automatic measurement, or produces accurate grading.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | A maker captures one value while a tape, garment, phone, unit, size, and project compete for attention. |
| Desire | To preserve what the number means when the maker returns to the pattern later. |
| Obstacle | A bare number can detach from body versus garment, unit, active size, project, or validation state. |
| Stakes | A contextless value can be entered, reused, or interpreted incorrectly even if the digits themselves are unchanged. |
| Change | The viewer moves from “save the number” to “save the number with the context that makes it interpretable.” |
| Contradiction | A smaller field can require more context, not less, because the physical task is already demanding. |
| Escalation | Tape reads → phone receives value → unit/meaning disappears → labels return → current proof or blocker → maker pauses with context intact. |
| Final image | Tape, phone, and pattern card align under `BODY / CM / SIZE / PROJECT`, or a blocker card if product proof is absent. |

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `reader-handoff`, which concerns a reader interpreting sizing and ease information from a completed pattern. `measurement-moment` changes the pain to **capturing a value during the maker’s real-world task before its meaning detaches**; the hook to **“A measurement is only useful when its meaning travels with it.”**; the visual metaphor from reader-facing sizing information to a tape measure, phone, and pattern card connected by context labels; the proof surface from a reader handoff to one current measurement or grading-entry surface; the emotional turn from reader confidence to maker control during capture; and the final line to **“Keep the number. Keep what it means.”**

It is distinct from `decision-ledger` because it concerns measurement semantics rather than financial truth states; from `gauge-reality` because it concerns capture context rather than material behavior; from `locale-drift` because unit/localization is not the central conflict; from `readability-under-load` because this is field capture rather than document use; and from `offline-continuity`, `recovery-proof`, `event-window`, `inherited-thread`, `evidence-over-output`, `correction-trace`, `chart-parity`, `false-completion`, and `release-gate-roster` because the conflict is active measurement capture and context preservation.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on a real but rights-cleared studio-like setup: neutral tape measure, clean sample garment or pattern card, and a device fixture with no personal data. A bare illustrative value such as `42` appears on a card labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`; it is not presented as a real measurement or product capture. The camera then adds four context cards: `BODY`, `CM`, `SIZE`, and `PROJECT`. These are illustrative props, not current controls. Do not show a person’s body, private measurements, names, or identifiable garment without consent.

The product insert is optional and blocked until a current, versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact measurement, unit, size, project, or validation labels. If absent, retain the blocker card `MEASUREMENT PROOF REQUIRED — NOT PRODUCT UI`. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Use one narrator family. Captions must not cover the number, unit, labels, hands, tape, proof, controls, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.
