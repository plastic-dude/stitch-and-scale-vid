## Research decision

Craft Yarn Council’s current knitting-abbreviation reference maps common shorthand such as `k`, `p`, `inc`, `dec`, `k2tog`, `ssk`, and `yo` to descriptions, while noting that designers may use special abbreviations not found on the list and that definitions are generally given at the beginning of a book or pattern. Digital.gov’s current plain-language guide emphasizes audience-specific clear content and separates principles, writing, design, and testing for understanding. Neither source proves a current Stitch & Scale terminology key, abbreviation validator, chart correctness, universal readability, or accessibility compliance.

The research changed the concept from generic pattern readability into **local notation meaning plus understanding review**. The film must not claim terminology validation, correctness, universal readability, or accessibility compliance.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect one verified abbreviation, notation, terminology, or key surface; it does not promise that shorthand is validated, correct, or understood by every maker.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | A short instruction is familiar to the writer but opaque to the maker. |
| Desire | To keep shorthand efficient without making the maker decode the pattern alone. |
| Obstacle | A special term or abbreviation appears before its meaning, or changes meaning locally. |
| Stakes | A maker can pause, guess, or interpret a step differently from the writer’s intent. |
| Change | The viewer moves from “the shorthand is obvious” to “define it at first use and test understanding.” |
| Contradiction | A standard-looking abbreviation can still be pattern-specific; a definition can aid understanding without proving correctness. |
| Escalation | Shorthand line appears → `KEY / FIRST USE / LOCAL TERM / RECHECK` split → definition card replaces correctness badge → current proof or blocker. |
| Final image | A note card beside `KEY / FIRST USE / LOCAL TERM / RECHECK`, with no approval stamp. |

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `readability-under-load`, which concerns maker legibility and comprehension under dense or mobile reading conditions. `notation-key` changes the pain to **shorthand appearing familiar while its local meaning is absent or inconsistent**; the hook to **“Shorter words need a longer welcome.”**; the visual metaphor from dense reading to `KEY / FIRST USE / LOCAL TERM / RECHECK`; the proof surface from typography/layout/readability to an abbreviation, notation, terminology, or key surface; the emotional turn from calm pacing under load to generous definition before decoding; and the final line to **“Define the shorthand. Keep the maker moving.”**

It is distinct from `chart-parity` because it is not chart-vs-text equivalence; from `equivalent-meaning` because it is not non-visual access; from `technique-profile` because it is not prerequisite complexity; from `source-note` because it is not provenance; from `as-of` because it is not temporal currentness; from `feedback-loop` because it is not response tracking; and from `false-completion`, `release-gate-roster`, `correction-trace`, `locale-drift`, `data-minimum`, `measurement-moment`, `gauge-reality`, `material-notes`, `care-context`, `sample-chain`, `support-boundary`, `brief-before-knit`, `permission-line`, `recovery-proof`, `decision-ledger`, `event-window`, `evidence-over-output`, `inherited-thread`, `offline-continuity`, and `reader-handoff` because the conflict is local term meaning versus assumed familiarity. It does not reopen, overwrite, rename, or consume the imported reservations.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on an original fictional instruction card labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`: `M1R, rep from *`. The line is intentionally incomplete and has no real pattern context. Four cards appear: `KEY`, `FIRST USE`, `LOCAL TERM`, and `RECHECK`. A definition card replaces a `STANDARD = CORRECT` badge. The device then displays a blocker or current proof surface, never a fabricated terminology validator.

The product insert is optional and blocked until a current, versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact abbreviation, notation, terminology, localized-term, chart-key, or understanding-review labels. If absent, retain `NOTATION PROOF REQUIRED — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Any real pattern page, CYC list, chart symbol, custom term, translator, maker, or founder voice/image requires source and rights review. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Use one narrator family. Captions must not cover instruction lines, key cards, proof, hands, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.
