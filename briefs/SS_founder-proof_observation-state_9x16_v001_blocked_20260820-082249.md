## Research-informed premise

The Craft Yarn Council’s current pattern-writing guidance tells designers to record what they did after each row or round rather than rely on memory. It then separates transcription, in which notes are cleaned up after the completed sample, from standards, terminology, sizing, technical editing, and testing. It also says changes made during design should be noted. [1]

CYC’s overview includes written patterns, video tutorials, charts, graphs, schematics, and combinations, emphasizes the mathematics involved in design and pattern writing, and asks testers to check directions, abbreviations, symbols, charts, materials, and finished appearance. [1]

Knitty’s current June 17, 2026 guidance distinguishes designer work, technical editing, optional test knitting, editorial selection, rights, and publication. [2] W3C’s current accessibility report template asks for review dates, scope, samples, included and excluded material, reviewers, tools, manual reviews, interpretative summary, detailed results, and recommendations. [3] WCAG-EM 2.0 separates scope definition, sample selection, evaluation, and findings reporting. [4]

## Dramatic engine

| Engine element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | Fictional gauge field and its evidence-state trail. |
| Desire | Let a designer or reviewer know whether a statement was seen, transcribed, calculated, interpreted, or left open. |
| Obstacle | One polished number hides whether it came from a note, formula, judgment, or unresolved question. |
| Stakes | A state error can make a calculated or interpreted value look like direct observation, or make an open question look resolved. No real defect is claimed. |
| Change | The naked value gains state, source, method, inputs, reviewer, and limit. |
| Contradiction | An observation can be wrong, a transcription can omit context, a calculation can use bad inputs, and an interpretation can be disputed. |
| Escalation | Naked value → source card → method card → reviewer/limit → unresolved boundary. |
| Final image | `EVIDENCE STATE DECLARED / HUMAN REVIEW REQUIRED`. |

## Treatment, beats, and visual grammar

Use one unbranded tabletop, one fictional gauge field, five evidence-state cards, one source/method rail, one reviewer card, one limit card, and one human-gate card. All cards are original illustrative props. Open on a clean number and reveal the method rather than adding an approval badge.

| Time | Beat | Image and action | Spoken idea | Evidence status |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5s | Naked value | `GAUGE: 20 ST / 10 CM` with no state. | “Did you see it—or infer it?” | Fictional field only. |
| 5–12s | Observation named | `OBSERVED: SWATCH NOTE`; `SOURCE: ROW 12 NOTE`. | “Start with the evidence state.” | Direct-note model, not proof. |
| 12–21s | Transcription named | `TRANSCRIBED: CLEAN COPY`; `FROM: SWATCH NOTE`. | “A clean copy may be a transcription.” | No fidelity claim. |
| 21–30s | Calculation named | `CALCULATED: 20 ST ÷ 10 CM`; `INPUTS: COUNT / LENGTH`. | “A number may come from inputs and a method.” | No formula correctness claim. |
| 30–38s | Interpretation/open state | `INTERPRETED: MAY MATCH TARGET`; `UNRESOLVED: HUMAN CHECK`. | “Interpretation is not the same as observation.” | Preserve disputed/open state. |
| 38–45s | Honest ending | Final card with state/source/method/reviewer/limit. | “Label how you know before you label it true.” | Blocked; no capability claim. |
