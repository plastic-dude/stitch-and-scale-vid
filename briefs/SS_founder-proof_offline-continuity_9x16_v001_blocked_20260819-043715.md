## Research decision

Fresh research on MDN offline/background operation and Android offline-first architecture changed the campaign toward **offline continuity**. The sources support local data, service workers, caching strategies, local source of truth, stale-state honesty, and explicit synchronization behavior. They do not prove that Stitch & Scale currently provides complete offline operation, background sync, conflict resolution, or a freshness indicator.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to test the local continuity contract, not to promise that every feature works offline or that synchronization is automatic.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | A designer records a critical project note while the network signal disappears, then checks what is local, what is pending, and what is not yet synced. |
| Desire | To keep the work safe and continue the next meaningful task without waiting for a perfect signal. |
| Obstacle | Intermittent connectivity, stale remote state, unclear pending writes, and the temptation to assume sync happened. |
| Stakes | A record can be duplicated, lost, or misread if local and network state are treated as identical without proof. |
| Change | The viewer moves from “offline is a feature” to “show me the local state and the honest sync state.” |
| Contradiction | The most trustworthy offline experience is not silent magic; it tells the user what is local, pending, stale, or unavailable. |
| Escalation | Signal drops → local record remains → pending/freshness state → current approved product proof or honest blocker. |
| Final image | A current approved offline/local/freshness surface beside a studio notebook, or an original illustrative device prop labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. |

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `correction-trace`, which focuses on a known post-release change and which copy carries it. `offline-continuity` changes the pain to **work continuity when network access disappears**; the hook to **“The signal can disappear without taking the work with it.”**; the visual metaphor from old/new pages to a device moving from signal to local record to an honestly labeled pending state; the proof surface from version/errata to current local-first, offline, freshness, or synchronization evidence; the emotional turn from maintenance accountability to calm resilience without false certainty; and the final line to **“Show me what stayed safe when the signal left.”** It is distinct from readability, chart parity, locale, gauge, reader handoff, false completion, and release-gate roster.

## Treatment and visual grammar

Open in a studio or market-like workspace with a device showing a generic illustrative project record. A signal indicator disappears. The record remains visible, but a second card asks whether it is local, pending, stale, or unavailable. If a current approved product capture is supplied, show one exact local/offline/freshness surface at readable scale with version/date and labels recorded in the manifest. Do not invent sync badges, conflict-resolution controls, cloud status, or offline guarantees.

Use one narrator family, restrained cuts, and physical continuity between device and notebook. Captions must not cover status labels, record content, hands, face, controls, or CTA. The visual climax is honest state visibility, not a green cloud check.
