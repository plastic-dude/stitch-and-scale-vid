## Research decision

Craft Yarn Council defines a schematic as a visual rendering with relevant measurements, an overview of shape and size, a fit reference, and a template for modification; it advises reviewing the schematic before beginning a project. Texas Tech University’s current accessible-PDF guide warns that “Print to PDF” can strip accessibility features, says automated tagging is not always accurate, and recommends checking reading order, tags, tables, actual text, and manual screen-reader review. Neither source proves Stitch & Scale has print-preview, PDF export, pagination, correct output, or accessibility capabilities.

The research changed the concept from generic PDF accessibility into **meaning preservation across screen and paper with manual review**. The film must not claim print fidelity, PDF accessibility, correct pagination, export success, or automated checking.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect one verified print-preview, export, or publication surface; it does not promise that the printed result matches the screen or remains accessible.

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `readability-under-load`, which concerns dense mobile reading and cognitive load. `paper-proof` changes the pain to **a document looking complete on screen while a printed page changes what a maker can see or use**; the hook to **“If the paper changes the pattern, the proof is not done.”**; the visual metaphor from mobile reading to `SCREEN / PRINT / READ / RECHECK` with a physical page and schematic/table; the proof surface from mobile readability to a current print-preview, export, or publication surface; the emotional turn from calm pacing under load to tactile rechecking across media; and the final line to **“Hold the page. Recheck the meaning.”**

It is distinct from `equivalent-meaning` because it is not alternate access semantics; from `chart-parity` because it is not chart-versus-text equivalence; from `size-boundary` and `gauge-reality` because it is not sizing or gauge logic; from `as-of` because it is not temporal currentness; from `notation-key` because it is not shorthand meaning; from `recovery-proof` because it is not restore evidence; and from `false-completion`, `release-gate-roster`, `correction-trace`, `material-notes`, `care-context`, `design-intent`, `sample-chain`, `measurement-moment`, `technique-profile`, `support-boundary`, `brief-before-knit`, `permission-line`, `decision-ledger`, `data-minimum`, `event-window`, `evidence-over-output`, `inherited-thread`, `offline-continuity`, `locale-drift`, and `reader-handoff` because the conflict is screen-to-paper preservation. It does not reopen, overwrite, rename, or consume the imported reservations.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on an original fictional pattern page labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI` containing a schematic-like outline, a small measurement block, and a table-shaped placeholder with no real pattern content. The page moves through four cards: `SCREEN`, `PRINT`, `READ`, and `RECHECK`. A paper fold or crop reveals a deliberately unresolved edge; no claim is made that a real export failed. A PDF-success, accessibility, or print-fidelity badge is rejected.

The product insert is optional and blocked until a current, versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact print-preview, export, page-break, chart-placement, schematic, table, reading-order, or output-review labels. If absent, retain `PRINT PROOF REQUIRED — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Any real pattern page, PDF, table, schematic, person, or founder voice/image requires source and rights review. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Use one narrator family. Captions must not cover paper, charts, measurements, proof, hands, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.

## Exact script

**0–7s — Narrator:** “If the paper changes the pattern, the proof is not done.”

**7–15s — Narrator:** “A clean screen is one surface. A working page is another.”

**15–25s — Narrator:** “Check the chart. Check the measurements. Check the table. Then read the page.”

**25–31s — Narrator:** “An automated check can help. It cannot replace holding the result.”

**31–40s — Narrator:** “If Stitch & Scale shows a current print or export surface, show exactly what it shows. If not, keep the claim open.”
