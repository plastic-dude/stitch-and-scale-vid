## Research decision

Stripe documents separate payment lifecycle states and says later refunds and disputes may change what appears in a dashboard even after a payment flow succeeds.[335] Stripe separately distinguishes canceling an incomplete payment from refunding a succeeded payment, and notes that refunds can be pending or fail.[336] Craft Yarn Council separates negotiated fees, a completed project, an invoice, and a signed design agreement/contract.[337] Stitch & Scale’s internal records contract requires stable IDs, date, amount/quantity, currency/unit, counterparty, source note, recorded-versus-estimated separation, and historical preservation.[342] Current platform and caption guidance supports a short vertical master with human-reviewed captions.[338] [339] [340] [341] None of this proves current Stitch & Scale payment behavior.

## Difference from nearest prior angle

The nearest prior angle is `submission-state`, which distinguishes a sent project from acceptance, contract, publication, or rights transfer. `payment-state` changes the pain to **confusing an agreement, invoice, due amount, successful payment, refund, dispute, or promise as one settled money fact**. The hook changes to **“Paid is not the whole story.”** The visual metaphor changes from project lifecycle states to a money-event timeline: `AGREED → INVOICED → DUE → DEPOSIT RECEIVED → PARTIALLY PAID → PAID → REFUNDED / DISPUTED / FAILED / WRITTEN OFF`. The proof surface changes from submission context to payment-context fields with project, counterparty, amount, currency, event, status, due/paid dates, refund/dispute, source, and notes. The emotional turn changes from separating editorial/rights outcomes to preserving truthful money history without claiming settlement or tax treatment. The final line changes to **“Name the payment state before you count the money.”**

It is distinct from `price-basis` because it is not about what a price is for; from `honest-price` because it is not hidden work and fees; from `decision-ledger` because it is specifically the lifecycle of a money event; from `work-scope` because it is not tester labor; from `availability-status` because it is not supply truth; from `value-state` because it is not blank/zero/invalid field semantics; from `quantity-basis` because it is not required/planned/actual quantity; and from `first-record` because it is not an empty workspace. It does not reopen or reuse `false-completion` or `release-gate-roster`.
