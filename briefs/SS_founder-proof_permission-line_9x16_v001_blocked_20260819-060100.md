## Research decision

ACID’s current UK-focused guide for crochet and knitting designers distinguishes protected creative works from individual techniques and stresses clear permissions, dated drafts, notes, photographs, and evidence trails. GOV.UK / Intellectual Property Office guidance separates intellectual-property rights, copyright, confidential information, disclosure, identification/control, and infringement/defence as distinct management areas. These sources do not establish universal law, legal validity of any wording, or any current Stitch & Scale rights-management feature.

The research changed the direction from “protect the pattern” to **make the permission legible before the pattern leaves**. The film may use illustrative questions such as `VIEW`, `EDIT`, and `SHARE`, but they must not be presented as current controls or legally sufficient terms.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect one verified share/permission/usage surface; it does not promise that the product prevents copying, enforces licensing, controls access, or proves ownership.

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `sample-chain`, which concerns the physical location, custodian, and return status of a sample. `permission-line` changes the pain to **an ambiguous permission boundary when a draft or pattern leaves the workspace**; the hook to **“Sharing the pattern is not the same as giving away the right to use it.”**; the visual metaphor from a physical sample handoff to a draft envelope with `VIEW`, `EDIT`, `SHARE`, and `ASK FIRST` cards; the proof surface from a sample/handoff ledger to a current share, permission, or usage-terms surface; the emotional turn from tactile custody to clear collaboration boundaries; and the final line to **“Name the permission before you press send.”**

It is distinct from `sample-chain` because it concerns intended use rather than physical location; from `recovery-proof` because it concerns who may use a work rather than whether a file can be restored; from `inherited-thread` because it is not provenance; from `evidence-over-output` because it is not generated language versus craft proof; and from `false-completion` / `release-gate-roster` because it is not a release or approval roster. It does not reopen, overwrite, rename, or consume the imported reservations.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on an original draft envelope or clean paper bundle carrying a fictional identifier such as `DRAFT A-01`, permanently labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. A hand moves it toward a phone or envelope flap. The motion pauses as three separate cards appear: `VIEW`, `EDIT`, `SHARE`; a fourth boundary card reads `ASK FIRST`. These are illustrative permission questions, not current product controls and not legal terms.

Do not show real copyrighted patterns, customer files, private links, identifiable collaborators, credentials, email addresses, platform permissions, or third-party licensing marks. A current product insert is optional and blocked until a versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact share or usage labels. If absent, retain `PERMISSION PROOF REQUIRED — NOT PRODUCT UI`. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Use one narrator family. Captions must not cover the draft ID, permission cards, hands, proof, controls, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.
