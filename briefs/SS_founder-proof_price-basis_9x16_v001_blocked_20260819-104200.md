## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the refreshed repository. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment through the free demo and early-access cohort remains the campaign objective. ## Research decision

Etsy’s current policy separates listing, transaction, payment-related, advertising/promotional, tax, and other fees, and describes its transaction fee in relation to displayed price plus specific charges.[178] The Craft Yarn Council designer-pitfalls URL currently redirects to a migration notice, so it cannot support specific industry-cost claims.[179] Stitch & Scale’s internal records contract distinguishes recorded transactions, estimates, planned costs, and designer assumptions, while requiring dates, currencies, sources, and stable identifiers.[180] These sources support explicit economic bases and evidence statuses, but do not prove that Stitch & Scale exposes pricing, fee, tax, or profitability behavior.

## Concept and dramatic engine

| Engine element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | A fictional price card is split into economic bases and evidence statuses. |
| Desire | To know what a number represents before relying on it. |
| Obstacle | Displayed price, recorded cost, platform fee, tax/other charge, and estimate can look like one total. |
| Stakes | A designer may mistake a price-like number for profit, income, or a settled record; no outcome is claimed. |
| Change | The fictional card labels each basis, date, currency, and source. |
| Contradiction | One number can be exact while its category is wrong. |
| Escalation | Bare price → basis cards → recorded/estimated status → source/date → proof or blocker. |
| Final image | `BASIS VISIBLE — RECORD REVIEWED`. |

## Difference from nearest prior angle

The nearest prior angle is `basis-label`, which focuses on the basis of a measurement or calculation number. `price-basis` changes the pain to **which economic category and evidence status a price-like number belongs to**. The hook changes to **“The price is not the whole story.”** The visual metaphor changes from calculation provenance to **`DISPLAYED PRICE / RECORDED COST / PLATFORM FEE / TAX OR OTHER CHARGE / ESTIMATE`**. The proof surface changes from a number-basis label to a current business-record or price/cost surface with date, currency, source, and status labels. The emotional turn changes from understanding a calculation to making a business record accountable without promising profit. The final line changes to **“Name the basis before you name the price.”**

It is distinct from `locale-number-format` because it is economic category and evidence status rather than punctuation and locale; from `first-record` because it is basis separation rather than first capture; from `credit-line` because it is financial record provenance rather than attribution; and from imported `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster` because it is not completion or evidence-roster accountability. Neither imported concept is reopened, renamed, overwritten, or approved.

## Treatment and production bible

Open on a neutral tabletop with an original abstract card marked `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. The fictional card reads `PRICE?` and is then separated into `DISPLAYED PRICE`, `RECORDED COST`, `PLATFORM FEE`, `TAX / OTHER CHARGE`, and `ESTIMATE`. Add `DATE / CURRENCY / SOURCE` cards. The final card reads `BASIS VISIBLE — RECORD REVIEWED`.

A real Stitch & Scale business-record or price/cost surface may replace the fictional card only if a current, approved, non-private capture or proof-registry entry verifies the exact surface, labels, product version/date, and rights to show it. If proof is absent, retain the blocker card and disclaimer. No pricing recommendation, accounting, tax, profit, margin, reconciliation, platform-fee computation, currency-conversion, or financial-outcome claim is permitted.

Use one narrator family. David may appear only with authorization and remains described as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Captions must not cover price cards, currency, dates, source labels, proof, tables, buttons, menus, logos, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free versions. Generated text remains illustrative and cannot substitute for current product proof.

## Caption map and audio plan

Use one narrator family with close, dry narration. Sound motif: a pencil underlines `DISPLAYED PRICE`, a soft click separates `RECORDED COST`, and a page turn lands on `SOURCE`. No profit-success chime, margin stamp, payment-confirmed cue, or income bed. Check for voice drift, doubled words, overlaps, accent changes, inconsistent mic distance, and room-tone jumps.

Captions are narration-verbatim, sentence by sentence, inside a declared safe zone that never covers price cards, currencies, dates, source labels, proof, tables, or CTA. Keep one basis family on screen at a time for mobile reading. Retain caption-on and caption-free review masters. YouTube’s official help supports uploading or editing captions; automatic captions are not accepted as final. TikTok and Instagram/Reels controls and overlays remain approval-time validation gates.
