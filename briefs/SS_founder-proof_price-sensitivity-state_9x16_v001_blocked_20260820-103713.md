## Dramatic engine before camera

**Subject:** A fictional `PRICE SENSITIVITY` card with one output marker and one changing input.
**Desire:** Know whether a projected outcome is stable enough to discuss or still needs a human decision.
**Obstacle:** Material, labor, overhead, fee, volume, or sales inputs can be estimated or move independently while the total retains a false aura of precision.
**Stakes:** A designer may treat a derived estimate as a recorded business fact. The film must not claim this happened to a Stitch & Scale user.
**Change:** Move from one polished total to a visible input delta, source/state label, affected output, and decision owner.
**Contradiction:** A more precise-looking number can be less honest if its most important input is hidden.
**Escalation:** Stable output card; one input changes; output marker moves; source and state appear; `NOT FINAL — DECISION NEEDED` replaces any approval cue.
**Final image:** `INPUT CHANGED → OUTPUT MOVED → HUMAN DECISION`, with `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Treatment

Open on a quiet cream desk. A paper card titled `PRICE SENSITIVITY` sits beside a pencil and a calculator-shaped prop with no display. The narrator says, “A price can move before the label does.” The hand writes `OUTPUT: PROJECTED PRICE` and places a neutral marker on a horizontal line. A second card reads `INPUT CHANGED: SAMPLE / LABOR / FEE` without a number. The output marker shifts. The card now shows `SOURCE: RECORD OR ESTIMATE`, `STATE: ESTIMATED`, and `EFFECT ON RESULT: MOVED`. A final strip reads `ASSUMPTION: REVIEW`, `DECISION OWNER: DESIGNER`, and `NOT FINAL — DECISION NEEDED`. The narrator refuses a green approval badge: “This is a sensitivity check, not a final price.” The final frame reads `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. The founder invitation asks founding testers which input they would want preserved when a derived business number changes; it does not claim a finished finance feature.
