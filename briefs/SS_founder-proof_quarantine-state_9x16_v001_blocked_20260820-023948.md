## Research-informed distinction

Pass 198 focused on a changed upstream dependency and the need to review a downstream decision. Pass 199 moves to the control state after uncertainty is known: the record remains visible for inspection and correction but is separated from reuse or delivery. GOV.UK error guidance recommends explaining what went wrong and how to fix it, preserving both passing and failing inputs, and keeping messages specific and actionable. Craft Yarn Council guidance identifies missing math, gauge, notes, records, charts, schematics, and permissions as distinct pitfalls and submission boundaries. [1] [2] [3]

> **Do not erase the uncertain record. Isolate it, explain it, and keep it out of reuse until it is reviewed.**

These sources inform general workflow education only. They do not establish that Stitch & Scale currently exposes quarantine, hold, nonconforming-output control, archive isolation, error recovery, reviewer assignment, or reuse restrictions.

## Dramatic engine

| Engine element | Design |
|---|---|
| Subject | A neutral uncertain record and a marked hold tray. |
| Desire | Keep the record available for correction without letting it travel as if it were ready. |
| Obstacle | The record looks finished enough to reuse, but its reason, evidence, correction, scope, owner, and review state are incomplete. |
| Stakes | Silent reuse can turn one unresolved record into a later assumption; the film must not claim a real incident or product outcome. |

## Treatment and beats

The film opens on a neutral tabletop. A record card reads `WORKING RECORD — ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. It sits in the shared work area beside neutral cards for calculation, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, and test knitting. A hand begins to slide the record toward a `READY TO REUSE` area, then stops when a concern card appears.

The concern is specific but illustrative: `REASON: GAUGE NOTE MISSING`. A second card reads `EVIDENCE: NOT SUPPLIED`. The record is moved into a clearly separated tray marked `HOLD — DO NOT REUSE`. Its reason and evidence cards remain physically attached. A correction card, reuse-scope card, owner card, and review card appear; no card claims a product feature or human decision actually occurred.

## Visual grammar and coverage

Use overhead and hands-only side-angle coverage, neutral paper, high contrast, sentence-case labels, and dedicated readable inserts for record ID, reason, evidence, correction, reuse scope, owner, review status, and disposition. Capture a wide work-area master, a macro of the concern card attaching, a close hold-tray transition, a human-gate boundary insert, a clean `HOLD — DO NOT REUSE` card, a caption-free proof pass, and a final disclaimer frame. Captions may not cover hands, cards, labels, numbers, logos, controls, menus, or the primary action.

Primary location is a quiet tabletop with neutral light and no private or branded material. Alternatives are a wall-mounted hold board, a no-hand version, or text-only original cards. Do not substitute a screenshot, copyrighted pattern, customer record, private message, logo, or current UI capture without approved proof and rights.

## Sound motif and audio plan

Use one approved narrator family only, restrained card movement, a soft tray-placement cue when the record is isolated, and a neutral hold tone when `REVIEW_STATUS: PENDING` and `DISPOSITION: HOLD — DO NOT REUSE` appear. No unlicensed music. Record room tone before and after each setup. Reject mixed voices, doubled words, overlaps, room-tone jumps, clipped consonants, stale UI, unsupported claims, and effects that conceal a defect. Deliver caption-on and caption-free review versions. Human-review captions; automatic captions are only a draft.
