## Research decision

FTC guidance says endorsements must be honest and not misleading, must reflect the endorser’s honest opinion, cannot support claims the marketer could not make, and may require clear disclosure of unexpected material connections.[351] The Copyright Office separates asking a copyright owner for permission from fair-use analysis and does not treat quotes as automatically permitted.[352] CYC separates submission, acceptance, fee discussion, contract, and project work.[353] Current platform and caption guidance supports a short vertical master with human-reviewed captions.[354] [355] [356] [357] Internal contracts require explicit source/date, privacy, rights/credit, export, and human evidence boundaries.[358] None of this proves current Stitch & Scale quote-consent behavior.

## Difference from nearest prior angle

The nearest prior angle is `permission-line`, which focuses on who may act or access a record. `quote-permission` changes the pain to **whether feedback may be reused publicly and exactly under what scope**. The hook changes to **“Feedback is not permission.”** The visual metaphor changes from an access boundary to a quote card passing through `PRIVATE FEEDBACK → QUOTE CANDIDATE → CONSENT SCOPE → DISCLOSURE/REDACTION → PUBLISHED / HOLD`. The proof surface changes from actor permission to a current evidence-rights record. The emotional turn changes from access control to respect for the speaker’s actual words and intended audience. The final line changes to **“Record the scope before you publish the quote.”**

It is distinct from `credit-line` because attribution is only one field; from `feedback-loop` because it is not routing or reviewing feedback; from `source-note` because it is not general provenance; from `privacy-by-default` because it is not local data ownership; from `submission-state` because it is not editorial or contract lifecycle; from `next-owner` because it is not the next action actor; and from `false-completion` or `release-gate-roster` because it is not a human release-gate roster. It does not reopen or reuse either imported concept.
