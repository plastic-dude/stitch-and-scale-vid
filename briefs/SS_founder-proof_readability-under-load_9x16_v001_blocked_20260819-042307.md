## Research decision

Fresh research on WCAG 2.2 contrast guidance and knitwear-specific accessibility practice changed the campaign toward **readability under load**. The sources support contrast, uncluttered layouts, defined abbreviations, full written instructions alongside charts, large keys, and testing patterns without charts. They do not prove that Stitch & Scale currently offers an accessible PDF mode, reflow, contrast checking, or chart-to-text equivalence.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect a pattern in the conditions where readers actually use it, not to promise accessibility compliance or universal usability.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | One pattern page under competing conditions: small screen, chart, key, hands, light, and attention. |
| Desire | To keep the next instruction usable when the reader is not sitting in a perfect review room. |
| Obstacle | Dense layout, low contrast, undefined shorthand, tiny keys, and chart-only dependence can make an otherwise correct pattern hard to use. |
| Stakes | A reader may lose the line, miss a key, or abandon the instruction; no product guarantee may be implied. |
| Change | The viewer moves from “it fits on the page” to “can a person still use it under load?” |
| Contradiction | A beautiful page can be less usable than a plain one when the reader needs clarity, zoom, or a written alternative. |
| Escalation | Busy desk → text/chart collision → clean single information family → current approved proof or honest blocker. |
| Final image | A readable current PDF/accessibility/preflight surface beside a calm, uncluttered instruction; otherwise an original illustrative prop labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. |

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `locale-drift`, which focuses on meaning changing across locale terminology and keys. `readability-under-load` changes the pain to **legibility and cognitive/visual load under real use**; the hook to **“A pattern can be correct and still be hard to use.”**; the visual metaphor from aligned locale pages to a page competing with glare, zoom, hands, chart, and key; the proof surface from locale/export comparison to current accessible-PDF, contrast, reflow, chart/key, or preflight evidence; the emotional turn from inclusive language confidence to relief through uncluttered use; and the final line to **“Make the next line easy to find.”** It is distinct from reader-handoff, correction-trace, gauge-reality, false-completion, and release-gate-roster.

## Treatment and visual grammar

Open with a pattern page on a phone beside yarn and needles. A hand zooms, loses the line, searches a tiny key, and then the frame resets to one readable information family. Props must be original and marked illustrative; no fabricated Stitch & Scale UI. If a current approved product capture exists, show one exact readable surface at a time with current labels, values, and version/date. Do not claim accessibility certification, screen-reader support, contrast compliance, reflow, or chart-to-text equivalence without proof.

Use restrained cuts and clear negative space. Captions must not cover chart, key, text, hands, face, counts, controls, or CTA. The visual climax is not a green accessibility badge; it is a reader finding the next line.

## Sound and audio plan

Begin with page rustle and small desk interruptions, then settle into one pencil underline and stable room tone. One narrator family, no music under the proof sentence, no mixed voices, no synthetic accessibility sounds, and no room-tone jumps. Produce caption-on and caption-free review versions.
