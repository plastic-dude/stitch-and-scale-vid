## Research decision

Fresh research on Knitty’s June 17, 2026 submission guidance, Craft Yarn Council sizing guidance, Craft Yarn Council knit-chart-symbol guidance, W3C caption guidance, and current YouTube caption guidance changed the decision toward **reader handoff and interpretation**. The sources support the importance of mathematically coherent, followable patterns; style-guide discipline; technical-editor guidance; reader-facing sizing, ease, schematics, legends, and symbols; and human-reviewed captions. They do not prove that Stitch & Scale currently checks any of those surfaces.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film asks designers to inspect the transition from designer intent to reader action. It does not promise a finished pattern, standards compliance, automatic chart checking, guaranteed fit, or reader success.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | A pattern instruction changing hands from its author to an unknown reader; David remains a truthful developer outsider. |
| Desire | To make the next reader decision understandable without requiring the reader to have been in the designer’s head. |
| Obstacle | A designer knows the intended size, ease, chart convention, and shorthand; a reader sees only the published instruction and may have to infer the missing bridge. |
| Stakes | A beautiful design can still create hesitation, mis-sizing, or a wrong next action when the handoff is unclear. The film must not imply the product has solved this without proof. |
| Change | The viewer moves from “I know what I meant” to “show what the reader can actually use.” |
| Contradiction | The more obvious an instruction feels to its author, the less visible its missing explanation may be to a new reader. |
| Escalation | Designer hand → size/ease choice → chart key or schematic pause → invitation to find the reader’s guess. |
| Final image | A current, readable reader-facing product or export-preflight surface beside one clearly identified point of reader uncertainty. If no approved current capture exists, use a neutral paper prop marked illustrative and do not imply it is product UI. |

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior concept is `publication-second-shift`, which made the broad post-design publication workload visible. The imported `false-completion` concept narrowed that to a finished-looking status that concealed an unresolved human gate. The imported `release-gate-roster` narrowed it again to ownership of tester evidence by size. `reader-handoff` changes the pain to **interpretation after authorship**; the hook to **“The person who made the pattern already knows what it means. The reader doesn’t.”**; the visual metaphor from checkbox/roster to a physical handoff across a desk; the proof surface from human-gate status to one reader-facing size, ease, schematic, chart-key, or instruction surface; the emotional turn from exposing false closure to empathy for the person arriving cold; and the final line to **“Show me where the reader has to guess.”** It is not a noun-swapped duplicate.

## Treatment and visual grammar

The film opens on two hands across a quiet desk: one places a pattern page down and the other receives it. David’s face or voice introduces the outsider truth without claiming craft authority. The paper prop may show an illustrative generic line such as “choose your size,” but it must not resemble current product UI or carry generated Stitch & Scale labels. The tension arrives when the receiving hand pauses at a size/ease choice, a chart key, or a schematic question. A verified current product capture may replace the prop only after the proof registry identifies its exact version, labels, controls, and visible values.

Use restrained cuts, close readable proof, and no fake app chrome, fabricated UI, glitch effects, or generated data. The product surface, if supplied, gets one information family per shot: size/ease, schematic, chart key, or instruction; never a dense full-screen table as the only proof. Captions stay away from hands, paper, face, proof, numbers, controls, and platform overlays.

## Beat and coverage plan

| Beat | Approx. time | Action | Proof purpose | Coverage |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| The handoff | 0–6s | A real paper pattern page crosses the desk from author-side to reader-side. | Establish authorship versus use without a product claim. | Overhead macro, two hands, clean room tone. |
| Author knowledge | 6–13s | David says the author already knows what the line means. | State the human contradiction. | Medium close-up; face clear of captions. |
| Reader pause | 13–21s | Receiving hand pauses at an illustrative “choose your size” or chart-key prop. | Make interpretation, not status, the obstacle. | Locked close-up; label prop as illustrative in production notes. |
| Product proof or blocker | 21–32s | One current approved reader-facing or preflight capture at readable scale, if supplied. | Show only verified UI truth. | Mobile-readable close-up; version and state in manifest. |
| Human gate | 32–37s | A card names the evidence that still belongs to a human: technical editing, print review, chart/schematic reading, or test knitting. | Keep distinct human evidence gates distinct. | One card at a time; never collapse them into “accuracy.” |
| Invitation | 37–42s | Return to the handoff and ask where the reader still has to guess. | Invite tester inspection without promising completion. | Clear end card; no fake button or urgency. |

## Sound and audio plan

Use one original, rights-cleared sound motif: a soft paper transfer and one pencil underline. David’s narration must come from one continuous voice family with stable room tone and microphone distance. No overlapping voices, music under the proof sentence, synthetic crowd, doubled words, or room-tone jumps. Deliver caption-on and caption-free review versions. The caption file must be human-reviewed; automatic captions may be an editable starting point only.

## Caption plan

Safe zone: keep captions within the platform-validated central safe area, normally lower third unless proof occupies it; move to a clear upper zone only after visual review. Use no more than two lines, preserve spoken wording, and identify speakers if more than one voice is used. Never cover hands, paper, face, numbers, labels, charts, schematics, controls, logos, or primary actions.
