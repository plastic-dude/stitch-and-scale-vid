## Research-informed premise

The U.S. Small Business Administration separates estimated fixed costs, selling price, projected unit sales, and estimated variable cost per unit in break-even planning. It also states that break-even analysis is an estimate for planning and is not intended to determine accounting or financing with complete accuracy before costs and production occur. [1] SBA business-plan guidance likewise separates cost structure, revenue streams, projections, and recorded financial statements. [2]

Craft Yarn Council guidance asks designers to keep notes while working rather than rely on memory; it separately calls for materials and yarn amounts, sizing, math checks, technical editing, schematics, invoices, negotiated design fees, submissions, and return shipping. [3] [4] The research supports a general workflow lesson: label whether a value is **recorded**, **estimated**, **derived**, or **assumed**. It does not prove that Stitch & Scale currently tracks these states or performs accounting.

> **A number can be useful without being settled.**

## Treatment and beats

The film opens on four original cards around a fictional design ledger. A single number tries to move from `ESTIMATED` into `DERIVED`; the transition pauses until its period, source note, and formula are visible. A `RECORDED` receipt remains distinct from a `DERIVED` view, while an `ASSUMED` projected-sales card remains explicitly unverified. The end is not a profitability claim; it is an invitation to label the record honestly.
