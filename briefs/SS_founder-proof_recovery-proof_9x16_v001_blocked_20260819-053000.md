## Research decision

Fresh research on CISA backup guidance and UK NCSC safe-export guidance changed the concept from generic “your data is yours” language to **recoverable ownership**. CISA recommends testing backup procedures and verifying integrity, while NCSC stresses export scope, destination, requester, authorisation, classification, and protection of sensitive information. Neither source proves any current Stitch & Scale backup, encryption, restore, sync, portability, or interoperability capability.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect recovery evidence; it does not promise that a file is automatically backed up, portable, encrypted, synchronized, or restorable.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | A designer prepares a pattern bundle to leave the workspace and asks whether it can be safely recovered, not merely downloaded. |
| Desire | To keep control of the work and know what would return after loss, migration, or interruption. |
| Obstacle | An export can be incomplete, too broad, sent to the wrong destination, unreadable, or never tested after creation. |
| Stakes | A designer can possess a file while still lacking a verified recovery path or exposing sensitive records. |
| Change | The viewer moves from “I exported it” to “I inspected scope, destination, integrity, and restoration.” |
| Contradiction | Ownership is not a slogan; it is a recoverable path that survives a test. |
| Escalation | Bundle selected → scope/destination checked → export leaves workspace → clean restore attempt → current proof or honest blocker. |
| Final image | The same pattern bundle returns into a clean workspace beside a visible `RESTORE TEST` result, or a blocker card if proof is absent. |

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `offline-continuity`, which concerns work remaining usable and honestly labeled when network access disappears. `recovery-proof` changes the pain to **ownership without verified recovery or safe export**; the hook to **“A file is not a backup until you can bring the work back.”**; the visual metaphor from connected/local/pending device state to a pattern bundle leaving and returning through a scope, destination, and restore check; the proof surface from offline/freshness/synchronization behavior to a current export, backup, or restore surface; the emotional turn from calm resilience under signal loss to quiet protection through tested recoverability; and the final line to **“Show me what came back.”**

It is distinct from `decision-ledger` because it concerns recoverability rather than truth-state classification; from `event-window` because it concerns ownership after loss rather than timing; from `correction-trace` because it is not version history; from `inherited-thread` because it is not founder provenance; and from imported `false-completion` / `release-gate-roster` because it is not a human release gate or roster. The imported concepts remain blocked and their filenames remain occupied.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on a clean, original pattern-bundle prop beside a device. A hand selects a deliberately narrow scope card: `PATTERN RECORD ONLY`, `DESTINATION: OWNER DEVICE`, `RESTORE TEST: PENDING`. These are illustrative labels and must be persistently marked `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Do not show private receipts, customer records, addresses, credentials, hidden metadata, personal family material, or a fake cloud-success badge.

The bundle moves from workspace to a neutral removable-media or file-envelope prop, then returns to a clean workspace. The restore sequence is a human test of the illustrative prop, not proof of product behavior. The product insert is optional and blocked until a current, versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact export/backup/restore labels. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; no craft expertise claim. Use one narrator family. Captions must not cover bundle scope, destination, proof result, hands, controls, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.
