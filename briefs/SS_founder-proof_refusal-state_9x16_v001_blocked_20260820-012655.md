## Concept and claim boundary

> “A refusal without a reason or safe next action is an ambiguous stop.”

A fictional request card meets a refusal card. The first refusal only says `DENIED`. The card then expands to show the request, protected scope, reason, owner, next safe action, review, status, and disposition. The emotional turn is not that the refusal is wrong; it is that a refusal must be **specific enough to protect a boundary without abandoning the person who encountered it**. The story is about refusal clarity, not product access enforcement, privacy compliance, eligibility decisions, error handling, safe alternatives, or permission capabilities.

NIST describes the Privacy Framework as a voluntary tool to help organizations improve individual privacy through enterprise risk management [915]. NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 lists Access Control and PII Processing and Transparency among control families and warns that mappings and crosswalks are not one-to-one and that scope and intended use matter [916]. GOV.UK Design System guidance says an error message should explain what went wrong and how to fix it, while permission or eligibility problems should be explained with useful next steps rather than treated as ordinary field errors [917]. These sources motivate the fictional refusal card and production review only; none proves that Stitch & Scale implements the Privacy Framework, access enforcement, permission messaging, error handling, or compliance.

All requests, scopes, reasons, owners, next actions, review states, statuses, and dispositions are fictional. Generated text is not UI truth. Exact product labels, warnings, and controls require a current approved product capture or proof-registry entry. David is a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not described as a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, technical editor, or tester.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | One fictional request and the refusal card that responds to it |
| Desire | Understand what the refusal protects and what can safely happen next |

| Final image | A refusal card with a protected-scope boundary and one safe next-action arrow |

## Treatment and beat sheet

Use a neutral evidence board with a fictional request card entering a red boundary. The first frame is intentionally insufficient: `DENIED`. The card opens into a bounded refusal. The safe next action is specific but fictional and non-operational. No permission grant, bypass, success state, user identity, private record, or current UI appears.

## Visual grammar and coverage plan

Use original abstract request and refusal cards, a single protected-scope boundary, one safe-next-action arrow, neutral dark slate with amber for undefined fields and red only for `REFUSAL BOUNDARY INCOMPLETE`. There is no green grant, bypass, success, eligible, correct, safe, secure, or approved state. Captions occupy a fixed lane away from `REQUEST`, `DENIED`, `SCOPE`, `REASON`, `OWNER`, `NEXT_SAFE_ACTION`, `REVIEWED`, `STATUS`, and `DISPOSITION`, tables, numbers, labels, buttons, menus, hands, and primary actions.

Coverage: locked overhead master; request-card insert; ambiguous-denial close-up; boundary reveal; owner field; safe-next-action insert; review/status/disposition hold; caption-free clean master; caption-on review master; mobile crop review; accessibility review frame. Alternative coverage is a static refusal card with the protected scope and one safe-next-action line if motion weakens legibility. Reject stale UI, caption collision, fake security, invented scope or reason, unauthorized bypass cues, universal language, mixed voices, doubled words, overlaps, room-tone jumps, and unreadable fields.

## Locations, arrangements, sound motif, and approved-assets plan

Location is a controlled desk or studio surface with no private material. Arrangement is original abstract request/refusal cards, a protected-scope boundary, cleared type, one cleared narrator, and original or cleared audio. Sound motif: one low refusal tone, silence when scope is undefined, one restrained boundary tick, and a neutral review tone. No alarm, security-warning fanfare, grant chime, bypass cue, or certainty cue. No final asset is approved.
