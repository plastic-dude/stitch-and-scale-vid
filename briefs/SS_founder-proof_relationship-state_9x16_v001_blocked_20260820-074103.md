## Research-informed premise

The Craft Yarn Council’s current project checklist separates the actual project, yarn labels and skein bands, gauge swatch, electronic instructions, printout, measurements, charts, schematic, invoice, agreement or contract, and return shipping. It also asks designers to reconfirm yarn, color, dye lot, sample size, and notes with the editor or design director. [1] Its “Where Do You Start?” guidance distinguishes idea, sketch, swatch, sample, submission, acceptance, possible contract, and later project stages, and says company-specific requirements apply. [2] These are related workflow records, not one interchangeable record.

CYC standards seek uniformity in yarn and pattern labeling but do not define an application data model or prove relationship automation. [3] Knitty’s current June 17, 2026 submission guidance separately distinguishes notes, sizes, charts, schematics, rights, technical editing, test knitting, and publication. [4]

## Dramatic engine

| Engine element | Director decision |

| Desire | Keep related records connected without merging their identities or claims. |
| Obstacle | A generic link or shared label hides direction, stage, owner, destination, and limits. |
| Stakes | A gauge, expense, submission, or test-knit record may be overread as pattern correctness, acceptance, payment, custody, or publication. No actual failure is claimed. |

## Treatment, beats, and visual grammar

Use one unbranded tabletop, one design card, one sample card, one gauge card, one pattern card, one expense card, one submission card, one test-knit card, and one unrelated receipt. All cards are original illustrative props. Open on a loose cluster that looks like one project. A generic `LINKED` label is challenged; typed connectors then reveal different meanings and directions.
