## Research-informed premise

NIST’s technical testing guide separates planning and conducting tests, analyzing findings, and developing mitigation strategies, while emphasizing the benefits and limitations of test techniques. [1] NIST SP 800-61r3 frames response around preparation, detection, response, recovery, and continual improvement; it is used here only as a bounded process analogy, not as a Stitch & Scale security or incident-response claim. [2]

CISA’s 10 June 2026 directive says remediation should be prioritized through explicit risk criteria, tracked with clear definitions and timelines, and followed by checks for residual compromise rather than assuming that applying a patch ends the risk. [3] Craft Yarn Council guidance says to keep notes, discuss problems or alternatives with the manufacturer or editor, match project gauge, and preserve distinct project, gauge, instruction, schematic, invoice, and return responsibilities. [4]

Internal Stitch & Scale QA guidance requires a defect ledger with code, severity, source, evidence, affected section or sizes, reproduction steps, and disposition of **verified**, **needs designer decision**, or **requires test knitting**. It separates calculation, record, technical edit, and test knit. [5]

> **Fixed is a status word. Remediation needs the trail.**

## Treatment and beats

The film opens on a fictional defect card stamped `FIXED`. The camera pulls back and reveals blank lanes for severity, affected scope, action, owner, evidence, retest, and residual limit. A remediation card records the action without claiming closure. A test or technical-edit card appears as one evidence lane, not a universal approval. The final line names the finding, remedy, evidence, and remaining review.

| Time | Beat | Image | Spoken idea | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4s | False closure | Fictional defect card with `FIXED` stamp | “Fixed is a status word. Remediation needs the trail.” | General workflow education |
| 4–10s | Finding | `FINDING_ID`, `FINDING_TYPE`, `SEVERITY`, and `SOURCE` cards appear | “What was found, how serious was it, and where did it come from?” | Fictional fields |
