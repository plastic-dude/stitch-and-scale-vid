## Research decision

Craft Yarn Council explains asterisks as repeat markers, brackets as repeated groups with a following count, and parentheses as context-dependent grouping; its examples show that the scope is carried by punctuation plus surrounding instruction. GOV.UK content guidance emphasizes user needs, clarity, format, and avoiding information that leaves users unable to find what they need. These sources support visible repeat boundaries, but they do not prove Stitch & Scale parses notation, highlights scope, validates repeats, detects size branches, or previews instructions.

## Concept and dramatic engine

| Engine element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | A compact repeat instruction contains a group and a stopping condition that are easy to misread. |
| Desire | To know exactly what belongs to the repeated sequence. |
| Obstacle | Asterisk, brackets, parentheses, repeat count, and exception text can distribute the meaning across a line. |
| Stakes | A maker may repeat too much, too little, or past the stated exception; no product outcome is claimed. |
| Change | The fictional card separates start, group, repeat, stop, and exception. |
| Contradiction | The smallest mark can control the largest part of the instruction. |
| Escalation | Asterisk appears → group is highlighted → count enters → stop/exception is named. |
| Final image | `SCOPE VISIBLE — REPEAT REVIEWED`. |

## Difference from nearest prior angle

The nearest prior angle is `count-check`, which focuses on whether stitch or row totals reconcile. `repeat-scope` changes the pain to **which tokens belong to a group, how often it repeats, where it ends, and what exception follows**. The hook changes to **“The asterisk is not the whole instruction.”** The visual metaphor changes from count arithmetic to **`START / GROUP / REPEAT / STOP / EXCEPTION`**. The proof surface changes from a count check to a notation scope display, repeat-group highlight, `repeat from` cue, grouping/parentheses explanation, size-specific branch, exception marker, or parsed-instruction preview. The emotional turn changes from numeric verification to inspectable sequence boundaries without parser certainty. The final line changes to **“Show what repeats before you repeat it.”**

It is distinct from `notation-key` because it is grouping and stopping scope rather than symbol definitions; from `direction-cue` because it is repeat membership rather than row traversal; from `chart-parity` because it is notation scope rather than chart/text agreement; from `prerequisite-state` because it is instruction grouping rather than readiness; and from imported `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster` because it is not completion or roster accountability. Neither imported concept is reopened, renamed, overwritten, or approved.

## Treatment and production bible

The asterisk is isolated; a bracket highlights a group; cards enter: `START`, `GROUP`, `REPEAT × N`, `STOP`, and `EXCEPTION`. The final card reads `SCOPE VISIBLE — REPEAT REVIEWED`.

A real Stitch & Scale notation display, scope highlight, parsed preview, or branch may replace the fictional card only if a current, approved, non-private capture or proof-registry entry verifies the exact surface, labels, product version/date, and rights to show it. Do not imply parser accuracy, automatic scope highlighting, repeat validation, size-branch detection, or count confirmation.
