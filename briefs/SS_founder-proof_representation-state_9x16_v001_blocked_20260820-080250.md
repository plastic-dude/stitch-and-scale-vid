## Research-informed premise

The Craft Yarn Council’s current “How to Read a Knitting Pattern” guidance treats abbreviations, terms, rows, rounds, side indicators, repeats, and shaping as scoped written instructions. [1] Its current chart-symbol guidance says charts may supplement or replace words, that standardized symbols have variations, and that a pattern key must be consulted. [2] Its current schematic guidance defines a schematic as a visual rendering with relevant measurements that provides size/shape overview, fit reference, and a modification template. [3]

CYC’s project checklist separately lists electronic instructions, printouts, measurements by size, charts, and schematics. [4] Its pattern-writing overview lists written patterns, video tutorials, charts, graphs, schematics, and combinations of these, then distinguishes notes, transcription, terminology/sizing checks, technical editing, and testing before release. [5] Knitty’s current June 17, 2026 submission guidance requires a text-only pattern document, separate image files, individual chart files when needed, and a schematic file for garments, while distinguishing editorial style, technical editing, test knitting, rights, and publication. [6]

W3C’s current tables tutorial says accessible data tables need structural header/data relationships and that visual cues alone are insufficient; its complex-image guidance calls for a two-part text alternative for images with substantial information. [7] [8] Representation presence is therefore not accessibility conformance, semantic parity, technical correctness, or publication readiness.

## Treatment, beats, and visual grammar

Use one unbranded tabletop, one fictional design ID, seven representation cards, one role/scope card, one reviewer card, and one limitation card. All cards are original illustrative props. Open on a tidy rail of formats that looks complete. Each card then reveals a different job and a different gap.

| Time | Beat | Image and action | Spoken idea | Evidence status |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5s | Format abundance | `PROSE`, `CHART`, `SCHEMATIC`, `TABLE`, `PHOTO`, `VIDEO` line up. | “A pattern can have more formats and still leave a gap.” | General representation principle. |
| 5–12s | Role appears | Cards gain `INSTRUCTIONS`, `PATTERN KEY`, `SIZE/SHAPE`, `MEASUREMENTS`, `CONTEXT`, `NOT PROVIDED`. | “Each format has a different job.” | CYC/Knitty synthesis. |
| 12–23s | Scope appears | `CHART / REPEAT ROWS`; `SCHEMATIC / FINISHED MEASUREMENTS`; `PHOTO / DESIGN CONTEXT`. | “Name the scope before you treat it as evidence.” | Bounded process principle. |
| 23–31s | Gaps visible | `VIDEO / NOT PROVIDED`; `TEXT ALTERNATIVE / REVIEW REQUIRED`. | “Presence is not the same as agreement or access.” | W3C accessibility boundary. |
| 31–39s | Human gates | `TECH EDIT`; `PRINT REVIEW`; `CHART READABILITY`; `SCHEMATIC SCALE`; `TEST KNIT`. | “The formats do not do the human review for you.” | Preserve distinct human gates. |
| 39–45s | Honest ending | Final card with type, role, presence, scope, limit, reviewer. | “Name the representation—and what it cannot prove.” | Blocked; no capability claim. |
