## Core creative distinction

The nearest recent prior is pass 243 `review-question-state`. Pass 243 asked what exactly a reviewer should check and separated question, subject, scope, expected evidence, response, reviewer, next check, and limit. Pass 244 asks a different question: **what does a saved copy or export prove, and what remains unproven until a restore test returns a correctly scoped, usable project?**

| Dimension | Pass 243 `review-question-state` | Pass 244 `restore-boundary-state` |
|---|---|---|
| Changed pain | Vague review requests that cannot be answered cleanly. | False confidence that a saved copy equals recoverable work. |
| Hook | “What exactly are you asking the reviewer to check?” | “A backup is not the same thing as getting your work back.” |
| Visual metaphor | A fictional review-question card. | A split fictional restore card: `FILE EXISTS` versus `RESTORE TEST`. |
| Proof surface | Question, subject, scope, expected evidence, response, reviewer, next check, limit. | Copy identity, version, scope, destination, restore attempt, opened result, and unresolved boundary. |
| Emotional turn | Relief through a question the evidence can answer. | Relief through keeping the copy while testing the return. |
| Final line | “Ask the question the evidence can actually answer.” | “Keep the copy. Test the return.” |

The angle is distinct from occupied historical slugs `backup-state`, `recovery-proof`, `offline-continuity`, `archival-state`, `retention-state`, and `resumption-state`. It does not claim backup, recovery, offline, archival, retention, or synchronization capability; it dramatizes the evidence boundary that a future product proof would need to show.

## Dramatic engine before camera

**Subject:** A fictional continuity record for one pattern project, represented by a paper card and an abstract, unlabeled file tile.  
**Desire:** Know whether the work can be recovered in the intended scope after a device change, mistake, or interruption.  
**Obstacle:** A saved, exported, or synced-looking record may not prove that the intended version, size data, chart, schematic, notes, or related assets can be restored and opened.  
**Stakes:** A designer may lose time, context, or trust in the continuity of a project; the video must not imply that this outcome has happened to a Stitch & Scale user.  
**Change:** Move from “I saved it” to “I can name and test the return.”  
**Contradiction:** The copy can exist while the recovery claim remains unresolved.  
**Escalation:** A reassuring `SAVED COPY` stamp appears; the card asks for version and scope; `RESTORE TEST` remains open; the founder declines to call it safe; the tester invitation narrows to one check.  
**Final image:** A split card with `FILE EXISTS` on the left, `RESTORE TEST: UNRESOLVED` on the right, and a small footer `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Treatment

Open on a dark tabletop with a single paper strip reading `RESTORE?`. A quiet founder voice says, “A backup is not the same thing as getting your work back.” The paper becomes a fictional continuity record, never a product screen. A `SAVED COPY` stamp is applied, but the voice asks for the project version, size/scope, destination, and the exact thing that was reopened. The paper splits into two columns: `FILE EXISTS` and `RESTORE TEST`. The left column is allowed to say `RECORDED`; the right remains `UNRESOLVED`. The founder admits the boundary: “I can show you a copy. I cannot call the return proven without a restore check.” The final beat invites founding testers to define the smallest meaningful continuity check in the current product, without implying that the product already offers it.
