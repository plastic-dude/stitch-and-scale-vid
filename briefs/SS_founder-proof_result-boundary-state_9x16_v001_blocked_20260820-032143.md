## Research-informed distinction

Pass 204 required intended use, criteria, test, supporting evidence, and limitations before assessing fit. Pass 205 makes the limitation operational: the record should state what the evidence supports, what it does not support, what uncertainty remains, and which uses or gates were not examined. NIST explains that traceability alone does not guarantee fitness for purpose and that results apply to the specific instrument or standard at the time of test unless otherwise stated. The UK Government Statistical Service recommends communicating what users can and cannot conclude and placing critical caveats prominently. W3C WCAG-EM requires defined scope, sample, evaluation, and reporting. Craft Yarn Council keeps gauge, instructions, and human craft checks distinct. [1] [2] [3] [4]

> **A result without its boundary invites a use it never tested.**

These sources inform general workflow education only. They do not establish that Stitch & Scale currently records uncertainty, displays limitation fields, prevents reuse, or produces traceable results.

## Treatment and beats

The film opens on a neutral tabletop. A finding card sits inside a bright frame labeled `TESTED SCOPE: ONE CHECK`. A grey outside area reads `NOT EVALUATED`. The result appears useful but does not occupy the whole board.

An evidence-chain card enters, followed by intended use, sample or scope, check performed, and limitations. A prominent `CANNOT_CONCLUDE` card lists other sizes, print, chart, schematic, and test knitting as outside this check. The final disposition stays held until current evidence and accountable review exist.
