## Research-informed distinction

Pass 201 bounded a reviewed clearance to the checked entity, purpose, and human-gate scope. Pass 202 adds the revalidation boundary: when a relevant input, material, recipient requirement, evidence state, or context changes, the old decision should not be silently reused. GOV.UK’s check-answers pattern returns changed sections to review before confirmation and keeps the transaction incomplete until confirmation. Craft Yarn Council guidance requires reconfirming yarn, color, dye lot, availability, sample size, notes, gauge, mathematics, and recipient-specific requirements. NIST continuous monitoring was identified as a relevant official topic, but substantive page content was not extracted in this pass because the official page returned a redirect; it is not used as proof of product behavior. [1] [2] [3]

> **A new input is not a new approval. It is a reason to check again.**

These sources inform general workflow education only. They do not establish that Stitch & Scale currently exposes review triggers, expiry semantics, continuous monitoring, change detection, automatic revalidation, or current trigger labels.

## Treatment and beats

The film opens on a neutral tabletop. A bounded clearance lane is marked `AUTHORIZED FOR: INTERNAL REVIEW / FOLLOW CHART 3 — SIZE M`. A neighboring card appears: `CHANGE_EVENT: MATERIAL OR DYE LOT CHANGED`. A red thread connects the change to the old lane.

The old clearance does not become automatically approved or automatically rejected. It changes to `RECHECK REQUIRED`. Cards identify the affected evidence, owner, and recheck status. The human gates remain separate, and the new decision stays pending until current evidence and accountable review exist.
