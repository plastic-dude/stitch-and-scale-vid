## Concept and claim boundary

> “Reviewable is not approved.”

Two fictional review cards describe the same craft record. One uses a stable field order; the other renames or shifts the same roles. The evidence may be readable, fresh, and sufficient, yet a reviewer cannot reliably compare the cards until the review target, scope, method, evidence, criteria, observation, decision, reviewer, reviewed-at, and limit use stable labels. The ending is a bounded human-review lesson, not a correctness, approval, accessibility-conformance, or product claim. The closing line is **“Name the review structure before you trust the review.”**

This package does not claim that Stitch & Scale exposes stable review templates, consistent labels, reviewer workflows, approval status, assessment automation, or accessibility conformance. Generated text is not UI truth. Exact product labels, controls, warnings, numbers, and behavior require a current approved product capture or proof-registry entry.

David may be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted. He must not be described as a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, technical editor, tester, or as using an unverified feature.

## Treatment and beat sheet

The treatment is an abstract repeatable-review lesson. A neutral board holds two cards labeled `REVIEW A` and `REVIEW B` for `RECORD R-001`. Their evidence is similar, but the field rails do not align. Stable labels and order are added, observation is separated from decision, and the final state remains `HUMAN REVIEW REQUIRED`.

## Visual grammar and coverage plan

Use a neutral evidence board, two repeated review cards, fixed field rails, stable-role labels highlighted when aligned, amber for renamed or displaced fields, red only for `REVIEW STRUCTURE UNSTABLE`, and no green approval state. No real UI, private records, recognizable people, copyrighted patterns, customer data, branded property, legal language, product claim, or reviewer identity. Captions occupy a fixed lane that never covers review target, scope, method, evidence, criteria, observation, decision, reviewer, reviewed-at, limits, tables, numbers, logos, buttons, menus, hands, or primary action.

Coverage: locked overhead master; two-card side-by-side; close readable proof holds for label drift; target/scope/method rails; evidence/criteria rails; observation/decision separation; reviewer/reviewed-at/limit hold; caption-free clean master; caption-on review master; accessibility review frame. Alternative coverage is a static aligned versus unstable card comparison if motion reduces legibility. Reject stale UI, caption collisions, mixed voices, doubled words, overlaps, room-tone jumps, unreadable proof, and unsupported claims rather than hiding defects with effects.
