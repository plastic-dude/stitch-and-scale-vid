## Core creative distinction

The nearest prior is pass 244 `restore-boundary-state`. Pass 244 asked whether a saved copy can be returned as the intended usable project. Pass 245 asks whether a decision inside that project remains understandable and challengeable after time, revision, or handoff.

| Dimension | Pass 244 `restore-boundary-state` | Pass 245 `revisitability-state` |
|---|---|---|
| Changed pain | False confidence that a saved copy equals a tested restore. | Context debt: a decision survives as a label after its rationale and reopen conditions disappear. |
| Hook | “A backup is not the same thing as getting your work back.” | “If future-you cannot tell why, the decision is not finished.” |
| Visual metaphor | Split `FILE EXISTS` / `RESTORE TEST` card. | Bold `DECISION` card with missing context fields revealed behind it. |
| Proof surface | Copy identity, version, scope, destination, restore attempt, opened result, scope match, result, limit. | `DECISION / WHY / SOURCE / ALTERNATIVES / ASSUMPTION / SCOPE / CONFIDENCE / REOPEN IF / STATUS`. |
| Emotional turn | Relief through testing the return. | Relief through leaving a compact revisit trail. |
| Final line | “Keep the copy. Test the return.” | “Leave future-you a reason, not just a result.” |

The angle is not a rename of `decision-basis-state`, `decision-rationale-state`, `record-purpose-state`, `context-state`, `source-note`, `review-question-state`, or `restore-boundary-state`. Those occupied concepts address the basis, rationale, purpose, context, source, question, or restore boundary separately. This angle specifically asks whether a future reader can reconstruct when a recorded choice still applies and what would cause it to reopen.

## Dramatic engine before camera

**Subject:** A fictional decision card for one pattern-project choice, represented by paper and a neutral file tile.  
**Desire:** Return to an old choice and understand it without reconstructing the entire project from memory.  
**Obstacle:** The label survives while the source, alternatives, assumptions, scope, confidence, and reopen condition are missing.  
**Stakes:** A designer may repeat a stale choice, misread a prior compromise, or reopen the wrong human gate; the video must not imply that this has happened to a Stitch & Scale user.  
**Change:** Move from “decision recorded” to “decision revisitable.”  
**Contradiction:** The shortest record may be easiest to file and hardest to trust later.  
**Escalation:** A bold result card appears; blank fields reveal missing context; the founder refuses to call the decision complete; a small future-reader trail is proposed as a tester question.  
**Final image:** A filled card with `DECISION`, `WHY`, `SOURCE`, `SCOPE`, and `REOPEN IF`, plus `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Treatment

Open on a clean desk with a bold paper card: `DECISION: KEEP SIZE M SAMPLE`. The narrator says, “If future-you cannot tell why, the decision is not finished.” A pencil lifts the result card to reveal blank fields behind it: `WHY`, `SOURCE`, `ALTERNATIVES`, `ASSUMPTION`, `SCOPE`, `CONFIDENCE`, and `REOPEN IF`. The paper is not a real pattern record and never shows a real number or product label. A second card asks, “Would you make the same choice now?” The narrator distinguishes a decision note from technical editing, test knitting, chart readability, schematic scale, physical print review, and publication approval. The ending presents a compact context trail and asks founding testers to show what the current product can actually preserve.

## Visual grammar and production bible

Use one narrator family, one tabletop, one paper system, one charcoal/cream/ink-blue palette, and a strict type hierarchy. The only readable words are the fictional labels listed in this brief, with the fixed footer `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Do not render real pattern numbers, chart symbols, schematic measurements, customer records, invoices, or UI controls. Generated text is not UI truth. Use only a current approved capture for product numbers, labels, warnings, and controls after proof review.

The primary location is a quiet desk with no private documents. Use a locked overhead camera plus one 45-degree macro angle. Keep all cards within a high-contrast central area. Use paper thickness, pencil motion, and a lifted card as the visual rhythm. No digital glitch, fake loading, fake save, fake database, or effect may imply product behavior. The arrangement is source note at left, decision card in center, context fields behind, and tester invitation at right.
