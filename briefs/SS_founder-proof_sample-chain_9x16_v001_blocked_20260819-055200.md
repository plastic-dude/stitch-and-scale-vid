## Research decision

Fresh research on Fashion for Good’s transparency-and-traceability topic and an academic sample-management system for fashion product development changed the concept from a broad supply-chain story to **small-workshop sample custody**. The academic source describes information capture and status support for iterative fabric-sample selection, but it is an enterprise-oriented 2009 RFID case and does not prove Stitch & Scale, independent-designer workflows, or RFID. The local craft-business workflow separately identifies sample location and return status as operational records.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect a current sample or handoff ledger surface; it does not promise that a sample is safe, traceable, delivered, returned, or automatically tracked.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | A physical sample leaves a small studio with a pattern identity and a promised return path. |
| Desire | To know which sample moved, who has it, where it is, and when it should return. |
| Obstacle | Physical handoffs are easy to remember vaguely and hard to reconstruct when the sample is mixed with other work. |
| Stakes | A designer may lose a sample, confuse versions, miss a return, or treat a test-knit handoff as a release decision. |
| Change | The viewer moves from “I sent the sweater” to “I recorded the sample’s identity and handoff state.” |
| Contradiction | A small record can protect a large amount of human work without pretending to provide logistics automation. |
| Escalation | Sample leaves → identity is questioned → custodian/location/return cards appear → current proof or blocker → human confirms handoff. |
| Final image | Sample envelope, pattern identity, custodian, location, and return status align, or a blocker card if proof is absent. |

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `recovery-proof`, which concerns a digital file leaving a workspace and whether it can be safely restored. `sample-chain` changes the pain to **a physical sample losing identity and return context during handoff**; the hook to **“The sample moved. Did its story move with it?”**; the visual metaphor from a digital bundle leaving and returning to a tagged garment/sample envelope moving between custodians; the proof surface from export/backup/restore to one current sample or handoff-ledger surface; the emotional turn from quiet protection through digital recoverability to tactile care for physical work; and the final line to **“Keep the sample. Keep the handoff.”**

It is distinct from `measurement-moment` because it concerns physical custody, not measurement semantics; from `event-window` because the date is not the obstacle; from `decision-ledger` because it is not financial state classification; and from `false-completion` / `release-gate-roster` because it is not a generic release gate or roster. It does not reopen, overwrite, rename, or consume the imported reservations.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on an original sample envelope or clean garment prop with a fictional identifier such as `SAMPLE A-01`, permanently labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. A hand moves it from `STUDIO` to `CUSTODIAN` to `LOCATION` to `RETURN STATUS`. These are illustrative physical cards, not current product controls. Do not show a real person’s name, address, shipment label, customer information, private submission, or copyrighted pattern page.

The product insert is optional and blocked until a current, versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact sample, handoff, custodian, location, or return labels. If absent, retain `SAMPLE-HANDOFF PROOF REQUIRED — NOT PRODUCT UI`. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Use one narrator family. Captions must not cover the sample ID, cards, hands, proof, controls, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.

## Rights, captions, audio, and platform plan

Use original sample envelope, clean garment or swatch prop, fictional ID, paper cards, device fixture, hands, and table. No shipping labels, real names, addresses, customer information, private submission details, copyrighted patterns, event logos, or external tracking UI. Any human custodian/model requires permission. David’s voice/image requires authorization. Audio must be original or licensed.

Use one narrator family. Sound motif: fabric movement, paper label, a soft pencil mark, and room tone; no delivery-success chime, loss alarm, or logistics promise. Human-review captions sentence by sentence and provide caption-on and caption-free versions. Captions may not cover sample ID, cards, hands, proof, controls, or CTA. Platform requirements remain validation gates.
