## Research-informed premise

Craft Yarn Council guidance says a submitted swatch or sample should carry the designer’s contact information and that a postage-paid envelope should accompany a submission when return is requested. It also describes project labels, notes, yarn labels, sample size, finishing, gauge, instructions, schematics, invoices, and return shipping as distinct submission concerns. [1] [2] Knitty’s June 17, 2026 submission guide supplies a current seasonal contrast: its Winter 2026 deadline is September 15, 2026, but its photography workflow says the sample never leaves the designer’s possession. Different destinations therefore create different custody modes; “sent” cannot safely stand for every workflow. [3]

The research note records category evidence from Asana and Notion documentation but makes no competitor superiority claim. Generic task, status, owner, URL, and timestamp fields do not by themselves prove a physical sample’s receipt, condition, return, or current holder. [4] [5] [6]

> **A sample being sent is not the same as a sample being accounted for.**

## Founder and product truth

David is a developer whose late mother knitted. He does not knit and must not be described as a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, technical editor, or tester. He may explain why a physical record needs visible custody states without claiming personal sample-making authority.

No current product-proof registry entry or approved, non-private, rights-cleared capture verifies that Stitch & Scale exposes a sample-custody, return, condition, current-holder, shipment, receipt, tracking, notification, or sample-location surface. Every proposed card below is a fictional prop and must be labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI` in the production bible and on any visible frame.

Do not claim that Stitch & Scale tracks parcels, knows where a sample is, confirms delivery, contacts a recipient, records condition, manages returns, sends reminders, protects samples, guarantees recovery, or replaces a publisher’s instructions. Do not show courier branding, real tracking numbers, names, addresses, barcodes, customer information, private garments, or manufacturer records.

## Treatment and beats

The film opens on a neutral tabletop with an original parcel prop and a single `SENT` mark. A hand adds the label, then pauses when the record reveals missing receipt, custody, return, and condition fields. The narrator distinguishes destinations that retain a sample from workflows in which the designer keeps it. The story ends with a bounded open state and an honest tester invitation, not a claim that the product already exposes the surface.

## Visual grammar and production bible

Use one quiet neutral tabletop, soft side light, restrained camera movement, and high-contrast original cards. The parcel must be a generic prop with no courier logo, barcode, address, tracking number, or realistic label. Use one hand actor only; David may appear in a neutral medium close-up or voice-only frame, but no handwork performance may imply he knits.

Every text card that describes a proposed record must display `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Never use a fake browser frame, fake app chrome, generated navigation, generated button, realistic customer data, or a fabricated Stitch & Scale screen. Keep one field family per close shot. Dense records must be split into readable inserts: identity, custody mode, receipt, review/return, and condition/open reason.

The production bible must preserve the same parcel orientation, card order, hand direction, desk surface, wardrobe, narrator family, room tone, and light temperature across all takes. Prop text is locked before any generation would be authorized. Use only original cards and neutral paper; no real pattern PDF, garment, sample, customer record, or publisher instruction.

### Alternatives

If a parcel prop is unavailable, use a plain envelope or a neutral sample card. If a hand actor is unavailable, use stop-motion cards with no implied product UI. If David’s talking-head recording is unavailable, use one approved founder voice over neutral tabletop footage. If proof is supplied later, replace only the fictional record with the exact current capture after re-review; do not infer labels or state transitions from the concept.

## Sound motif and audio plan

Use one approved narrator family with a close, dry voice and consistent room tone. The sound motif is a single paper-label press followed by a restrained paper stop when `RECEIVED_AT` remains blank. No music is required. If a paper sound is retained, caption it only when it conveys meaning, for example `[paper stop]`; do not let it compete with narration.

Record clean room tone before and after each setup. Reject mixed voices, doubled words, overlaps, accent shifts, room-tone jumps, clipped consonants, stale UI sounds, and effects that conceal the words **receipt**, **return**, **condition**, **holder**, or **open**. Produce caption-on and caption-free review versions. Automatic captions may be a draft only; a human must verify names, field labels, punctuation, timing, line breaks, and meaningful sound cues.

## Acceptance gates and blocker

The package cannot move beyond **blocked** until the product owner supplies a current, versioned, non-private, rights-cleared proof-registry entry or approved capture for the exact sample-custody surface. It must identify `SAMPLE S-001`, design ID, sample type, custody mode, current holder, destination, label ID, dispatch time, receipt time, review status, return requested, returned time, condition at return, unreturned reason, source/basis, reviewer, review time, and status. Preserve technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, and test knitting as distinct human evidence gates. No custody record replaces any of them. Review the actual frame at caption-on and caption-free states; reject any caption that covers faces, hands, labels, proof, numbers, controls, logos, or primary actions. Reject stale UI, unsupported claims, mixed voices, doubled words, overlaps, room-tone jumps, duplicate filenames, and prop text that could be mistaken for current product UI.
