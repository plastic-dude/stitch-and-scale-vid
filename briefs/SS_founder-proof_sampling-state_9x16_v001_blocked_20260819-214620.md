## Concept, distinctness, and research boundary

A sample can exist without representing the population being discussed. The nearest prior angles are `evidence-coverage` and `test-knit-coverage`, which concern evidence-to-claim mapping and size-roster test-knit coverage. `sampling-state` asks whether a sample’s target population, selection method, size, conditions, exclusions, observations, limits, review, and decision are explicit before a broader claim is made. Hook: “Sampled is not representative.” Fictional proof surface: `TARGET POPULATION / SAMPLE / SELECTION METHOD / SIZE / CONDITIONS / EXCLUSIONS / OBSERVATIONS / LIMITS / REVIEWED / DECISION`. Emotional turn: “We tested one” becomes “We know what this test can and cannot support.” Final line: “Name what the sample cannot speak for.” Distinct from `audit-state`, `sensitivity-state`, and generic scope concepts.

NIST’s statistical-method handbook supports the need for method and context around sampling, variation, and uncertainty. Craft Yarn Council describes quality review across design/editing/printing and the use of standards, technical editing, and tester feedback. These sources support workflow education only; they do not prove that Stitch & Scale currently exposes sampling, representativeness, or broader-claim controls.
