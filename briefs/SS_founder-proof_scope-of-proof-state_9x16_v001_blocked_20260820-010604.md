## Concept and claim boundary

> “Proof in one scope is not proof everywhere.”

A fictional proof card is initially presented as though it is universal. The card then declares `VALID_FOR`, version, size, gauge, context, audience, test conditions, exclusions, reviewer, and disposition. Everything outside the declared frame turns grey and remains unverified. The story is about a **declared proof scope**, not a product validity guarantee, NIST assessment, generalizability claim, grading or fit result, gauge calculation, or universal behavior.

NIST AI RMF 1.0 frames validity and reliability around intended goals and the design, development, use, and evaluation context of a system [906]. NIST SP 800-53A describes customizable assessment procedures that can be tailored to scope and organizational risk tolerance [907]. Craft Yarn Council explains gauge as stitches and rows per inch and notes that the specified gauge should be achieved for the intended size, with a swatch adjusted when the measured result differs [908]. These sources motivate the fictional scope card and production review only; none proves that Stitch & Scale implements the AI RMF, runs NIST assessments, or calculates, validates, grades, or guarantees gauge or fit.

All validity scopes, versions, sizes, gauges, contexts, audiences, test conditions, exclusions, reviewers, and dispositions are fictional. Generated text is not UI truth. Exact product labels, values, warnings, and controls require a current approved product capture or proof-registry entry. David is a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not described as a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, technical editor, or tester.
