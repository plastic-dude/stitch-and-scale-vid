## Research-informed premise

The Information Commissioner’s Office updated its purpose-limitation guidance on 23 March 2026. It says purposes should be specified, documented, and communicated, and that reuse should be compatible with the original purpose. It connects purpose specification with data minimization, accountability, and avoiding function creep. It also says compatibility assessment should consider context, sensitivity, consequences, and safeguards such as pseudonymization. [1]

The ICO’s data-minimization guidance describes data as adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the specified purpose, while noting that the right amount depends on the purpose and can differ across people or groups. That guidance is under review, so this package uses it as a bounded privacy design reference rather than legal advice. [2]

NIST’s Privacy Framework Core includes configurations that permit selective collection or disclosure of data elements and frames privacy as risk management rather than a single approval badge. [3] Craft Yarn Council guidance demonstrates that a craft submission may contain contact details, project numbers, yarn labels, instructions, measurements, invoices, and return-shipping information across distinct artifacts. [4]

> **The same record does not need to travel whole.**

## Dramatic engine

| Engine element | Design |
|---|---|
| Subject | One fictional full evidence card containing contact, project, tester, material, and review fields, plus three viewer windows. |
| Desire | Let a designer or reviewer make a useful handoff without exposing unrelated personal or project data. |
| Obstacle | A full-card export tries to travel unchanged to every audience. |
| Stakes | Names, contact details, private comments, submission information, or unrelated business data can move beyond the original purpose; no actual incident is claimed. |
| Change | Each audience receives a purpose-specific field set; hidden fields become `HIDDEN FOR THIS VIEW` or `ROLE LABEL`, not a compliance badge. |
| Contradiction | A smaller view can protect exposure while still being too small to support the purpose; minimization cannot mean omitting necessary context. |
| Escalation | `FULL RECORD` → `TESTER REVIEW` → `EDITOR REVIEW` → `PUBLIC HANDOFF` → `PURPOSE?` → `AUDIENCE?` → `NECESSARY FIELDS?` → `EXPOSURE REVIEW OPEN`. |
| Final image | Three views hold different field sets with explicit hidden and visible boundaries; no viewer receives a green `PRIVATE` or `COMPLIANT` stamp. |

## Treatment and beats

The film opens on a full fictional record with more fields than any viewer needs. Three windows appear. The tester sees scope and method, the editor sees finding and pattern version, and the public handoff sees role, disposition, and next action. Contact details and private comments are hidden or replaced with role labels. The final card asks for purpose, audience, and field necessity, then holds at an open exposure review.
