## Research decision

One Wild Designs’ current guide describes grading as turning one size into a multi-sized pattern, gives example bust series, and notes that its size-inclusion snapshot considers one measurement while other measurements matter. Craft Yarn Council guidance distinguishes general sizing information and finished-garment schematics from actual body-measurement charts, and states that fit and length charts are guidelines; individual changes may alter a garment’s appearance. Neither source proves universal fit, a universal size-inclusion threshold, current Stitch & Scale grading, or an outcome for an individual maker.

The research changed the concept from generic size inclusion into **range transparency plus fit boundary**. The film must not claim universal fit, size inclusivity, accuracy, or current grading capabilities.

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `measurement-moment`, which concerns preserving the meaning of a captured measurement through unit, size, body/garment context, and project identity. `size-boundary` changes the pain to **a range label being mistaken for a promise that every body will fit or that the range is universally inclusive**; the hook to **“Show the range. Name the boundary. Don’t promise the fit.”**; the visual metaphor from mobile measurement capture to a size strip opening into `BODY / FINISHED / EASE / RANGE`; the proof surface from measurement/grading entry to a current size-range, grading, schematic, or ease surface; the emotional turn from maker control through context to fit-honest transparency; and the final line to **“Show the range. Name the boundary.”**

It is distinct from `measurement-moment` because it concerns communicated coverage and fit boundaries rather than capturing a measurement; from `gauge-reality` because it is not gauge or fabric behavior; from `technique-profile` because it is not prerequisite signaling; from `data-minimum` because it is not privacy or data collection scope; and from `false-completion`, `release-gate-roster`, `correction-trace`, `care-context`, `material-notes`, `sample-chain`, `support-boundary`, `brief-before-knit`, `feedback-loop`, `permission-line`, `recovery-proof`, `decision-ledger`, `event-window`, `evidence-over-output`, `inherited-thread`, `offline-continuity`, `locale-drift`, `reader-handoff`, `chart-parity`, `equivalent-meaning`, and `readability-under-load` because the conflict is range communication versus fit promise. It does not reopen, overwrite, rename, or consume the imported reservations.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on an original fictional range strip labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI` with neutral placeholders such as `RANGE: 1–8`. The strip separates into cards reading `BODY`, `FINISHED`, `EASE`, and `RANGE`. A `FITS EVERYONE` badge is deliberately rejected and replaced by `CHECK THE SCHEMATIC`. No real measurements, body data, current size labels, pattern charts, or inclusive-size symbols appear.

The product insert is optional and blocked until a current, versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact size-range, grading, body/finished measurement, ease, schematic, or size-selection labels. If absent, retain `SIZE PROOF REQUIRED — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Any real size table, pattern page, schematic, model, body measurement, or size-inclusion mark requires source and rights review. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Use one narrator family. Captions must not cover range strips, measurements, proof, hands, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.
