The nearest prior is pass 247 `derived-freshness-state`. Pass 247 asked whether a downstream value is still current after an upstream source changes. Pass 248 asks whether a checked sample legitimately represents the supported size range.

| Dimension | Pass 247 `derived-freshness-state` | Pass 248 `size-coverage-state` |

## Dramatic engine before camera

**Subject:** A fictional three-card size-range strip with one central card and two boundary cards.  
**Desire:** Know what evidence exists across the supported range before treating a pattern as covered.  
**Obstacle:** The central size has a visible check while boundary sizes and alternate representations remain unresolved.  
**Stakes:** A designer may mistake one checked sample for evidence about every supported size; the video must not claim this happened to a Stitch & Scale user.  
**Change:** Move from “one sample passed” to “sample, scope, result, and limit are explicit.”  
**Contradiction:** A green check in the middle can be more misleading than an honest unresolved edge.  
**Escalation:** The central card is checked; boundary cards remain neutral; the scope field narrows; the limit field prevents overclaiming; the narrator names the human gate.  
**Final image:** `CENTER SIZE: CHECKED` beside `BOUNDARY A: UNRESOLVED` and `BOUNDARY B: UNRESOLVED`, with `SAMPLE / SCOPE / RESULT / LIMIT` and `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Treatment

Open on a clean desk with three paper cards reading `SIZE A`, `SIZE M`, and `SIZE Z`. The narrator says, “The middle size passed. What about the edges?” A pencil marks only `SIZE M` with `SAMPLE CHECKED`. The camera cuts closer as the two boundary cards reveal `NOT REVIEWED`. A second strip states `SAMPLE / SCOPE / RESULT / LIMIT`. The narrator explains that one checked sample is not evidence for every size. The card then separates technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, and test knitting into distinct human gates. The founder turns the paper away and says he will not pretend it is the app. The final card asks founding testers what the current product can actually show about supported-size coverage.

## Visual grammar and production bible

Use one narrator family, one tabletop, one paper system, one charcoal/cream/ink-blue palette, and a strict type hierarchy. The only readable words are the fictional labels listed in this brief, with the fixed footer `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Do not render real stitch counts, row counts, yardage, body measurements, fit outcomes, charts, schematic measurements, customer records, or UI controls. Generated text is not UI truth. Any future product number, label, warning, or control must come from a current approved capture.

The primary location is a quiet desk with no private documents. Use one locked overhead camera and one 45-degree macro angle. Keep all three cards within a high-contrast central area. Use a single central check, two unresolved edges, and a scope strip as the visual rhythm. No digital glitch, fake loading, fake save, fake database, or effect may imply product behavior. The arrangement is boundary card at left, central card in the middle, boundary card at right, and scope fields below.
