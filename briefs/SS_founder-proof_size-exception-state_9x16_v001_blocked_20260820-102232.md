## Core creative distinction

The nearest prior is pass 248 `size-coverage-state`. That concept asked whether every listed size had evidence coverage. Pass 256 asks what happens **inside a covered range** when the default grading rule stops explaining an affected body point or construction relationship.

| Dimension | Pass 248 `size-coverage-state` | Pass 256 `size-exception-state` |
|---|---|---|
| Changed pain | A size can appear in a range without evidence that it was covered. | A covered range can hide a breakpoint where the default rule no longer explains the fit or construction. |
| Hook | “Every size needs evidence.” | “A size range can be complete while one rule quietly stops making sense.” |
| Visual metaphor | Coverage roster or evidence matrix. | A paper grading ruler reaches a marked breakpoint and flips from `DEFAULT RULE` to `EXCEPTION: HUMAN REVIEW`. |
| Proof surface | Size, evidence source, coverage state, human size review, status. | Size, body point, default rule, exception, affected pieces, why, maker note, technical edit, test knit, status. |
| Emotional turn | A complete-looking range becomes accountable to evidence. | A smooth-looking rule admits its edge instead of pretending to be universal. |
| Final line | “Show me the size nobody checked.” | “Show me where the rule stops—and who reviews the exception.” |

The concept is not a noun-swapped duplicate of `size-boundary`, `grade-transparency`, `fit-criteria-state`, `gauge-state`, `decision-conditions-state`, `substitution-authorization-state`, `derived-freshness-state`, `exception-state`, `decision-ledger`, `false-completion`, or `release-gate-roster`. It focuses on an explicit grading breakpoint and affected construction relationships, not on a size roster, generic exception, business number, or release gate.

## Dramatic engine before camera

**Subject:** A fictional paper `SIZE EXCEPTION` card and a narrow grading ruler.
**Desire:** Make the point where a default rule stops being self-explanatory visible to a designer and reviewer.
**Obstacle:** A polished size range can hide a design-specific change in body point, ease, length, sleeve, neckline, armhole, or shaping relationship.
**Stakes:** A maker may follow a rule that was not intended for the edge case. The film must not claim this happened to a Stitch & Scale user.
**Change:** Move from a smooth default line to a labeled exception with affected pieces and separate human review gates.
**Contradiction:** More sizes do not automatically mean more explanation.
**Escalation:** Default rule appears; ruler reaches breakpoint; exception appears; affected pieces appear; technical edit and test knit remain separate; status becomes hold.
**Final image:** `DEFAULT RULE → BREAKPOINT → EXCEPTION / HUMAN REVIEW` with `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Treatment

Open on a quiet cream desk. A paper card titled `SIZE EXCEPTION` sits beside a narrow ruler marked only with abstract ticks, not numbers. The narrator says, “A size range can be complete while one rule quietly stops making sense.” The hand writes `BODY POINT: SLEEVE / ARMHOLE` and `DEFAULT RULE: SAME GRADE`. The ruler reaches a blue breakpoint. The card changes to `EXCEPTION: REVIEW REQUIRED` and adds `AFFECTED PIECES: SLEEVE + ARMHOLE`, `WHY: RELATIONSHIP CHANGES`, and `MAKER NOTE: EXPLAIN BEFORE USE`. A second strip adds `TECHNICAL EDIT: HUMAN REVIEW` and `TEST KNIT: SEPARATE GATE`. The narrator refuses a green approval badge: “More sizes do not automatically mean more explanation.” The final card reads `STATUS: HOLD`, then `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. The founder invitation asks founding testers what evidence a product should preserve at a grading breakpoint; it does not claim a finished grading feature.

## Visual grammar and production bible

Use one narrator family, one quiet tabletop, one cream/charcoal/ink-blue paper system, one ruler, and a strict type hierarchy. The only readable words are the exact illustrative labels in this brief plus the fixed footer `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Do not render real size numbers, body measurements, stitch counts, row counts, grade increments, pattern text, chart symbols, schematic measurements, screenshots, tester names, customer data, product controls, or approval badges. Generated text is not UI truth.

Use one locked overhead camera and one 45-degree macro angle. Keep the ruler and exception card in a central high-contrast area, with a caption-safe band above and platform-control buffer below. Show one field family per close shot rather than a dense grading table. The breakpoint must be legible without captions. Do not use a fake calculator, automatic grading animation, fit guarantee, standards badge, competitor screen, or “corrected” number.
