## Research decision

W3C guidance says prerecorded synchronized media needs captions unless it is a clearly labeled media alternative for text, and says captions include dialogue, speaker identification, and meaningful sound information.[183] W3C also says prerecorded audio-only content needs an equivalent time-based alternative and notes parallel presentation can help cognitive, language, and learning needs.[184] Craft Yarn Council pattern guidance shows that rows, abbreviations, repeats, and notation carry distinct meanings.[185] These sources support a semantic spoken checklist, but do not prove Stitch & Scale provides audio, TTS, transcript generation, or accessible pattern reading.

## Concept and dramatic engine

| Engine element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | A fictional spoken-instruction checklist separates context from action. |
| Desire | To hear or caption an instruction without losing its row, side, size, number, unit, or repeat meaning. |
| Obstacle | Speech cannot point at punctuation, and a bare action can sound identical across contexts. |
| Stakes | A listener may apply a correct action to the wrong row, side, size, or unit; no product outcome is claimed. |
| Change | The fictional checklist adds context before action and preserves equivalent text. |
| Contradiction | The shorter spoken instruction can be the less usable one. |
| Escalation | Bare action → row/round → side/size → number/unit → action/repeat → equivalent text/proof. |
| Final image | `CONTEXT SPOKEN — TEXT PRESERVED`. |

## Difference from nearest prior angle

The nearest prior angle is `hands-busy`, which focuses on what survives while a maker’s hands are occupied. `spoken-index` changes the pain to **the semantic fields and ordering required for a spoken instruction to remain auditable, plus the equivalent text that must remain available**. The hook changes to **“A spoken instruction cannot point at the punctuation.”** The visual metaphor changes from occupied hands to **`ROW/ROUND → SIDE → SIZE → NUMBER → UNIT → ACTION → REPEAT`**. The proof surface changes from hand-state workflow to a current spoken-instruction or audio-alternative surface with equivalent text and speaker/voice labeling. The emotional turn changes from physical convenience to semantic confidence without claiming product audio. The final line changes to **“Say the context before the action.”**

It is distinct from `caption-clearance` because it is semantic completeness and equivalent text rather than caption placement; from `direction-cue` because it is spoken context rather than visual traversal; from `repeat-scope` because it includes the full spoken field order and accessible alternative rather than punctuation scope; and from imported `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster` because it is not completion or evidence-roster accountability. Neither imported concept is reopened, renamed, overwritten, or approved.

## Treatment and production bible

Open on a neutral tabletop with an original abstract card marked `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. A fictional short line reads `K2, P2`, then disappears. The remaining cards enter: `ROW/ROUND`, `SIDE`, `SIZE`, `NUMBER`, `UNIT`, `ACTION`, and `REPEAT`. A second card reads `EQUIVALENT TEXT AVAILABLE`. The final card reads `CONTEXT SPOKEN — TEXT PRESERVED`.

A real Stitch & Scale spoken-instruction, audio-alternative, transcript, or equivalent-text surface may replace the fictional card only if a current, approved, non-private capture or proof-registry entry verifies the exact surface, labels, product version/date, and rights to show it. If proof is absent, retain the blocker card and disclaimer. No TTS, spoken pattern mode, audio export, audio navigation, accessible pattern reading, or WCAG conformance claim is permitted.

Use one narrator family. David may appear only with authorization and remains described as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Captions must not cover semantic cards, proof, numbers, tables, buttons, menus, logos, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free versions. Generated text remains illustrative and cannot substitute for current product proof.

## Caption map and audio plan

Use one narrator family with close, dry narration. Sound motif: a soft click precedes each context card, and a page turn lands on `EQUIVALENT TEXT AVAILABLE`. No TTS-success chime, accessibility-pass stamp, or “understood” cue. Check for voice drift, doubled words, overlaps, accent changes, inconsistent mic distance, and room-tone jumps.

Captions are narration-verbatim, sentence by sentence, inside a declared safe zone that never covers semantic cards, proof, numbers, tables, or CTA. Keep one field family on screen at a time for mobile reading. Retain caption-on and caption-free review masters and an editable transcript if audio is ever approved. Automatic captions are not accepted as final. TikTok and Instagram/Reels controls and overlays remain approval-time validation gates.

## Locations, arrangements, and visual grammar

Use one quiet tabletop location with diffuse light, high-contrast abstract cards, a neutral phone mockup, and a hand entering only to place the context cards. Avoid real pattern instructions, customer recordings, personal data, screenshots, projects, and identifiable workspace content. Arrange `ACTION → ROW/ROUND → SIDE → SIZE/NUMBER/UNIT → ACTION/REPEAT → EQUIVALENT TEXT` as a vertical sequence; use close-ups for one semantic family at a time. Keep all generated text visibly fictional and never style it as current UI.
