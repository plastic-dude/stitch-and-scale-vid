## Research decision

CYC says company and magazine requirements vary, generally asks for a sketch and swatch, advises submitting to one recipient at a time, notes that accepted work may be expected to be exclusive, describes confirmation/return/review states, and says a contract may follow acceptance.[292] CYC also warns about submission and recordkeeping pitfalls.[293] The U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes fixed original work, ownership, licenses, transfers, material objects, and recordation.[294] [295] Stitch & Scale’s contracts keep lifecycle, rights, publication, technical editing, and records separate.[296] [297] These sources support a typed submission checklist, but do not prove product or legal behavior.

## Difference from nearest prior angle

The nearest prior angle is `deadline-truth`, which focuses on the type and basis of a date. `submission-state` changes the pain to **whether a sent project is being mistaken for accepted, contracted, published, or rights-transferred work**. The hook changes to **“Submitted is not accepted.”** The visual metaphor changes from typed dates to **`PROJECT / RECIPIENT / SUBMISSION DATE / REVIEW STATE / RESPONSE DATE / RIGHTS STATUS / CONTRACT STATUS / PUBLICATION STATUS / SOURCE / NOTES`**. The proof surface changes from a deadline-context surface to a current submission-context surface. The emotional turn changes from clarifying a date’s obligation to separating a handoff event from an editorial, contractual, publication, or rights outcome. The final line changes to **“Label the state before you call it accepted.”**

It is distinct from `credit-line` because it does not identify a public attribution; from `artifact-status` because it does not classify a file’s condition; from `deadline-truth` because it is about submission lifecycle rather than date basis; and from imported `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster` because it is not a completion or evidence-roster concept. Neither imported concept is reopened, renamed, overwritten, or approved.
