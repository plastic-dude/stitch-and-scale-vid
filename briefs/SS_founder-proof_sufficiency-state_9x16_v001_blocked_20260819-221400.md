## Concept and claim boundary

> “Relevant is not sufficient.”

One fictional decision card asks whether to proceed with a craft change. A relevant evidence card enters, but the required-versus-present checklist shows missing condition and review fields. The threshold remains unmet and the disposition is `HOLD`. The ending is a bounded minimum-evidence lesson, not a product promise, threshold recommendation, fit claim, or approval. The closing line is **“Name what is still missing before you decide.”**

This package does not claim that Stitch & Scale exposes a minimum-evidence gate, sufficiency threshold, completeness score, risk engine, authorization flow, coverage analyzer, or automated decision. Generated text is not UI truth. Exact product labels, controls, warnings, numbers, and behavior require a current approved product capture or proof-registry entry.

David may be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted. He must not be described as a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, technical editor, tester, or as using an unverified feature.

## Visual grammar and coverage plan

Use a neutral evidence board, one decision card, a required-evidence checklist beside present-evidence tiles, a bold but calm missing-fields row, amber for incomplete coverage, red only for a threshold not met, and no green approval state. No real UI, private records, recognizable people, copyrighted patterns, customer data, branded property, legal language, product claim, or fit promise. Captions occupy a fixed lane that never covers decision, required evidence, present evidence, missing fields, coverage, conditions, review, limits, threshold, tables, numbers, logos, buttons, menus, hands, or primary action.

Coverage: locked overhead master; medium decision-card push-in; close readable proof holds for required/present comparison; missing row insert; coverage/limit insert; threshold/owner/disposition hold; caption-free clean master; caption-on review master; accessibility review frame. Alternative coverage is a static checklist comparison if motion reduces legibility. Reject stale UI, caption collisions, mixed voices, doubled words, overlaps, room-tone jumps, unreadable proof, and unsupported claims rather than hiding defects with effects.
