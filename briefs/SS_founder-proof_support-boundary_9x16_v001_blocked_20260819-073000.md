## Research decision

Craft Industry Alliance’s current guidance on precise pattern writing emphasizes thoroughness, not assuming knowledge, concise and clear wording, consistency, maker-centered placement, and enlisting help because an author cannot see every mistake in their own work. GoSadi’s current FAQ separates pattern testing, clear instructions, schematics, measurements, difficulty, publishing, and its own platform role; its business and platform claims were not transferred to Stitch & Scale. Neither source proves a current Stitch & Scale support channel, response time, technical-editor access, or issue-resolution workflow.

The research changed the concept from a generic help promise into **question routing and ownership clarity**. The film must not claim response time, support availability, technical-editor access, pattern correctness, issue resolution, or customer satisfaction.

## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the checked repository state. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment remains the working objective through the free demo and early-access cohort. The film invites designers to inspect one verified support-routing, issue-category, or help-context surface; it does not promise a support inbox, response SLA, helpdesk, or resolution outcome.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | A maker’s question arrives without an obvious owner. |
| Desire | To put the question where the person or process that can examine it is clear. |
| Obstacle | Pattern clarification, test-knit feedback, technical editing, and product issues can look similar at first glance. |
| Stakes | A question sent to the wrong owner can stall, repeat, or lose its useful context. |
| Change | The viewer moves from “send it somewhere” to “name the question type and next owner.” |
| Contradiction | Routing is not deflection; it is a way to preserve context and avoid claiming the wrong kind of help. |
| Escalation | Question card appears → four possible owners compete → context tags separate it → `NEXT OWNER?` remains open → current proof or blocker. |
| Final image | One question card sits beside `PATTERN / TEST KNIT / TECH EDIT / PRODUCT` and a visible `NEXT OWNER?` state. |

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `feedback-loop`, which concerns what happens after an observation is received and whether a response and recheck path is visible. `support-boundary` changes the pain to **a maker’s question entering the wrong queue because distinct kinds of help are treated as one thing**; the hook to **“The fastest way to lose a question is to give it the wrong owner.”**; the visual metaphor from response states to one question card splitting into `PATTERN / TEST KNIT / TECH EDIT / PRODUCT`; the proof surface from feedback/issue response to a current support-routing, issue-category, or help-context surface; the emotional turn from response accountability to responsible ownership without deflection; and the final line to **“Name the question. Name the next owner.”**

It is distinct from `feedback-loop` because it is pre-response classification rather than response state; from `brief-before-knit` because it is question handling after an issue arises, not participation scope; from `reader-handoff` because it is routing ownership rather than a reading transfer; from `technique-profile` because it is not prerequisite signaling; and from `false-completion`, `release-gate-roster`, `correction-trace`, `care-context`, `material-notes`, `sample-chain`, `gauge-reality`, `event-window`, `permission-line`, `recovery-proof`, `measurement-moment`, `decision-ledger`, `evidence-over-output`, `inherited-thread`, `offline-continuity`, `locale-drift`, `chart-parity`, `equivalent-meaning`, and `readability-under-load` because the conflict is question ownership versus undifferentiated help. It does not reopen, overwrite, rename, or consume the imported reservations.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on an original question card labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. The card contains a neutral fictional prompt such as `ROW 12 LOOKS DIFFERENT — WHO OWNS THIS?`, not a real user message, pattern instruction, private detail, or product bug. Four paper tabs appear: `PATTERN`, `TEST KNIT`, `TECH EDIT`, `PRODUCT`. Each tab contains a neutral question type, not a promise of a person or service. A final card reads `NEXT OWNER?`, not `SOLVED`, `ANSWERED`, or `SLA`.

The product insert is optional and blocked until a current, versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact support-routing, issue-category, help-context, or ownership labels. If absent, retain `SUPPORT PROOF REQUIRED — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Any real user message, tester identity, technical-editor identity, or support exchange requires permission and appropriate review. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Use one narrator family. Captions must not cover question text, owner tabs, proof, hands, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.
