## Research decision

The Craft Yarn Council describes Basic, Easy, Intermediate, and Complex project levels as general explanations of techniques that may appear in pattern instructions, including stitches, increases/decreases, colorwork, shaping, and simultaneous techniques. It asks publications using its downloadable symbols to provide credit and notify CYC. Natalie in Stitches argues that broad labels are relative and subjective, that makers may choose projects for emotional reasons, and that a person’s time knitting does not map cleanly to their technique range. Neither source proves an objective difficulty scale, a user-success prediction, or a Stitch & Scale technique-profile feature.

The research changed the concept from generic difficulty labeling into **technique transparency plus maker agency**. The film must not claim beginner friendliness, ease, objective difficulty, pattern correctness, or individual success.

## Difference from prior concepts

The nearest prior angle is `readability-under-load`, which concerns dense information becoming hard to scan on a mobile screen. `technique-profile` changes the pain to **a broad project-level label hiding the techniques a maker may need**; the hook to **“A label is a starting point. The techniques are the real invitation.”**; the visual metaphor from dense scan burden to a fictional level badge opening into `SKILLS / TOOLS / SEQUENCES / PREREQUISITES`; the proof surface from readability proof to a current technique or prerequisite profile surface; the emotional turn from calm scanning to maker agency without judgment; and the final line to **“Name the work. Let the maker choose.”**

It is distinct from `gauge-reality` because it is not gauge/fabric behavior; from `brief-before-knit` and `feedback-loop` because it is not tester participation or response; from `equivalent-meaning` because it is not access-mode equivalence; from `chart-parity` because it is not chart/instruction parity; from `care-context`, `material-notes`, and `sample-chain` because it is not material care or physical custody; and from `false-completion`, `release-gate-roster`, `event-window`, `permission-line`, `recovery-proof`, `measurement-moment`, `decision-ledger`, `evidence-over-output`, `inherited-thread`, `offline-continuity`, `correction-trace`, `locale-drift`, and `reader-handoff` because the conflict is complexity signaling versus personal skill profile. It does not reopen, overwrite, rename, or consume the imported reservations.

## Treatment, visual grammar, and production bible

Open on a fictional, abstract level badge reading `LEVEL: INTERMEDIATE?`, permanently labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. The badge expands into four cards: `SKILLS`, `TOOLS`, `SEQUENCES`, `PREREQUISITES`. Use neutral placeholders such as `COLORWORK`, `SHORT ROWS`, `CHART READING`, and `NEEDLE CHANGE` only as fictional examples, not as a real pattern’s instructions. A final card reads `YOU DECIDE`, not `EASY`, `BEGINNER-FRIENDLY`, or `PASS`.

The product insert is optional and blocked until a current, versioned, non-private proof capture verifies exact level, technique, prerequisite, or project-context labels. If absent, retain `TECHNIQUE PROOF REQUIRED — NOT PRODUCT UI`. Do not use CYC downloadable symbols or logos without source credit and notification requirements being cleared; safest treatment uses original typography. David may appear only with authorization and must be described only as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Use one narrator family. Captions must not cover level, technique cards, proof, hands, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free cuts.
