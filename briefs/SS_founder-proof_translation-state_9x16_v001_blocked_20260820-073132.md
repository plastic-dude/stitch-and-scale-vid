## Research-informed premise

Knitty’s current June 17, 2026 guidance says that if a published pattern is translated, permission is up to the copyright holder and the translation should include a note that permission was granted and a link to the original English version; the creator retains copyright. Knitty also separates pattern text, images, charts, schematics, signed statements, and submission files. [1] CYC’s standards page describes voluntary guidelines covering yarn and needle labeling, patterns, charts, abbreviations, sizing, and related technical surfaces. [2] W3C explains that the language of each passage or phrase should be programmatically determinable so assistive technologies can use appropriate pronunciation and presentation rules, and distinguishes language metadata from text-processing language declarations. [3] [4]

These sources support a bounded record-design distinction: **a translated file is not simply the same artifact in another language**. It needs source identity, target locale, language metadata, units, abbreviations, linked charts/schematics, permission, reviewer, and status. The sources do not prove translation quality, semantic accuracy, legal compliance, technical correctness, chart equivalence, or any Stitch & Scale capability.

Knitty’s Winter 2026 context is seasonal research only. It must not be used as urgency, affiliation, or publication outcome. The film remains year-round and does not mention Knitty or its deadline.

## Visual grammar and production bible

Use one neutral tabletop, diffuse side light, restrained camera movement, and high-contrast original cards. The source and target cards must be abstract, with no real pattern text, chart, schematic, translation, logo, publication mark, or customer record. Every card must display `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

Keep `SOURCE_ARTIFACT_ID`, `SOURCE_VERSION`, `SOURCE_LOCALE`, `TARGET_ARTIFACT_ID`, `TARGET_LOCALE`, `LANGUAGE_OF_PARTS`, `DIRECTION`, `UNIT_SYSTEM`, `ABBREVIATION_KEY`, `CHART_REFERENCE`, `SCHEMATIC_REFERENCE`, `PERMISSION_STATUS`, `RIGHTS_BASIS`, `TRANSLATOR_OR_SOURCE`, `REVIEWER`, `TRANSLATION_STATUS`, and `RELEASE_STATUS` as separate field families. Do not show a green `TRANSLATED`, `APPROVED`, or `PUBLISHED` badge. Do not imply that a language tag proves a translation is correct or that a permission note proves publication acceptance. Generated text is not UI truth.

Lock card orientation, typography, tabletop, hand direction, narrator family, room tone, and light temperature across takes. David may appear in a neutral developer frame or remain voice-only; he must not perform knitting or be framed as a craft practitioner. Technical editing, calculation, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, care review, and test knitting remain distinct human evidence gates.

## Sound motif and audio plan

Use one approved narrator family with consistent room tone. The optional sound motif is a soft card slide from source to target, a held pause when `PERMISSION_STATUS` appears, and silence on `TRANSLATION: UNVERIFIED`. No approval chime, translation-success bed, or publication-ready sound.

Record room tone before and after each setup. Reject mixed voices, doubled words, overlaps, accent shifts, clipped consonants, room-tone jumps, stale UI sounds, and effects that conceal **source**, **locale**, **permission**, **units**, **terms**, **review**, or **status**. Deliver caption-on and caption-free review versions. Automatic captions are draft-only; a human must verify every technical term, language, locale, rights, and status word.
