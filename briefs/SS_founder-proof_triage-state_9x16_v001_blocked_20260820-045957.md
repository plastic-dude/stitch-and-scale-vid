## Research-informed premise

NIST SP 800-30 says risk assessments provide information needed to determine appropriate courses of action in response to identified risks, and it treats impact analysis, residual risk, and risk mitigation as parts of a broader risk-management process. [1] CISA’s Binding Operational Directive 26-04, published 10 June 2026, says federal agencies should focus on the highest-risk vulnerabilities rather than treat all vulnerabilities and systems equally. It uses explicit variables including asset exposure, known-exploited-vulnerability status, exploit automation, and technical impact, and requires roles, internal validation, tracking, reporting, and timelines. It also says timelines can change when facts change. This is a federal cyber directive, not a Stitch & Scale requirement or product capability. [2]

Craft Yarn Council’s designer pitfalls list identifies competing pattern problems including poor or missing measurements, incorrect sizing, gauge mismatch, missing materials or quantities, incorrect math, missing stitch counts, absent repeats or abbreviations, incomplete instructions, missing charts, and schematics not drawn to scale. It also warns against relying on memory and says designers should keep records. [3] Its submission guidance says to discuss yarn or color problems with a manufacturer or editor, reconfirm sample size, keep notes, match project gauge, and preserve distinct instructions, measurements, schematics, invoices, and return responsibilities. [4]

> **First found is not always first to fix.**

## Dramatic engine

| Engine element | Design |
|---|---|
| Subject | Five fictional finding cards: gauge mismatch, missing stitch count, measurement conflict, chart inconsistency, and schematic issue. |
| Desire | Let a designer or reviewer decide which finding deserves attention first without losing the others or inventing urgency. |
| Obstacle | All cards arrive together; the first-in stack and loudest label compete with evidence. |
| Stakes | A weakly justified order can delay a wider-scope issue, overreact to a narrow issue, or hide uncertainty; no real release consequence is claimed. |
| Change | Replace `FIRST IN` with an evidence-bound triage board that shows scope, consequence, urgency signal, dependency, uncertainty, rationale, owner, and review time. |
| Contradiction | A finding can be important without being first, and a first position can remain provisional when evidence or scope changes. |
| Escalation | `FIRST IN` → five cards collide → `SCOPE?` → `CONSEQUENCE?` → `URGENCY SIGNAL?` → `DEPENDENCY?` → `UNCERTAINTY?` → `RATIONALE?` → `ORDER: REVIEW REQUIRED`. |
| Final image | One card is provisionally first with its rationale visible; the other cards remain visible, and no `URGENT`, `SAFE`, or `APPROVED` stamp appears. |

## Treatment and beats

The film opens with five fictional finding cards arriving at once. A noisy `FIRST IN` stack is replaced by an evidence-bound board. Each card shows scope, consequence, urgency signal, dependency, uncertainty, and rationale. One card moves to `ORDER: 01 — REVIEW REQUIRED`, while the others remain visible and unclosed. The final line explains why the order exists without claiming that the product makes the decision.
