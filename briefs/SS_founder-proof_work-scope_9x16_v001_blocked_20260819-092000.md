## Campaign assumption

No active human brief was present in the refreshed repository. This run assumes founding-tester recruitment through the free demo and early-access cohort remains the campaign objective. The film is a year-round story about a clear labor request, not a test-call management or deadline promise. It invites designers to inspect one current test-call scope, required/optional task list, materials and techniques field, deadline/milestone panel, reward/terms panel, or pause/decline path if proof is supplied; it does not promise that Stitch & Scale provides any of those behaviors.

## Research decision

Yarnpond’s current system description exposes test-call details such as techniques, yarn and needle requirements, deadline, sizes, tester requirements, milestones, and rewards, and describes designer-controlled milestone dates. The page is evidence of Yarnpond’s workflow, not a universal test-knit standard or Stitch & Scale proof. The Elizabeth Smith Knits page was opened but returned no readable content, so no claim is taken from it. The craft-business and mobile-maker contracts support visible scope, status, dates, ownership, and next action. These findings support a labor-scope concept without claiming test-call management.

## Concept and dramatic engine

| Engine element | Director decision |

| Subject | A designer is about to ask another person to test a pattern. |
| Desire | To make the requested labor understandable before someone agrees. |

## Difference from nearest prior angle

The nearest prior angle is `brief-before-knit`, which focuses on whether the designer’s intention and expectations are clear before work begins. `work-scope` changes the pain to **the boundary of requested labor: what is required, what is optional, what is due, what is rewarded, and what to do if the scope no longer fits**. The hook changes to **“Before you ask for a test, name the work.”** The visual metaphor changes from a design-brief card to **`REQUIRED / OPTIONAL / MATERIALS / DEADLINE / REWARD OR TERMS / PAUSE OR ASK`**. The proof surface changes from brief/intention language to a current test-call scope, required/optional task list, materials and techniques field, deadline/milestone panel, reward/terms panel, or pause/decline path. The emotional turn changes from understanding design intent to consent through clarity. The final line changes to **“Make the ask clear enough to accept—or decline.”**

It is distinct from `brief-before-knit` because it is the labor boundary rather than design intention; from `change-notice` because it is not a changed requirement; from `event-window` because it is not a campaign time window; from `credit-line` because it is not attribution or payment confirmation; from `support-boundary` because it is not help triage; and from imported `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster` because it is not completion or roster accountability. Neither imported concept is reopened, renamed, overwritten, or approved.

## Treatment and production bible

Open on a neutral tabletop with an original fictional card marked `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. The first card reads `CAN YOU TEST THIS?` A second layer reveals empty fields. The fields fill one at a time: `REQUIRED`, `OPTIONAL`, `MATERIALS`, `DEADLINE`, `REWARD OR TERMS`, and `PAUSE OR ASK`. The final card reads `ASK CLEARLY — ACCEPT OR DECLINE`.

A real Stitch & Scale screen may replace the fictional card only if a current, approved, non-private capture or proof-registry entry verifies the exact surface, labels, product version/date, and rights to show it. If proof is absent, retain the blocker card and disclaimer. No real tester names, messages, pattern files, photos, deadlines, rewards, or private terms may appear without scoped permission. Do not imply task estimates, deadline reminders, milestone tracking, reward administration, or tester applications.

Use one narrator family. David may appear only with authorization and remains described as a developer whose late mother knitted; he is not a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. Captions must not cover hands, request cards, fields, buttons, menus, logos, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free versions. Generated text remains illustrative and cannot substitute for current product proof.
