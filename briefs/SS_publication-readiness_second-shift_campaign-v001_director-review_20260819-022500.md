## Campaign classification

This is a **product-proof / founding-tester recruitment** campaign, not a generic brand film and not a tutorial. The audience is a designer at the moment after the creative rush, when the garment exists but publication responsibility expands: sizes, instructions, records, test-knit coverage, and final release evidence. The film makes that invisible second shift visible, then shows Stitch & Scale as a local-first place to inspect the work without pretending that software replaces the designer, technical editor, or test knitter.

The working assumption is deliberate because the repository currently has no active campaign brief, open reviewer order, or current media manifest. If a human brief changes the objective to event attendance, launch, education, or partner outreach, this plan should be reclassified before generation rather than forcing the CTA into the wrong film.

## Concept: “The Pattern After the Pattern”

A designer folds away a finished sample. The room should feel finished. Then the table quietly repopulates: size notes, a gauge card, a test-knit list, a schematic, a warning, a backup file, a release checklist. The creative contradiction is that the “finished pattern” is the moment the unseen work becomes most accountable.

The film does not show an infinite feature tour. It follows one object—the finished garment—from pride to scrutiny to a calmer release decision. Stitch & Scale appears only when the physical evidence and the visible product state need a second surface. The final image returns to the garment, now beside a small, legible checklist: not “done forever,” but “ready for the next honest check.”

## Dramatic engine

The subject is one independent designer and the finished garment they have just set down. Their desire is to believe the pattern can finally leave the room. The obstacle is not a villain or a broken app; it is the accumulation of quiet responsibilities that appear after the creative work. The stakes are trust: a buyer, tester, or future knitter should not inherit an invisible unresolved decision. The change is from private relief to visible, bounded readiness. The final image is the garment beside a short evidence trail, with one deliberately unresolved item marked for a human decision rather than hidden.

The film escalates by changing the room’s sound and density: soft fabric and a chair at first; paper and pencil; a close warning; a clean, calm product surface; then silence and one honest invitation. There is no “before/after productivity” claim. The transformation is clarity, not guaranteed speed or sales.

## Treatment and visual grammar

Shoot in a real maker’s workspace with warm daylight, tactile surfaces, and restrained movement. The camera begins observational and close: hands, fabric, paper, pencil, edge of a schematic. Screen recordings are treated as evidence inserts, captured from the current build and never recreated with generated text. Use shallow focus for emotional detail and locked, square-on close-ups for numbers, warnings, and UI states. No shot should require a viewer to read a dense table at phone scale.

The frame language has one rule: **beauty earns the right to become proof**. Every atmosphere shot is followed by a legible evidence shot or an intentional emotional turn. Captions sit in a declared lower-third safe zone only when the lower third is empty; when hands, tables, or UI occupy the area, captions move to the upper safe zone or the shot changes. No caption or graphic may cover a face, hand, garment detail, table, number, button, menu, logo, or primary action.

## Production bible

| Department | Direction |
|---|---|
| Subject | One adult designer, hands and working surface prioritized; face optional, never required for proof. Cast a real person with permission to show their workspace. |
| Location | A real or convincingly owned maker workspace; no generic coworking space, no luxury craft fantasy. |
| Props | Finished knitwear sample, size cards, gauge note, pencil, notebook, phone or laptop, printed release-evidence card, optional yarn label. All written props are approved in advance. |
| Wardrobe | Neutral, tactile, no visible third-party logos, no garment that implies the subject authored a specific unverified pattern. |
| Lighting | Natural daylight or soft diffused key; preserve paper contrast and garment texture. No flashing UI or dramatic color grading that changes the truth of the screen. |
| Camera | Macro/close tactile coverage plus locked screen proof; 16:9 master framed with 9:16 extraction protection but not assumed crop-safe. Capture true vertical alternatives. |
| Motion | Slow observational push, hand-held micro-movement only where it conveys fatigue or attention. Avoid transitions that hide continuity defects. |
| Sound | One narrator family, close-mic intimacy, real paper/fabric sounds, restrained original tonal bed. No trending track dependency. |
| Typography | One caption family, high contrast, sentence case, two lines maximum where practical, safe-zone tested. Generated text never stands in for UI truth. |
| Color | Wool, paper, graphite, muted green/blue accent sampled from the verified product surface; do not fabricate brand colors not in the current build. |

## Caption map and audio plan

Captions are verbatim, reviewed against the final voice recording, and exported as a timed sidecar where supported. The caption map reserves two layout variants: lower safe zone when the frame is clear, upper safe zone when lower-third proof is present. Non-speech cues use concise brackets only when they materially help comprehension, for example `[paper folds]`; no decorative caption chatter.

The audio plan uses one narrator family only. Record a dry, intimate voice with consistent distance and no doubled lines. Build the sound bed from original or cleared elements: fabric movement, pencil, paper, one restrained tonal motif. The music remains below intelligibility and never covers the evidence moment. Review for mixed voices, word doubling, room-tone jumps, clipping, and abrupt music starts.

## Risk register

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation / stop condition |
|---|---|---|
| UI capture is stale or visually inaccurate | High | Capture from the current build after brief approval; Reviewer compares every claim to the build. |
| Captions cover hands, proof, or buttons | High | Caption-free and caption-on review at 9:16, 16:9, and 1:1; reposition or cut, never shrink into illegibility. |
| Film becomes a feature tour | High | One pain, one subject, five proof moments maximum; preserve the room’s emotional turn. |
| Human gates are implied to be automated | High | Keep “needs human review” visible; prohibit “ready” or “guaranteed” language. |
| Mixed or synthetic voice drift | High | One narrator family, dry voice reference, transcript check, full listen-through. |
| Rights ambiguity around maker or garment | High | Written releases before generation; otherwise use a controlled tabletop setup with owned props. |
| Product text is unreadable in vertical cut | Medium | True vertical capture and close proof shots; no crop-only adaptation. |
| Emotional tone becomes patronizing | Medium | Direct, specific language; no “you’re doing it wrong,” no fake urgency, no guilt. |
| Backup claim overstates durability | Medium | Say only what the current workflow and audit evidence support; avoid “never lose your work.” |
