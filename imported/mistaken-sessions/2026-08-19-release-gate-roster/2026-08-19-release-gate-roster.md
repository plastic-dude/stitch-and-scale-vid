## Creative implication

The nearest prior ledger angle is `test-knit-coverage`. The new angle is `release-gate-roster`. It changes the primary pain from “a table can list sizes nobody tested” to “a release checklist can look complete while coverage evidence is still unowned.” The hook changes from a universal-size statement to a contradiction: “The checkbox says every size. The roster says who actually made it.” The visual metaphor changes from a size table to an empty release-room roster with named size slots. The proof surface changes from a roster by graded size to a release gate showing one verified tester slot at a time. The emotional turn changes from finding a missing size to choosing accountability over a reassuring checkbox. The final line changes to: “Before the PDF goes out, show me who checked the size.”

This is a material distinction rather than noun substitution, but it remains adjacent to the existing angle. It therefore requires an explicit angle-ledger entry and must not be represented as a proven product feature until current captures confirm that the product can display the proposed proof surface.

## Research limits and next action

No source reviewed here validates Stitch & Scale’s current UI, tester-roster implementation, adoption, accuracy, pricing, or customer outcomes. No claim about those matters may be spoken or displayed. The single next action is: **the product owner or Generator must add a current product-proof registry entry and approved, versioned captures for the release-gate roster surface, including truthful labels and a note distinguishing test knitting from technical editing and other human evidence gates.**
