## One-sentence concept

A founder watches a reassuring “all sizes covered” checkbox give way to an empty evidence roster, then distinguishes test knitting from other human review gates and invites founding testers to help make the release record auditable—only if the current demo can truthfully show that roster.

## Dramatic engine

| Element | Director decision |
|---|---|
| Subject | David, a developer and honest outsider to knitting, speaking from the release decision rather than claiming craft authority. |
| Desire | To make a pattern-release decision answerable to visible human evidence. |
| Obstacle | A complete-looking checkbox or size list can conceal who checked which size and which evidence gate remains open. |
| Stakes | A designer may publish a document whose confidence exceeds its verified coverage; the film must not imply that Stitch & Scale itself has solved this without proof. |
| Change | The viewer moves from “the list looks complete” to “show the responsible evidence by size and gate.” |
| Contradiction | A finished-looking checklist can still be unfinished as evidence. |
| Escalation | Checkbox → empty roster → named evidence gates → conditional product invitation. |
| Final image | A clean, unfilled release-gate roster frame with a single verified slot highlighted only after an approved current capture is supplied; otherwise use a neutral placeholder card marked “PROOF REQUIRED,” never a fabricated UI. |

## Treatment and visual grammar

The visual language is an audit-room metaphor: quiet desk light, paper checklist, one real hand moving a single mark, then a verified product capture at readable scale. Do not use knitting-hand performance by David. Any handwork close-up must come from a rights-cleared approved asset and must not imply David is the maker. Use restrained cuts, no glitch effects, no fake app chrome, no generated product labels, and no cinematic overlays that obscure proof.

The film should begin with an actual checkbox or paper list as a tactile false reassurance. The contradiction appears when the camera holds on the blank “who checked?” field. The product proof, if available, follows as one close shot of one size/gate state at a time. The camera must not display a dense full-screen table as the only evidence. Captions are short, lower-third, and never placed over hands, proof, numbers, controls, faces, logos, or primary actions.

## Sound motif and audio plan

Use one original, rights-cleared sound bed built from a soft pencil mark and a single restrained paper turn. The sound should support the checklist contradiction, not dramatize a failure. David’s narration is one continuous approved voice family with consistent room tone and microphone distance. No overlapping voices, synthetic crowd, doubled words, or music under the critical proof sentence. Deliver a caption-free review mix and a caption-on review mix; both must be listened to from beginning to end.

Captions may use a maximum of two lines and 34 characters per line where possible. Captions must never cover the face, hands, paper fields, roster proof, numbers, labels, logos, buttons, menus, or the CTA. The caption map must be checked against the actual platform UI overlays before release.
