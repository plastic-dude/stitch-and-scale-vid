## Fresh research question

What should a future Test Knit campaign promise—and explicitly refuse to promise—when the live product shows roster coverage, tester feedback, yardage, and launch timing?

## Research synthesis

Ravelry describes pattern testers as people who knit patterns before publication and provide feedback; their finished projects can also show a design in different sizes, yarns, and colors.[1] A designer-practice review distinguishes test knitting from tech editing: testers can expose confusing instructions and validate yardage, but test knitting should not be sold as a replacement for professional technical editing.[2] A second practitioner account emphasizes that test coverage benefits from backup testers because life events and dropouts can leave a size untested.[3]

These sources are practical industry guidance rather than product documentation. They support a careful creative claim structure: “organise coverage and feedback before release” is supportable from the live surface; “every size has been validated,” “error-free,” “fit guaranteed,” or “ready to publish” is not supportable while the product shows 0 finished testers and no confirmed tester for each size.
