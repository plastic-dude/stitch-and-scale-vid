## Fresh research question

What ethical labor and disclosure rules should constrain a future Test Knit campaign so the product is not marketed as having validated sizes or unpaid labor without transparent expectations?

## Research synthesis

A practitioner account frames test knitting as real work that commonly uses the tester’s own yarn and uncompensated time, and argues that the process can become exclusionary when expensive yarn or social access is treated as a gate.[1] A terminology guide distinguishes test knitting, where the tester generally keeps the finished object and provides usability feedback, from sample knitting, where the knitter produces a promotional sample and is typically compensated.[2] A designer resource publishes explicit contracts, separates test and sample expectations, sets payment for sample knitting, and asks for agreed timelines, progress updates, privacy before release, and clear final deliverables.[3]

These are industry-practice sources, not legal advice or product documentation. They support a strict claim rule for a future campaign: the app can be shown organising tester slots, yardage, feedback, timelines, and a recruitment call; it must not imply that testers are paid, that testing is complete, that every size is validated, or that a pattern is publication-ready unless the current evidence actually says so. If a future campaign discusses testers, it should show the stated incentive/expectation and avoid glamorising unpaid work.
