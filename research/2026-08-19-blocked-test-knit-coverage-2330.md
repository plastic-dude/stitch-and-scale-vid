## Fresh research question

What can standard sizing tables and grading evidence establish, and what remains unverified until a garment or pattern is actually test-knit and checked across sizes?

## Evidence

The Craft Yarn Council says fit depends on body measurements plus ease and that sizing charts and schematics are guidelines that should be reviewed before beginning a project. It lists multiple measurement families rather than treating a single chest value as complete fit evidence. [1] Its women’s chart covers a range from XS through 5X, but a published table is still a measurement reference, not proof that every size has been physically knitted and evaluated. [2]

ASTM D5585 describes body-measurement tables as a baseline for developing patterns and garments, taking fabric, ease, movement, styling, and fit into account. The standard is therefore useful evidence for inputs and design reference, not a guarantee of finished-garment fit or test-knit coverage. [3]

## Decision

Reserve `test-knit-coverage` as a distinct future campaign: the pain is a nominal size range that looks complete while some sizes lack a real person and documented check. The hook, visual metaphor, proof surface, emotional turn, and final line must differ from `privacy-by-default`, `receipt-vs-invoice`, and the prior spreadsheet concepts. A future brief must show a verified roster or explicit missing coverage; it must never turn a sizing table, standard, or generated avatar into a claim that a size was tested.

The current privacy-by-default brief remains the sole active unpublished Generator contract. No new brief is created during this firing.
