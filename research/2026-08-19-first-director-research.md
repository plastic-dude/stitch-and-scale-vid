# First Temporary Director Wide-Research Pass

**Date:** 2026-08-19
**Research question:** How can the first Stitch & Scale promotional concept dramatize the real burden of knitwear grading without making unsupported product claims or reducing the video to a generic feature tour?

## Audience and craft-reality findings

### Sister Mountain — How to Grade Knitting Patterns Using a Spreadsheet
URL: https://www.sistermountain.com/blog/grade-knitting-patterns-spreadsheet
Accessed: 2026-08-19

The source presents grading as a barrier that can stop knitters from publishing designs because the mathematics across many sizes feels overwhelming. It describes a real spreadsheet workflow involving size charts, body measurements, ease, garment measurements, gauge, stitch counts, and row counts. It recommends keeping ease visible as its own row, using one primary unit consistently, and applying formulas across sizes. It also emphasizes that more sizes increase inclusivity but make the workflow more consequential.

**Decision impact:** The campaign should show the invisible work as a sequence of decisions rather than say that Stitch & Scale magically solves grading. The proof surface should focus on one visible change or derivation at a time, with ease and size relationships presented as inspectable concepts. The script should avoid claiming that every grading case is automated or error-free.

### Natalie in Stitches — Knitwear Design and the Power of Excel
URL: https://natalieinstitches.com/2023/10/25/knitwear-design-and-the-power-of-excel/
Last updated: February 2026; accessed 2026-08-19

The source describes “blank spreadsheet syndrome,” the difficulty of entering an unstructured spreadsheet, the importance of intention and process, and the usefulness of visualizing mathematical relationships. It recommends drawing ideas by hand, being explicit about what the numbers need to do, and talking with other people such as technical editors because fresh eyes improve design communication and verification.

**Decision impact:** The campaign should dramatize the emotional friction of opening a blank or overloaded spreadsheet and then turn toward visible structure, human review, and an invitation to test the work. The creative should not insult Excel or claim a replacement; it should show why a purpose-built, inspectable workflow may be easier to question and discuss.

### School of SweetGeorgia — Grading Knitwear for Beginners
URL: https://www.schoolofsweetgeorgia.com/courses/grading-knitwear-for-beginners/
Accessed: 2026-08-19

The course description frames knitwear grading as a route to adapt patterns across sizes and body measurements. It identifies size charts, grade ratios, wearing/design ease, master size charts, gauge, target finished measurements, and pattern instructions as distinct concepts. It reinforces that spreadsheets are a common learning and production medium.

**Decision impact:** The brief should preserve the distinction between body measurements, ease, gauge, finished measurements, and pattern instructions. It should show one transition rather than flattening all of these into a vague “grading made easy” promise.

## Chosen creative direction

The first concept should use the available `spreadsheet-11pm` territory only if the existing angle ledger confirms it has not already been produced. The core story is not “Stitch & Scale replaces Excel.” It is: a designer opens a familiar spreadsheet late at night, sees the human work buried inside columns, then moves into a purpose-built workspace where one grading decision can be inspected and questioned before publication. The emotional turn is respect for invisible labor, followed by an honest request for founding testers to check the math.

**Primary pain:** Unpaid spreadsheet labor and unstructured grading decisions consume the designer’s night.
**Proof surface:** One approved product capture showing one explicit, readable measurement/ease/derived-value relationship; no dense full-screen table as proof.
**Contradiction:** The work is creative, but the last mile is often a grey grid of decisions nobody else can audit easily.
**Truthful promise:** The video lets viewers see the decision path and invites them to test whether the workflow helps; it does not promise perfect grading or universal automation.
