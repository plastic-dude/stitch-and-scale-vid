## NASA Technical Assessment

URL: https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-7-technical-assessment/

NASA describes technical assessment as monitoring progress through periodic technical reviews and technical indicators such as measures of effectiveness, measures of performance, key performance parameters, and technical performance measures. It says status reporting shows where a project stands against a measure, while assessment analytically converts reported output into a form from which trends and variances can be understood. Assessment results feed decision analysis and possible corrective action. NASA also describes preserving historical data for trend identification and capturing assessment results, findings, recommendations, decisions, rationale, assumptions, and lessons learned.

This is systems-engineering assessment guidance, not proof of a Stitch & Scale outcome-measurement or trend-analysis feature.

## NASA Technical Risk Management

URL: https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-4-technical-risk-management/

NASA describes risk as scenarios, likelihoods, and consequences with uncertainty. It says mitigation/contingency plans are implemented when thresholds trigger them, and results/actions are monitored and reported. Residual risk or consequence impacts are considered in deciding whether mitigation continues.

This does not establish a product residual-risk or outcome-tracking capability.

## NIST Cybersecurity Framework page

URL: https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework

NIST’s current page describes the Cybersecurity Framework as helping organizations understand and improve cybersecurity risk management. It also points to CSF 2.0 outcomes and evidence-ready automation/reporting resources. These are cybersecurity governance resources, not Stitch & Scale product claims.

## Research implication

A fresh concept may show a fictional after-action record with `ACTION COMPLETED / INTENDED EFFECT / OBSERVED MEASURE / BASELINE / FOLLOW-UP PERIOD / TREND / RESIDUAL STATE / RECOMMENDATION / STATUS`. It must distinguish completion of an action from evidence that an intended effect occurred, and must not claim the product measures customer outcomes, improvement, fit, accuracy, adoption, or business performance.
