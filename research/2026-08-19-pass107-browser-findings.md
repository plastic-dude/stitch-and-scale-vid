## NIST Recommended Calibration Interval

URL: https://www.nist.gov/calibrations/recommended-calibration-interval

NIST states that it does not generally require or recommend one fixed recalibration interval for all measuring instruments, devices, or standards. Intervals depend on accuracy requirements, contract or regulatory requirements, inherent stability, and environmental factors. Where no external interval exists, NIST recommends an internal measurement-assurance program using cross-comparisons and control-chart analysis to characterize short- and long-term behavior, compare time-dependent behavior with application accuracy requirements, and refine the interval using as-submitted and post-calibration accuracy and precision data.

This supports a bounded review-window concept, not a universal expiry rule and not a Stitch & Scale reminder, calibration, or expiry feature.

## NASA life-cycle and technical reviews

URL: https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayCA.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7123_001B_&page_name=Chapter5

The accessed NASA document is explicitly marked obsolete and no longer used. Its historical guidance says lifecycle reviews are event-based and occur when entrance criteria are satisfied rather than simply by calendar milestones. It also describes periodic technical status reviews, historical decision records, action follow-up, and closure/verification of review actions.

Because the document is obsolete, it is used only as a bounded historical research lead; no current NASA requirement is claimed from it.

## Research implication

A fresh concept may show a fictional record with `REVIEW WINDOW / BASIS FOR WINDOW / LAST REVIEW / DUE BY / OVERDUE? / EXTENSION RATIONALE / NEXT ACTION / STATUS`. It must distinguish a policy-defined review window from a universal expiry date and must not claim Stitch & Scale sends reminders, calculates expiry, enforces freshness, or validates currentness.
