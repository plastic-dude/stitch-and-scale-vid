## Research synthesis and decision shift

The fresh question is: **When a knitwear measurement differs from a target, what proves whether the difference is intentional ease, allowed tolerance, measurement uncertainty, rounding, or an actual failure?** The research changes the creative from generic “error” to a bounded comparison card naming `TARGET / OBSERVED / DIFFERENCE / EASE OR TOLERANCE / UNCERTAINTY / ROUNDING / DECISION / EXPLANATION`, explicitly illustrative until product proof verifies exact labels. Candidate angle: `tolerance-state` — **“Within range is not understood.”** Visual metaphor: observed value, target, and uncertainty band meet an intentional ease/tolerance band; the decision pauses until the reason is named. Emotional turn: a pass/fail verdict becomes an explainable measurement decision. Final line: “Name the range before you call it a failure.”

The candidate is distinct from `acceptance-criteria`, which concerns explicit pass requirements; `rounding-rule`, which concerns precision changes; `uncertainty-context`, which records uncertainty context; `fit-intent`, which records intended fit; `comparison-frame`, which defines what is being compared; `value-state`, which classifies a value; `error-budget`, if present in prior work, which allocates error; `gauge-reality`, which concerns observed gauge; and `absence-state`, which concerns non-values. `tolerance-state` focuses on the decision boundary between target, observed difference, intended ease/tolerance, uncertainty, rounding, and explanation.
