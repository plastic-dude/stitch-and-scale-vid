## Research synthesis and decision shift

The fresh question is: **What proves an assigned review or craft-work task is actually available and feasible before its due time, rather than only having an owner name?** The research changes the creative from generic assignment to a capacity card naming `TASK / OWNER / REQUIRED TIME / AVAILABLE WINDOW / CONFLICT / DUE / ACCEPTED / FALLBACK`, explicitly illustrative until product proof verifies exact labels. Candidate angle: `capacity-state` — **“Assigned is not available.”** Visual metaphor: an assigned task passes through time-window and conflict checks before entering an accepted queue. Emotional turn: deadline anxiety becomes feasible commitment. Final line: “Name the capacity before you promise the date.”

The candidate is distinct from `priority-state`, which concerns why items are ranked; `acknowledgement-state`, which concerns the message-to-action lifecycle; `review-expiry`, which concerns freshness; `response-state`, which concerns planned versus enacted response; `commit-state`, which concerns save acknowledgement; and `dependency-state`, which concerns downstream impact after an upstream change. `capacity-state` focuses on **whether a named owner has a feasible window and resources to accept the task**, not how urgent the task is or whether a message was acknowledged.
