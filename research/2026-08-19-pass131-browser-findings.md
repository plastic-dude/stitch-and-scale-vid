## Research synthesis and decision shift

The fresh question is: **When a review item is marked closed, what proves it has an outcome, evidence, owner, and no open follow-up rather than being dismissed, hidden, or merely marked done?** Research changes the creative from generic completion to substantive closure: `ITEM / OUTCOME / EVIDENCE / OWNER / FOLLOW-UP / VERIFIED / IMPROVEMENT / CLOSED AT`, explicitly illustrative until product proof verifies exact labels. Candidate angle: `closure-state` — **“Closed is not resolved.”** Visual metaphor: a review item passes through outcome, evidence, follow-up, verification, improvement, and closure rather than disappearing. Emotional turn: false completion anxiety becomes accountable closure. Final line: “Name what closed before you call it resolved.”

The candidate is distinct from `outcome-proof`, which asks whether something being done actually improved the result; `acknowledgement-state`, which asks whether a message was delivered/seen/acknowledged/acted upon; `response-state`, which separates plan from enacted response; `review-expiry`, which concerns freshness; `retention-state`, which concerns retention; `false-completion`, which is an imported blocked concept; and `release-gate-roster`, which is an imported narrower successor requiring current roster proof. `closure-state` focuses on **the conditions and evidence required to close an item without hiding unresolved follow-up**, not simply whether an outcome improved or a message was acknowledged.
