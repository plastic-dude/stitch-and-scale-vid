## Provisional research synthesis

The fresh question is: **When a recorded measurement or pattern output is used for a specific craft purpose, what proves it is fit for that purpose rather than merely present, saved, or structurally valid?** Candidate angle: `fitness-state` — **“Present is not fit for purpose.”** Fictional proof surface: `INTENDED USE / INPUTS / CONSTRAINTS / OUTPUT / ACCEPTANCE TEST / LIMITS / REVIEWED / DECISION`, explicitly illustrative until current product proof verifies exact labels. Visual metaphor: one saved output approaches two different use gates; it passes only when intended use, inputs, constraints, acceptance test, and limits are named. Emotional turn: “The record exists” becomes “The record is suitable for this decision.” Final line: “Name the purpose before you trust the result.”

The candidate is provisionally distinct from `schema-state` (whether the record has a defined structure), `calibration-state` (measurement reference and conditions), `context-state` (semantic attachment of a value), `acceptance-criteria` (what a gate requires), `applicability-scope` (where a rule applies), `comparison-basis` (whether comparison is valid), `publication-readiness` (release state), and `test-knit-coverage` (human evidence coverage). It focuses on intended use and fitness decision, including inputs, constraints, output, acceptance test, limits, review, and decision—not record shape, measurement conditions, semantic context, gate criteria alone, rule applicability, comparison validity, release readiness, or test-knit coverage.

## Research limit

The research supports a bounded fitness-for-purpose workflow concept only. It does not establish product capability, sizing accuracy, fit outcome, compliance, customer results, or that any output is suitable for a user’s personal project. It does not replace calculation, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, or test knitting as separate human evidence gates.
