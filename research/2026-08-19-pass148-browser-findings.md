## Provisional research synthesis

The fresh question is: **When a craft test, review, or sample is used to support a broader claim, what proves it covers the relevant sizes, conditions, and edge cases rather than merely existing?** Candidate angle: `sampling-state` — **“Sampled is not representative.”** Fictional proof surface: `TARGET POPULATION / SAMPLE / SELECTION METHOD / SIZE / CONDITIONS / EXCLUSIONS / OBSERVATIONS / LIMITS / REVIEWED / DECISION`, explicitly illustrative until current product proof verifies exact labels. Visual metaphor: one test card sits in a field of many size/condition cards; only an explicit selection method and limits stop the sample from expanding into an unsupported population claim. Emotional turn: “We tested one” becomes “We know what this test can and cannot support.” Final line: “Name what the sample cannot speak for.”

The candidate is distinct from `evidence-coverage` (whether claims map to evidence items), `test-knit-coverage` (whether graded sizes have test-knit evidence), `audit-state` (whether a bounded audit procedure produced findings), `sensitivity-state` (downstream change from input perturbation), `scope` concepts, and `representability` candidates. It focuses on sample-to-population boundaries, selection method, sample size, conditions, exclusions, observations, limits, review, and decision—not evidence mapping, size roster completion, audit procedure, perturbation response, or generic scope.

## Research limit

The research supports a bounded sampling/representativeness workflow concept only. It does not establish product capability, sample adequacy, statistical validity, representativeness, customer outcomes, accuracy, or that any single test supports all sizes or conditions. It does not replace calculation, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, or test knitting as separate human evidence gates.
