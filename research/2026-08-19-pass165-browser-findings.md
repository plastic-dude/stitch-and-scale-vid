# Pass 165 browser findings — accountability-state

**Date/time:** 2026-08-19 22:42 Africa/Lagos  
**Angle under review:** `accountability-state` — working premise: a decision or response may have an owner or role label while still lacking an answerable action, traceable event, expected response, due point, evidence, and disposition. This is planning only; no current Stitch & Scale capability is asserted.

## Official sources reviewed

### NIST accountability glossary
URL: https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/accountability

NIST's glossary includes definitions that frame accountability as answerability to proper authority and as the property or goal that actions of an entity can be traced uniquely to that entity. The page also links accountability to responsibility assignment and after-action analysis in cited source contexts. Used only as a conceptual boundary, this supports separating a named role from a traceable, answerable action. It does not prove Stitch & Scale ownership, audit trails, approvals, legal responsibility, or product accountability.

Used only as craft workflow context, this supports a handoff card that records the action, responsible person, evidence, and disposition instead of treating a role label or instruction view as completed follow-through. It does not prove a Stitch & Scale assignment, accountability, human-gate, or success feature.

## Accountability model for planning

A fictional action card will use `FINDING_OR_DECISION / ACCOUNTABLE_ROLE / ACCOUNTABLE_PERSON / REQUIRED_ACTION / DUE_POINT / EVIDENCE_OF_ACTION / ACKNOWLEDGED_AT / RESPONSE / EXCEPTION / DISPOSITION`. Role and authority remain separate: a person may be authorized to decide without being the person answerable for a follow-up, and a named owner without an action or evidence is not accountability. The card must not imply legal liability or automated assignment.

## Distinctness decision

`accountability-state` is evaluated as distinct from `authority-state`, `role-state`, `escalation-state`, `rationale-state`, `custody-state`, `audit-state`, and imported `false-completion` / `release-gate-roster`. The angle concerns whether a bounded action is assigned to an answerable actor with a due point and evidence of response, not who can authorize, what a role is called, whether a finding was routed, why a decision followed, who handled evidence, whether an audit ran, or whether human gates are complete.
