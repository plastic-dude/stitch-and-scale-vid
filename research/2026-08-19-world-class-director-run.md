## Knitwear-publication workflow findings

Knitty’s June 17, 2026 submission guidance treats publication as a chain of proof rather than a single export: the complete submission must contain a finished pattern, coherent grading, measurements in imperial and metric, photographs, schematics, charts when required, and an originality statement. It explicitly says the math must make sense, the instructions must produce the intended design, and the work must be the designer’s own. It also distinguishes technical editing from the designer’s responsibility to do their best work. [4]

This changes the campaign choice. The strongest new story is not another “the spreadsheet is hard” or “show me the math” spot. It is a publication-readiness film about the invisible second shift after the beautiful design: sizes, notes, evidence, photos, schematic, and the final decision about whether the pattern is actually ready. The product can show current QA, release-evidence, technical-editor, and local-record surfaces without claiming that software replaces a technical editor or test knitter.

## Additional platform and product verification

Instagram’s current help page states that Reels accept aspect ratios from 1.91:1 through 9:16, require at least 30 FPS and 720-pixel resolution, and recommend a 420×654 cover image. [5] The campaign therefore reserves a true 9:16 master and a separate cover-safe frame rather than treating the feed crop as an afterthought.

The local Vite preview responds successfully on the real export route `/project/sample-crew-neck-sweater/pdf` with HTTP 200. This confirms the route exists for capture planning, but a timed-out browser session means this run does not claim visual inspection of the live page. The brief will therefore use only previously verified product surfaces and require the Generator/Reviewer to validate every actual UI capture against the current build.
