## Fresh research question

When a promotional video uses AI-generated or AI-modified creative assets, what disclosure and proof boundaries must be preserved across platforms and jurisdictions?

## External evidence

Google Ads guidance dated July 2026 says AI-generated or AI-modified image and video ad creatives may require disclosures or labels under emerging rules, and that Google’s label setting does not guarantee compliance with all legal obligations. [1] The European Commission states that Article 50 transparency obligations apply from 2 August 2026 to certain AI systems and content, and explains that providers and deployers must use appropriate marking or labelling approaches for applicable synthetic or manipulated content. [2] The Commission’s code of practice is a voluntary practical means of demonstrating compliance, while the underlying transparency obligations remain legal obligations. [3]

## Current product reconciliation

The active privacy-by-default video contract uses approved live product captures for UI truth and remains `approved-for-generation` on `origin/main`. Any generated atmospheric footage must not be mistaken for product evidence. If AI-generated or AI-modified creative is used in a future campaign, the manifest and platform matrix must record the asset’s origin, disclosure decision, and any platform-specific label requirement. No second brief is created in this firing.

## Decision

Create one precise single-flight blocker: preserve the published privacy-by-default contract as the only active Generator lane while generation and independent review remain outstanding. Reserve `disclosure-honesty` as a materially distinct future angle, not as a replacement brief. Do not claim legal compliance merely because a platform offers an AI label setting.
