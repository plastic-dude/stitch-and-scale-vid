## Fresh wide-research question

What evidence boundary should govern objective promotional claims about a pattern’s credibility, checks, score, or marketplace readiness?

## External evidence

The FTC states that advertisers and agencies must have a reasonable basis for objective express or implied advertising claims before disseminating them. If an advertisement communicates a particular level or type of support, the advertiser should possess the amount and type of substantiation the claim communicates. [1] FTC advertising guidance summarizes the same principle: advertising claims must be truthful, non-deceptive, and evidence-based. [2]

The UK ASA/CAP guidance says marketers must hold documentary evidence before publishing objective claims that consumers are likely to regard as factual. It also says testimonials alone are unlikely to substantiate objective claims, and that evidence must match the type and level of claim. [3]

## Current product reconciliation

The live Publish surface presents visible checks, warnings, a 92/100 credibility report, nine graded sizes, estimated yardage, and a generated buyer-facing credibility statement. These are useful receipts of the observed project state, but they do not automatically prove human tech-editor approval, completed test knitting, marketplace acceptance, universal fit, buyer outcomes, or final commercial performance. The warnings must remain visible and claim language must not exceed the evidence.

## Future angle decision

Reserve `credibility-with-receipts` as a materially distinct future angle: instead of saying “trust us,” show the exact checks, inputs, warnings, and source boundaries that make a claim narrower and more honest. This differs from `warning-visible-gate` by centering the evidence chain behind a claim, not merely the presence of a warning; it differs from `honest-price-model` by centering substantiation and traceability, not labor-cost modeling. No new Generator brief is created while the privacy-by-default contract remains unpublished.
