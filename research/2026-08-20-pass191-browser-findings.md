# Pass 191 Wide-Research Findings — `audience-readiness-state`

**Research date:** 2026-08-20  
**Research question:** How can a craft-production output be judged ready for an external audience or publication context without conflating internal completion, audience fit, accessibility, rights, or distribution readiness?  
**Status:** Research complete; concept package remains blocked pending current product proof.

## Synthesis and changed decision

The sources support a bounded insight: **an output can be internally complete yet not ready for the people or publication context that must use it**. User needs, accessibility, testing, consumer-facing standards, rights, destination profile, and distribution constraints should be considered as separate evidence. A truthful audience-readiness record should identify the audience context, intended use, required standards, tested access path, open limitations, rights status, destination/distribution, and disposition rather than using an internal “done” label as public approval.

## Selected concept and distinctness

- **Angle slug:** `audience-readiness-state`
- **Hook:** “Done for the team is not ready for the audience.”
- **Final line:** “Name who this output is ready for—and what is still untested.”
- **Nearest prior:** `publication-boundary-state` (pass 190).
- **Changed pain:** from bounding an exact output package before publication or handoff, to distinguishing internal package readiness from the audience, accessibility, standards, rights, and destination evidence required for an external person to use or receive it.
- **Visual metaphor:** A sealed internal folder is placed beside a public-facing sample card; the sample stays marked `NOT YET AUDIENCE-READY` until audience context, standards, access test, rights, destination, open limitations, and disposition are visible.
- **Proof surface:** `AUDIENCE A-001 / INTENDED_USER / INTENDED_USE / REQUIRED_STANDARD / ACCESS_TEST / RIGHTS / DESTINATION / OPEN_LIMITATION / DISPOSITION`.
- **Emotional turn:** confidence in internal completion becomes humility about what an external person has not yet been protected or tested against.

`audience-readiness-state` is distinct from `publication-boundary-state` (exact output, version, included components, open exclusions, approvals, rights, platform profile, disposition, transfer scope), `closure-scope-state` (what remains open around an issue-level closure), `scope-of-proof-state` (where proof applies), `discoverability-state` (finding the next action), `readability-under-load` (reading burden), `qualification-state` (evidence allowed to support a decision), and imported `false-completion` / `release-gate-roster` concepts. It focuses on external audience context and untested limitations, not internal packaging, generic completion, or a human-gate roster.
