# Pass 197 Wide Research — Authorization State

**Date:** 2026-08-20  
**Director role:** Planning only; no video generation or upload  
**Research question:** When an observed effect appears fit for an intended use, what evidence shows that the acceptance decision is authorized, bounded, reversible, and recorded by the person or role accountable for that decision?

## Decision changed by research

Pass 196 established that an observed change is not automatically fit for a named intended use. Pass 197 moves one step later in the decision chain: even when suitability evidence is available, a visible “accepted” state still does not show **who had authority to accept it, what scope the decision covers, what limitations remain, or when the decision should be revisited**. The fresh angle is therefore `authorization-state`, not another variation on effect, acceptability, approval, sign-off, or release status.

The film will teach a general evidence discipline using original illustrative cards. It will not claim that Stitch & Scale currently exposes an authorization record, role-based decision control, approval workflow, audit trail, or release authority. No current product-proof registry entry or approved capture verifies such a surface.

## Source findings

### 1. NIST Risk Management Framework — authorization is a distinct decision step

NIST’s current Risk Management Framework page, updated 2026-07-24, describes an integrated process for managing security, privacy, and cyber supply-chain risk across the system life cycle. Its sequence includes **Assess**, **Authorize**, and **Monitor** as separate steps. NIST therefore supports the planning distinction that assessment evidence and an authorization decision are not the same event. It does not establish that Stitch & Scale uses this framework or that a craft workflow requires a security authorization model. [1]

**Decision impact:** The dramatic engine should show a completed evidence packet beside an unused authorization card. The evidence may support a decision, but it does not itself identify the decision owner, scope, expiry or review trigger.

### 2. GOV.UK Design System — check answers before an irreversible submission

The GOV.UK “Check answers” pattern recommends a single review page immediately before confirmation for small to medium transactions. It says the page helps users see that sections are complete, gives them a second chance to correct errors, and makes clear that the transaction is not complete until the user confirms the information. It also recommends relevant sections, explicit action wording, and a **Change** link beside each section. [2]

**Decision impact:** The visual treatment will use a pre-decision review card with explicit fields and a visible “change / hold” path before any acceptance mark. The card must not imply that merely viewing a summary is authorization.

### 3. Craft Yarn Council — acceptance depends on specific submission requirements

The Craft Yarn Council’s project-submission guidance states that individual companies or publications may impose specific requirements. It separates the actual project, gauge swatch, project instructions, schematic, invoice, and return shipping, and includes details such as matching project gauge, complete measurements, readable schematics, and checking the math. [3]

**Decision impact:** The script must preserve the project’s human evidence boundaries: technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, and test knitting are distinct gates. An authorization record cannot replace any of them, and a generic “approved” stamp cannot claim those checks passed.

### 4. Craft Yarn Council — ideas are submitted to a specific recipient and may be exclusive

The Council’s “Where Do You Start?” guidance says to ask a company or magazine how it prefers ideas submitted, submit to one company or magazine at a time, and understand that acceptance may lead to a fee discussion and contract. It also notes that a submission may be returned because it does not fit the recipient’s needs. [4]

**Decision impact:** The planning surface should include **decision scope / recipient / disposition**, not just a yes/no status. The film must not invent a Stitch & Scale business submission workflow, contract, exclusivity rule, or commercial outcome; these are source-informed examples of why authority and scope can matter.

## Audience and workflow relevance

The primary audience remains independent knitwear designers and pattern publishers coordinating calculations, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, test knitting, samples, swatches, revisions, tester feedback, submissions, and intended users. The angle is useful because the person who observes or records evidence may not be the person authorized to accept a deliverable for a particular use. This is a planning hypothesis, not a measured audience finding; no current user study or product telemetry is available in the repository.

The founder truth remains unchanged: David is a developer whose late mother knitted. He does not knit and must not be described as a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, technical editor, or tester. Any on-camera explanation must remain about evidence boundaries and product-proof limits.

## Platform, accessibility, and visual opportunity

The planned output remains a 9:16, 45-second mobile-first social explainer with destination platform still unconfirmed. Dense records require dedicated close inserts rather than relying on a wide tabletop view. Captions must not cover hands, evidence cards, labels, numbers, logos, controls, menus, or the primary action. Caption-on and caption-free review versions remain mandatory. No platform-specific safe-area or caption-placement requirement is treated as verified until the destination is named and its current rules are checked.

The visual opportunity is a visible difference between **evidence complete** and **authorized for this scope**: a hand reaches for an acceptance stamp, then a scope-and-owner card reveals that authority, limitations, and review timing are still blank. This is materially distinct from pass 196’s intended-use acceptability card because it begins after suitability evidence is shown and focuses on the legitimacy and boundaries of the decision.

## Product truth, rights, and uncertainty

No current product-proof registry exists for an authorization surface, role/owner field, scope field, expiry or review trigger, decision log, or disposition control. All proposed cards are original props labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`. A paper card cannot be presented as a current product screen. No customer name, private record, testimonial, revenue, adoption, accuracy, capability, credential, urgency, final pricing, or outcome may be introduced.

Rights for original cards, neutral tabletop materials, narrator, foley, and music-free sound are unverified until a production inventory is approved. No copyrighted pattern excerpt, private message, customer data, recognizable person, logo, or product screenshot is permitted in the blocked plan.

## Research limitations

The sources are general governance, service-design, and craft-industry guidance. They do not prove that the product supports authorization workflows, that the audience experiences a specific failure, or that any particular platform will distribute the film. The Craft Yarn Council pages are industry guidance rather than a current Stitch & Scale product specification. The research supports a conceptual planning distinction only.
