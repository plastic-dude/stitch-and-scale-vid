# Pass 200 Wide Research — Reopening State

**Date:** 2026-08-20  
**Director role:** Planning only; no video generation or upload  
**Research question:** What evidence is needed to reopen a held record after correction, so that “fixed” is not mistaken for reviewed and cleared for reuse?

## Decision changed by research

Pass 199 established that an uncertain or nonconforming record can be preserved and isolated from reuse while its reason, evidence, correction path, scope, owner, and review state remain visible. Pass 200 moves to the transition out of hold: a correction action is not itself clearance. The fresh angle is `reopening-state`, focused on the evidence and accountable review needed before a held record can re-enter a defined reuse scope.

The film will not claim that Stitch & Scale currently has corrective-action plans, re-review workflows, clearance controls, reopening permissions, or release automation. It uses original neutral cards labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Source findings

### 1. NIST glossary — corrective actions are actions tied to sustaining authorization

NIST’s glossary defines a Corrective Action Plan as identifying corrective actions for removing or reducing deficiencies or risks identified during assessment, with actions that need to be performed to obtain or sustain authorization. [1]

**Decision impact:** The visual engine will separate `CORRECTION PERFORMED` from `REVIEWED AGAIN` and `CLEARED FOR SCOPE`. A filled correction card cannot be used as a clearance stamp. This is source-informed framing, not a claim that Stitch & Scale uses NIST authorization or CAP terminology.

### 2. GOV.UK Design System — preserve answers and explain how to fix the error

GOV.UK guidance says error messages should explain what went wrong and how to fix it. It recommends not clearing form fields and keeping both passing and failing answers so users can see what went wrong, edit their previous answer, and avoid re-entering information. It also emphasizes specific, clear, concise messages and a distinct route when the problem is with the service rather than user input. [2]

**Decision impact:** The treatment keeps the held record and its original reason visible while adding correction evidence. The record does not jump directly to “ready”; it moves through a clearly named review step with a defined scope.

### 3. Craft Yarn Council — acceptance requires multiple, separately evidenced components

Craft Yarn Council guidance requires distinct submission evidence including the actual project, gauge swatch, project instructions, measurements, materials, charts, and a schematic to scale. It also says to keep notes, double-check math, match project gauge, and follow the specific company or publication instructions. [3]

**Decision impact:** Reopening cannot collapse calculation, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, or test knitting into one generic correction mark. Each remains a separate human evidence gate, with status not supplied in the illustrative record.

### 4. Craft Yarn Council — problems can require discussion before proceeding

The Council advises contacting the manufacturer or editor when there is a problem with yarn or color and discussing the concern, including suggesting an alternative. It also distinguishes a project idea being accepted from later preparation and submission requirements. [3]

**Decision impact:** The final state will name a review owner and reuse scope rather than implying that a corrected file automatically reopens. No real editor, manufacturer, contract, recipient, or commercial outcome is introduced.
