# Pass 205 Wide Research — Result Boundary State

**Date:** 2026-08-20  
**Director role:** Planning only; no video generation or upload  
**Research question:** How should a criteria-fit result state its limitations so that people do not reuse it outside the tested scope or infer certainty that the evidence does not support?

## Decision changed by research

Pass 204 required intended use, criteria, test, supporting evidence, and limitations before assessing fit. Pass 205 makes the limitation operational: the record should state what the evidence supports, what it does not support, what uncertainty remains, and which uses or gates were not examined. The fresh angle is `result-boundary-state`, focused on preventing a scoped result from being silently generalized.

The film will not claim that Stitch & Scale currently records uncertainty, displays limitation fields, prevents reuse, or produces traceable results. It will use original neutral cards labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Source findings

### 1. NIST traceability — traceability alone does not guarantee fitness for purpose

NIST explains that traceability requires a documented unbroken chain of calibrations, each contributing to measurement uncertainty. It expressly states that traceability alone does not signify or guarantee fitness for purpose because uncertainty must be sufficiently small for a particular measurement need. NIST also says that results apply only to the specific instrument or standard at the time of test unless otherwise stated. [1]

**Decision impact:** The visual record will separate `EVIDENCE_CHAIN` from `INTENDED_USE`, `UNCERTAINTY_OR_LIMITS`, and `FIT_SCOPE`. A documented chain or clean record will not become universal fitness proof.

### 2. UK Government Statistical Service — communicate what users can and cannot conclude

The UK Government Statistical Service says that transparent communication of quality, uncertainty, and change helps prevent users from drawing conclusions not supported by findings. It recommends explaining limitations, uncertainty, appropriate uses, what can and cannot be concluded, and placing critical caveats prominently rather than hiding them in background notes. [2]

**Decision impact:** Limitations will be shown in the main visual record, not buried as a footnote. The final disposition will remain scoped, and the record will include an explicit `CANNOT_CONCLUDE` field.

### 3. W3C WCAG-EM — define scope, sample, evaluation, and report findings

W3C’s WCAG-EM describes a structured accessibility evaluation process: define evaluation scope and goal, explore assets, select a representative sample, evaluate the sample, and report findings. It states that Easy Checks are only a general sense of a few issues and that more thorough evaluation requires defined scope and expertise. [3]

**Decision impact:** A small check or one sample will be labeled as such. The treatment will show `SAMPLE_OR_SCOPE`, `CHECK_PERFORMED`, `FINDING`, and `NOT_EVALUATED`, without claiming WCAG conformance or overall accessibility.

### 4. Craft Yarn Council — gauge evidence is necessary but not the whole project

Craft Yarn Council says gauge is critical to project success and requires a swatch made with specified yarn, needles, and pattern stitch. It also advises reading the relevant instructions fully, performing what the pattern says, and using another reader when instructions are hard to follow. [4]

**Decision impact:** The limitations card will keep gauge, calculations, technical editing, print review, charts, schematics, and test knitting separate. A gauge result will not be represented as full pattern or project acceptance.

## Audience and workflow relevance

The primary audience remains independent knitwear designers and pattern publishers coordinating calculations, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, test knitting, samples, swatches, revisions, tester feedback, submissions, and intended users. The planning hypothesis is that a visible result may be reused beyond its tested scope when limitations are hidden or detached from the evidence. No current user study, incident report, product telemetry, or product-proof registry entry verifies this as a measured Stitch & Scale outcome.

## Research limitations

NIST traceability and measurement uncertainty are not knitwear workflow specifications. UK Government Statistical Service guidance concerns official statistics and communication of uncertainty, not Stitch & Scale product behavior. W3C WCAG-EM concerns digital accessibility evaluation and does not establish any product conformance. Craft Yarn Council guidance is industry guidance, not a product contract or measured audience study. The concept is source-informed educational framing only.
