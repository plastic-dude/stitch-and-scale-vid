# Pass 208 Wide Research — Evidence Applicability State

**Date:** 2026-08-20  
**Director role:** Planning only; no video generation or upload  
**Research question:** How should a qualified evidence item be tested for applicability to the exact claim or decision context, rather than being treated as sufficient merely because it passed a source-qualification step?

## Decision changed by research

Pass 207 qualified a source against intended use, relevance, timeliness, validity, completeness, accuracy basis, integrity, sample coverage, and method. Pass 208 adds the applicability step: the record should identify the exact claim or decision, required context, audience or user, environment, scope, and assumptions, then show whether the qualified evidence actually bears on that claim. A source can be relevant and well-preserved but still not apply to the intended claim or operational context. The fresh angle is `evidence-applicability-state`, focused on the fit between qualified evidence and the exact decision it is being asked to support.

The film will not claim that Stitch & Scale currently maps evidence to claims, validates context, supports applicability judgments, or blocks misapplied evidence. It will use original neutral cards labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Source findings

### 1. NIST — validation is for a specific intended use and defined requirements

NIST defines validation as confirmation through strong, sound, objective evidence that requirements for a specific intended use or application have been fulfilled. It also describes validation as determining whether an object or process is acceptable according to a predefined set of tests and results. [1]

**Decision impact:** The visual record will separate `EVIDENCE_ID`, `TARGET_CLAIM_OR_DECISION`, `REQUIRED_CONTEXT`, `USER_OR_AUDIENCE`, `ENVIRONMENT`, `ASSUMPTIONS`, `APPLICABILITY_TEST`, and `APPLICABILITY_STATUS`. A qualified source will not receive a universal support stamp.

### 2. GOV.UK Government Data Quality Framework — quality requirements vary by purpose and user

The Government Data Quality Framework says data quality is fitness for purpose, that the level of quality required varies by purpose, and that different users’ needs may conflict. It recommends understanding users, documenting purpose and lifecycle, and applying appropriate assessment measures rather than one-size-fits-all assurance. [2]

**Decision impact:** The treatment will make purpose, user, and operational context visible before applicability is decided. The decision card will remain `HOLD` when context or intended audience is unspecified.

### 3. W3C WCAG-EM 2 — scope, user agents, sample, and environment affect conclusions

W3C WCAG-EM 2 requires evaluators to define the target product and exact scope, select a conformance level, define the browser and assistive-technology baseline, select a representative sample, evaluate it, and report findings. It warns that evaluation of a selected subset generally does not support a conformance claim for an entire digital product, and that evaluations can become obsolete after changes. [3]

**Decision impact:** The applicability record will include target scope, sample or coverage, environment, user or assistive-technology context, and evaluation date or version. It will not claim WCAG conformance, universal accessibility, or product-wide support.

### 4. Craft Yarn Council — a test answers the question defined by its materials and instructions

Craft Yarn Council says gauge must be achieved with the specified yarn, needles, and pattern stitch for a project to fit, and recommends reading the relevant instructions fully and doing what the pattern says. [4]

**Decision impact:** The package will keep calculation, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, and test knitting as separate human evidence gates. A gauge example will not be presented as evidence applicable to every material, size, instruction, chart, schematic, or finished project.

## Audience and workflow relevance

The primary audience remains independent knitwear designers and pattern publishers coordinating calculations, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, test knitting, samples, swatches, revisions, tester feedback, submissions, and intended users. The planning hypothesis is that evidence can be qualified and still be applied to the wrong audience, environment, version, material, size, or claim. No current user study, incident report, product telemetry, or product-proof registry entry verifies this as a measured Stitch & Scale outcome.

David is a developer whose late mother knitted. He does not knit and must not be described as a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, or technical editor. The film teaches applicability boundaries and does not claim personal craft practice.

The card passes its source-quality checks but stops when the target claim, user, environment, and assumptions are missing. The emotional turn is precision: good evidence is not automatically relevant to every decision.

## Research limitations

NIST validation definitions are general assurance terminology and do not establish Stitch & Scale product behavior. GOV.UK data-quality guidance concerns public-sector data quality and does not establish an applicability feature. W3C WCAG-EM 2 concerns digital accessibility evaluation and does not establish product conformance. Craft Yarn Council guidance is industry guidance, not a product contract or measured audience study. The concept is source-informed educational framing only.
