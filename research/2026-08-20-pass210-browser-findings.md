# Pass 210 Wide Research — Decision Conditions State

**Date:** 2026-08-20  
**Director role:** Planning only; no video generation or upload  
**Research question:** How should explicit conditions constrain a decision’s validity, and when should a change in those conditions force reassessment rather than silent reuse?

## Decision changed by research

Pass 209 recorded the reasoning that connects applicable evidence to a bounded disposition. Pass 210 makes the conditions that bound that disposition explicit: identify the entity, purpose, environment, version, sample, human gate, prerequisites, limitations, and review trigger that must remain true for the decision to be reused. A decision may be reasoned and documented yet still be invalid outside its conditions. The fresh angle is `decision-conditions-state`, focused on the conditions attached to a decision, not the existence of rationale or the act of re-review itself.

The film will not claim that Stitch & Scale currently records conditional decisions, enforces prerequisites, expires dispositions, or automatically triggers reassessment. It will use original neutral cards labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Source findings

### 1. NIST — authorization can be a specific permission or an official risk decision

NIST’s authorization glossary includes permission or access granted to a system entity for a resource, verification that a requested action is approved for a specific entity, and an official management decision to authorize operation based on an agreed set of controls and explicit risk acceptance. NIST’s authorization-package glossary describes assessment results and supporting documentation as information used in a risk-based authorization decision. [1] [2]

**Decision impact:** The visual record will separate `DECISION_ID`, `AUTHORIZED_ENTITY`, `AUTHORIZED_PURPOSE`, `PREREQUISITES`, `CONDITIONS`, `LIMITATIONS`, `EXPIRY_OR_REVIEW_TRIGGER`, and `CONDITIONAL_STATUS`. It will not claim a Stitch & Scale authorization feature or legal permission.

### 2. W3C WCAG-EM 2 — scope, baseline, sample, and change affect the validity of evaluation statements

W3C WCAG-EM 2 requires explicit target scope, conformance level, browser and assistive-technology baseline, representative sample, evaluation, and reporting. It states that selected-subset evaluation generally cannot support a claim for an entire website and that evaluations performed during development can become obsolete after even minor changes. It also describes re-running evaluation after issues are repaired or periodically to monitor progress. [3]

**Decision impact:** The conditions record will keep scope, baseline, sample, date/version, and change trigger visible. It will not claim WCAG conformance, universal accessibility, product-wide coverage, or current Stitch & Scale evaluation.

### 3. Craft Yarn Council — material and instruction conditions matter to the result

Craft Yarn Council says gauge must be achieved with the specified yarn, needles, and pattern stitch for a project to fit, recommends a gauge swatch, and advises reading the relevant instructions fully and doing what the pattern says. [4]

**Decision impact:** The treatment will show material, needle, stitch, instruction, size, and human-gate conditions as separate fields. A result from one specified swatch or instruction cannot be presented as valid for all materials, sizes, charts, schematics, or project gates.

## Audience and workflow relevance

The primary audience remains independent knitwear designers and pattern publishers coordinating calculations, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, test knitting, samples, swatches, revisions, tester feedback, submissions, and intended users. The planning hypothesis is that a decision record can survive while the conditions that made it valid are forgotten or changed. No current user study, incident report, product telemetry, or product-proof registry entry verifies this as a measured Stitch & Scale outcome.

## Research limitations

NIST authorization terminology concerns system access and security-risk management and does not establish a Stitch & Scale permission or conditional-release feature. W3C WCAG-EM 2 concerns digital accessibility evaluation and does not establish product conformance. Craft Yarn Council guidance is industry guidance, not a product contract or measured audience study. The concept is source-informed educational framing only.
