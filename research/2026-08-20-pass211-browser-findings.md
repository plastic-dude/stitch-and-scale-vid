# Pass 211 Wide Research — Accountable Owner State

**Date:** 2026-08-20  
**Director role:** Planning only; no video generation or upload  
**Research question:** How should accountability for a conditional decision be assigned, maintained, and handed back when conditions change, so responsibility is not implied by a label or left with nobody?

## Decision changed by research

Pass 210 made the conditions attached to a decision explicit. Pass 211 makes the accountable role explicit: identify who is authorized to accept or reject the decision, who supplied or checked the evidence, who monitors conditions, who must be consulted for a human gate, and what happens when the named owner is unavailable or conditions change. The fresh angle is `accountable-owner-state`, focused on responsibility and handback rather than conditions alone, authorization alone, or a generic decision owner field.

The film will not claim that Stitch & Scale currently assigns accountable roles, routes decisions, escalates ownership, or maintains coverage when a person is unavailable. It will use original neutral cards labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Source findings

### 1. NIST — an authorizing official formally assumes responsibility

NIST defines an authorizing official as an official with authority to formally assume responsibility for operating a system at an acceptable level of risk. The definition distinguishes authority from mere participation in a process. [1]

**Decision impact:** The visual record will separate `DECISION_ID`, `ACCOUNTABLE_ROLE`, `AUTHORITY_OR_SCOPE`, `EVIDENCE_SUPPLIER`, `REVIEWER_OR_CHECKER`, `CONSULTED_HUMAN_GATE`, `CONDITION_MONITOR`, `HAND_BACK_TRIGGER`, `COVERAGE_OR_DELEGATE`, and `DISPOSITION`. It will not claim that Stitch & Scale exposes an authorizing official, role-based access, or a delegation feature.

### 2. ICO — accountability requires responsibility, demonstrable records, and ongoing review

The ICO’s accountability and governance guidance states that accountability means being responsible for compliance and being able to demonstrate it. It recommends keeping evidence of steps taken, maintaining documentation, reviewing and updating measures, and recording what was done and why. The guidance notes that accountability obligations are ongoing and that measures should be updated when they are no longer fit for purpose or when practices change. The page carries an explicit notice that it is under review because of legislative changes, so it is used as current governance guidance with that limitation recorded. [2]

**Decision impact:** The treatment will show responsibility as an ongoing record with role, evidence, reason, review interval or trigger, and handback path. It will not make legal or compliance claims about Stitch & Scale.

### 3. W3C — comprehensive evaluation needs diverse expertise and collaborative responsibility

W3C says effective accessibility evaluation requires diverse expertise and perspectives, may use collaborative evaluation processes, and should communicate results clearly. It describes the value of shared tools and templates, involving users with disabilities, and continuing review rather than leaving accessibility oversight to one office or one kind of expertise. [3]

**Decision impact:** The owner surface will keep reviewer, consulted human gate, and user or specialist input distinct. It will not claim accessibility conformance, complete coverage, or a product review by disabled users.

### 4. Craft Yarn Council — responsibilities are distributed across designer, editor, manufacturer, and recipient requirements

Craft Yarn Council guidance tells designers to reconfirm yarn and color with an editor or design director, check dye lots, reconfirm sample size, keep notes, contact the manufacturer or editor when a yarn or color problem appears, and comply with the specific submission company’s requirements. It also keeps the actual project, gauge swatch, instructions, schematic, measurements, charts, technical editing, and mathematical checks distinct. [4]

**Decision impact:** The treatment will show that an accountable role does not replace the editor, manufacturer, tester, technical editor, physical print reviewer, chart reviewer, schematic reviewer, or test knitter. It will use generic roles only and will not claim any current Stitch & Scale roster or assignment capability.

## Audience and workflow relevance

The primary audience remains independent knitwear designers and pattern publishers coordinating calculations, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, test knitting, samples, swatches, revisions, tester feedback, submissions, and intended users. The planning hypothesis is that records can name a decision without making clear who can accept it, who checked the evidence, who must be consulted, who maintains the conditions, or who takes over after a change. ## Research limitations

NIST authorizing-official terminology concerns system-risk responsibility and does not establish a Stitch & Scale role or delegation feature. ICO guidance is governance and privacy guidance, carries a current under-review notice, and does not establish Stitch & Scale legal compliance. W3C accessibility guidance concerns evaluation expertise and collaboration and does not establish product conformance. Craft Yarn Council guidance is industry guidance, not a product contract or measured audience study. The concept is source-informed educational framing only.
