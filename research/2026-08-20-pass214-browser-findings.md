# Wide Research Findings — Pass 214 — `substitution-authorization-state`

**Research date:** 2026-08-20  
**Research question:** When a knitwear project uses a yarn other than the named yarn, what must remain explicit about material fit, gauge, fiber structure, dye lot, availability, editor or publisher confirmation, disclosure, and test evidence before the substitution can travel into a pattern or submission?  
**Decision status:** Research complete; planning package remains **blocked** pending current product proof, rights, destination platform, and Director gates.

## Decision changed by research

Select **`substitution-authorization-state`** as the fresh angle. The pain is not merely whether a substitute yarn can reach a stated gauge, and it is not the existing `substitution-basis` question. The changed pain is that a substitute can look technically plausible while its **fit evidence, confirmation or permission, disclosure, dye-lot record, availability check, swatch result, and review status** remain unrecorded or silently assumed.

The treatment must keep four questions distinct: **Can it physically behave for this design? Has the relevant editor, design director, publisher, or owner confirmed the substitution where confirmation is required? Has the substitution been disclosed at the correct pattern or submission boundary? What swatch, test, or review evidence supports the statement?** No one card may collapse those questions into an “approved” badge.

This is workflow education, not a product demonstration, material recommendation, calculation result, or technical-editing claim. It does not establish that Stitch & Scale currently compares yarns, verifies gauge, detects substitutions, records permissions, or authorizes materials.

## Audience and craft pain

A designer, editor, publisher, or intended user may treat “same gauge” as the end of a substitution decision. The sources show why that is incomplete: category gauge ranges are guidelines; fiber content and structure can change fabric behavior; row gauge and pattern construction matter; a swatch must be made and compared; and an editor or design director may need to confirm yarn and color selection. Availability, dye lot, sample size, pattern disclosure, and rights or permission also sit on different tracks.

That separation is valuable for a quiet founder-proof explainer because it names a real workflow hazard without pretending the product solves it. The visual treatment should show a substitute card moving through **FIT EVIDENCE**, **CONFIRMATION**, **DISCLOSURE**, and **REVIEW** lanes. A physical swatch can support a fit question; it cannot silently prove permission. An editor confirmation can authorize a choice for one scope; it does not replace test knitting or technical editing. A pattern note can disclose an alternate; it does not prove the alternate behaves identically.

## Distinctness decision

| Nearby occupied territory | What it already asks | What `substitution-authorization-state` changes |
|---|---|---|
| `substitution-basis` | The basis or criteria for evaluating a substitute | Whether fit evidence, confirmation, disclosure, and review status are separately recorded before the substitute travels |
| `gauge-reality` / `gauge-state` | Material behavior versus category shorthand or gauge state | No new gauge claim; the focus is on authorization and disclosure boundaries around a proposed substitute |
| `material-trace` | Where a material came from and how it travels | The authority and evidence state of using a different material for a named design or submission |
| `price-basis` / `record-estimate-state` | Price inputs or record versus estimate status | No financial or estimate state; the focus is substitution permission, disclosure, and review evidence |
| `accountable-owner-state` | Who owns or checks a conditional decision | No general decision roster; the substitution-specific confirmation, scope, and evidence fields are the subject |
| `cross-reference-state` | Reader route from source to target | No pointer path; the issue is whether a substitute’s fit, confirmation, disclosure, and evidence are distinguished |

## Visual opportunity and dramatic engine seed

Open with a substitute yarn card stamped `SAME GAUGE?` trying to enter a green `APPROVED` lane. Four amber questions stop it: `FIT EVIDENCE`, `CONFIRMATION SCOPE`, `DISCLOSURE LOCATION`, and `REVIEW RECORD`. A large swatch card supports one fit observation; a confirmation card names a role and scope; a pattern-note card records where the alternate is disclosed; a reviewer card remains separate. The final image shows `STATUS: PENDING HUMAN REVIEW`, not a product approval.

The hook is: **“A substitute can fit the gauge and still need a record.”** The emotional turn is from convenient certainty to responsible clarity: a material choice needs evidence, scope, disclosure, and the right human review. The final line is: **“Name what fits, who confirmed it, where it is disclosed, and what remains to review.”**

## Platform and production findings

Plan a 45-second 9:16 master only as a blocked assumption. Use one narrator family, caption-on and caption-free reviews, close inserts for one card at a time, and a destination-specific safe area that protects swatch observations, yarn labels, permissions, pattern notes, hands, and the final line. No real yarn label, private contract, current publisher submission, company logo, customer record, copyrighted pattern, or current product UI may appear without rights clearance. No platform destination is confirmed; platform rules and safe-zone behavior must be rechecked after the destination is named.

The film must not claim that Stitch & Scale recommends a substitute, verifies compatibility, checks dye lots, confirms availability, obtains permission, validates gauge, detects disclosure gaps, or replaces an editor, technical editor, calculation, physical print review, chart readability review, schematic-scale check, or test knit. It must not claim identical fit, drape, durability, care behavior, or outcome. Avoid real prices, revenue, adoption, accuracy, testimonials, credentials, urgency, or capabilities.
