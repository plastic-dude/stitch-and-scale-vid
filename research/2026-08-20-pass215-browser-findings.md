# Wide Research Findings — Pass 215 — `test-protocol-state`

**Research date:** 2026-08-20  
**Research question:** What must a knitwear test record preserve about inputs, method, conditions, scope, observer, result, uncertainty, and limits before a test result can be interpreted or repeated without being mistaken for publication clearance?  
**Decision status:** Research complete; planning package remains **blocked** pending current product proof, rights, destination platform, and Director gates.

## Decision changed by research

Select **`test-protocol-state`** as the fresh angle. The changed pain is not whether a test exists, whether a human gate is complete, or whether a result is favorable. It is whether the test result can be interpreted because its **question, scope, inputs, method, conditions, observer or tester, observation, result, uncertainty, limit, and follow-up** are visible.

The National Academies’ reproducibility framework is used only as a transferable documentation analogy. It does not make a knitting test a scientific experiment, and it does not authorize claims about product correctness. Craft Yarn Council guidance supplies the knitwear-specific evidence: sample conditions, yarn and color confirmation, dye lot, availability, notes, gauge swatch, stitch and row counts, instructions, measurements, schematic, and distinct submission responsibilities.

No current product-proof registry or approved capture verifies that Stitch & Scale exposes or validates this test-protocol surface. All proposed cards are illustrative and must be labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

## Audience and craft pain

A designer or reviewer can say “tested” and still leave a reader unable to tell what was tested, with which pattern version and size, using which materials and tools, under which method and conditions, by whom, when, and with what limitations. A favorable result without that boundary may travel farther than the evidence supports. A test record also cannot silently replace calculation, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, or test knitting; those are different gates.

This gives the campaign a quiet but useful visual opportunity: a green `PASSED` stamp appears on a fictional test card, but the camera reveals that the question, pattern version, scope, conditions, observer, uncertainty, and follow-up fields are blank. The stamp is replaced with `RESULT: OBSERVED` and `STATUS: REVIEW BOUNDARY OPEN`. The film teaches the distinction without claiming that the product runs tests.

## Distinctness decision

| Nearby occupied territory | What it already asks | What `test-protocol-state` changes |
|---|---|---|
| `test-knit-coverage` / imported `release-gate-roster` lineage | Who participated in a human gate or which coverage roster exists | What the test record actually describes and what its conditions and limits allow someone to infer |
| `outcome-proof` | Whether an action produced an intended observed effect | The protocol and boundary of the test itself, before treating a result as transferable or complete |
| `acceptance-criteria` | Whether a result meets a named criterion | The inputs, method, conditions, observer, uncertainty, and limit that make a test result interpretable |
| `replication-state` | Whether a result is repeated or reproduced | The record needed to understand one test and decide whether any later repetition addresses the same question |
| `gauge-state` / `gauge-reality` | Gauge state or material behavior versus category shorthand | The complete protocol record around a test; gauge is one field, not the whole protocol |
| `substitution-authorization-state` | Whether an alternate yarn has fit evidence, confirmation scope, disclosure, and review | No material substitution decision; the focus is protocol completeness for any bounded test |
| `record-estimate-state` | Whether a business value is recorded, estimated, derived, or assumed | No financial values; the focus is test inputs, method, conditions, result, uncertainty, and limit |

## Visual opportunity and dramatic engine seed

Open on a fictional card stamped `PASSED` beside an unmarked pattern version and size. The camera moves through the blank protocol fields: `QUESTION`, `METHOD`, `CONDITIONS`, `OBSERVER`, `UNCERTAINTY`, and `LIMIT`. A swatch or test note fills one observation field; it does not fill the others. The green stamp fades to `RESULT: OBSERVED` and `STATUS: REVIEW BOUNDARY OPEN`. The final image holds a clear separation between a test record and the distinct human gates of calculation, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, and test knitting.

The hook is: **“A test result needs the conditions that make it readable.”** The emotional turn is from the comfort of a green stamp to responsible interpretation: name the question, method, conditions, scope, result, and limit before the result travels. The final line is: **“Name what was tested, under which conditions, and what the result still cannot prove.”**
