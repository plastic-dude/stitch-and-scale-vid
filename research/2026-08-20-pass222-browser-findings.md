# Pass 222 wide research: sample-return-state

**Research date:** 2026-08-20 (Africa/Lagos)  
**Director pass:** 222  
**Campaign assumption:** No active human brief was present. This pass therefore assumes the current founding-tester recruitment campaign: an organic, proof-led 9:16 founder short for independent knitwear and knitting-pattern designers; quiet, specific, craft-respecting tone; no paid distribution assumed; standard brief tier pending proof. This is a planning assumption, not a product claim.

## Fresh research question

When a knitwear sample, swatch, or project leaves a designer’s control, what record distinguishes **prepared, labeled, shipped, received, under review, returned, condition-checked, and custody-resolved** instead of collapsing the whole event into “sent” or “done”?

The question is deliberately narrower than recent work. It concerns the physical sample’s custody and return evidence, not yarn availability, a deadline, a care instruction, a release roster, a generic handoff, or whether a task is complete.

## Research synthesis

A sample can be physically labeled and shipped while its later state remains unclear. The evidence distinguishes at least six questions: what object was prepared; what identifier traveled with it; whether the intended recipient received it; whether review occurred; whether the object was returned or retained under an explicit rule; and whether its condition or contents were checked on return. The craft sources support the need for labels, notes, submission materials, and return instructions, but they do not establish a product workflow.

The seasonal context is relevant but must stay modest. Knitty’s current Winter 2026 timeline shows that publication work is active in this period, while its sample-retention photography policy demonstrates that custody rules differ by destination. That makes **custody mode** a stronger story than “ship every sample”: a record should preserve the destination’s instruction and the designer’s intended return condition.

The category research does not justify a competitor attack. It supports only a narrow creative contrast: a generic status such as “sent” or “done” is not, on its own, a custody record. The film should not name Asana, Trello, Notion, or any other competitor, and should not claim Stitch & Scale is better or already supports the proposed fields.

## Decision

Select the new angle **`sample-return-state`**. It is materially different from the nearest recent angle, `care-instruction-state`, because it concerns the lifecycle and custody of a physical sample after preparation or dispatch, not treatment instructions. It is also distinct from `material-availability-state` (whether materials are available), `deadline-condition-state` (what a date means), `handoff-state` (a broader transfer), `request-ownership-state` (who owns a request), and the imported human-gate concepts `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster` (whether evidence gates are complete or rostered by size).

The proposed hook is: **“A sample being sent is not the same as a sample being accounted for.”** The visual metaphor is a labeled parcel whose status card changes from `PREPARED` to `SENT`, then stops at a blank `RECEIVED / RETURNED / CONDITION CHECK` strip. The final line is: **“Record where the sample is, who has it, and what happens next.”** These lines are creative language, not claims about current product UI.

The proof surface, if the product owner supplies it, must distinguish: `SAMPLE S-001 / DESIGN_ID / SAMPLE_TYPE / CUSTODY_MODE / CURRENT_HOLDER / DESTINATION / LABEL_ID / DISPATCHED_AT / RECEIVED_AT / REVIEW_STATUS / RETURN_REQUESTED / RETURNED_AT / CONDITION_AT_RETURN / UNRETURNED_REASON / SOURCE_OR_BASIS / REVIEWER / REVIEWED_AT / STATUS`. Until the exact surface is verified, all on-screen cards must be fictional props clearly labeled as illustrative and the Generator must not act.
