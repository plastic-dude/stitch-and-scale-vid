# Pass 223 wide research: cost-allocation-state

**Research date:** 2026-08-20 (Africa/Lagos)  
**Director pass:** 223  
**Campaign assumption:** No active human brief was present. This pass assumes the current founding-tester recruitment campaign: an organic, proof-led 9:16 founder short for independent knitwear designers and craft-business operators; quiet, specific, craft-respecting tone; no paid distribution assumed; standard brief tier pending proof. This is a planning assumption, not a product claim.

> **Financial and tax boundary:** This research is record-design and creative planning, not accounting or tax advice. It does not recommend a deduction, allocation method, financial decision, or filing position. A qualified accountant or tax professional must review any consequential use.

## Fresh research question

When one real expense benefits more than one design, sample, channel, or business activity, what record keeps the original transaction intact while showing the allocation basis, share, evidence, reviewer, and status instead of turning a derived allocation into a settled fact?

The question is deliberately distinct from `quantity-basis` (what amount of material is required or used), `price-basis` (which economic category a price-like number belongs to), `record-estimate-state` (recorded versus estimated values), and `payment-state` (money movement). It focuses on **shared-cost attribution**, not advice about tax treatment or profitability.

## Research synthesis

A shared expense begins as one recorded transaction. A derived allocation can then distribute some or all of its benefit across designs or activities, but that distribution needs a declared basis, documented share, supporting evidence, and review status. The evidence supports the distinction between the source transaction and an allocation record; it does not support a universal formula or a current Stitch & Scale feature claim.

The craft context makes the pain concrete without inventing outcomes. A sample, photography session, tech-editing invoice, or shipping cost can sit near more than one design or publication activity. The creative treatment should not assert that any category is shared in a particular business. It should show the record question: **what was bought, which designs benefited, how was the share determined, and is that allocation reviewed or still estimated?**

The seasonal context is publication preparation around Knitty’s Winter 2026 deadline, but the film must not create urgency, imply a partnership, or state that any designer must allocate costs before that date. The category research does not justify a competitor attack. It supports only a narrow contrast between project assignment and allocation-basis documentation.

## Decision

Select the new angle **`cost-allocation-state`**. It is materially different from the nearest recent angle, `sample-return-state`, because it concerns the attribution of a recorded shared expense across benefiting designs or activities, not the custody of a physical sample. It is also distinct from `quantity-basis`, `price-basis`, `record-estimate-state`, `payment-state`, `material-trace`, `project-context-state`, and the imported human-gate concepts.

The proposed hook is: **“One receipt can serve more than one design. The split still needs a basis.”** The visual metaphor is one fictional receipt card feeding two design cards; the arrows stop until `ALLOCATION BASIS`, `SHARE`, `EVIDENCE`, and `REVIEW STATUS` appear. The final line is: **“Keep the receipt. Name the basis. Label the split.”** These are creative record-design lines, not claims about current product UI.

The proof surface, if the product owner supplies it, must distinguish: `COST C-001 / ORIGINAL_TRANSACTION / DATE / VENDOR_OR_SOURCE / CATEGORY / CURRENCY / GROSS_AMOUNT / BENEFITING_DESIGN_IDS / ALLOCATION_BASIS / SHARE_OR_PERCENT / ALLOCATED_AMOUNT / SUPPORTING_EVIDENCE / REVIEWER / REVIEWED_AT / STATUS / DERIVED_FROM`. Until the exact surface is verified, every card is fictional and visibly marked `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`, with no financial or tax conclusion.
