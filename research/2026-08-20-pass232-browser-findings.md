## Fresh question

**When a pattern or project package looks complete, which individual files, records, rights statements, physical materials, and human review gates make it complete for a named destination—and which missing item should stop the handoff?**

This question is distinct from `evidence-status-state`, which asks what a status applies to and which evidence window supports it. It is also distinct from `decision-basis-state`, which asks what evidence and roles sit beneath one decision. Pass 232 asks whether the **bundle itself** contains the distinct components needed by a named submission or publication destination, without treating a single PDF, checkbox, export, or polished appearance as proof of package completeness.

## Evidence gathered

### 1. Craft-publication workflows treat the package as multiple artifacts, not one polished file

Knitty’s current submission guide was updated June 17, 2026 and solicits patterns for Winter 2026 with a September 15, 2026 deadline. It says an unestablished contributor must submit a completed design with specified elements rather than only a proposal. The guide requires the written pattern as a Word or text-only document without images, individual high-quality image files, a schematic for garments, individual chart files when needed, and a signed, dated originality and rights statement. It also asks for both Imperial and Metric measurements, current yarn where possible or an alternate if discontinued, and clear images showing relevant design features. The guide states that technical editors work on the pattern and that Knitty does not test knit patterns as part of its own process; a test knit may be an optional assurance for a designer but is not a substitute for the publication’s technical editing. These are separate submission components and separate human activities, not one generic “ready” state. [1]

Craft Yarn Council industry guidance says requirements vary by company or publication, then lists a completed project, label or hang tag, yarn labels or skein bands, a gauge swatch with stitch and row count, electronic instructions, materials and quantities by size, measurements, sizing, skill level, finishing, schematic, charts where needed, invoice or design agreement, and return shipping. It separately warns designers to reconfirm yarn/color, dye lot, manufacturer availability, sample size, and to keep notes rather than rely on memory. It says a gauge swatch must match project gauge, instructions must contain explicit materials and measurement fields, schematics must be drawn to scale and show actual knitted measurements and direction of work, and return shipping is a separate responsibility. [2]

The Craft Yarn Council’s “Where Do You Start?” guidance further says each company or magazine has its own requirements, a concept submission may be a sketch and swatch or a completed project, a submission should be made to one company or magazine at a time because accepted designs may be exclusive, and receipt or review may take weeks. This means package completeness is destination-specific, temporally bounded, and rights-sensitive; it cannot be inferred from a universal checklist. [3]

Vogue Knitting’s current design-submission page shows a materially different package shape: one page PDF per design with swatch and sketch images and a brief explanation of techniques, with contact information on each PDF and issue-specific email subject requirements. It states that submission does not guarantee publication and that the publication may hold submissions while a season’s selection is finalized. This provides a useful comparison without supporting a competitor-superiority claim: different destinations define “complete” differently. [4]

### 2. Adjacent tools demonstrate export breadth, not destination acceptance

EnvisioKnit’s user manual describes separate export paths for a full pattern document and an individual chart. Its ZIP export can contain PDF, DOCX, ODT, PNG, SVG, text, and a snapshot of the design file. It also offers print output and a preview before export. This demonstrates that an export bundle can contain multiple files and formats, but it does not prove that the bundle satisfies a publication’s requirements, that its charts are technically correct, that its measurements are right, or that physical print review, technical editing, and test knitting have occurred. [5]

Stitch Fiddle’s current download and print documentation distinguishes printable vector documents, SVG, raster images, DOCX, ODT, spreadsheets, legends, selected chart areas, and multi-page chart downloads. It notes that vector images support better print quality and that a chart can be added to a text document in another editor. This again shows an adjacent tool’s export affordances, not a publication-ready package or a Stitch & Scale capability. [6]

KnitCompanion’s current public documentation describes importing PDFs from multiple sources, adding selected pages to projects, local/cloud/Ravelry locations, offline use after sync, backups, and project packing. It also warns about duplicate files during simultaneous device sync and describes deletion and cloud behavior. These documented behaviors support a user pain around file identity, scope, and package ambiguity, but they do not establish that Stitch & Scale syncs, integrates with Ravelry, manages submissions, or guarantees backup. No competitor superiority claim is made. [7]

### 3. Platform, accessibility, caption, and audio constraints affect package planning

TikTok’s official accessibility page documents auto-generated captions, editable creator captions, language selection, text-to-speech, alternative text for photo posts, and photosensitivity warnings. It explains that auto-generated captions can be edited or removed. The production implication is to create a verbatim script, review caption-on and caption-free versions, and not rely on unreviewed automatic captions for a dense proof package. TikTok guidance does not prove that any Stitch & Scale package is accessible or that a generated video will meet every platform condition. [8]

YouTube’s official captions guidance says subtitles and captions expand access, including for deaf or hard-of-hearing viewers and viewers who speak another language, and allows captions to be added during upload or in YouTube Studio. W3C’s current media guidance defines captions as synchronized text for speech and needed non-speech audio information, explains that automatic captions are not sufficient unless confirmed fully accurate, and notes that captions may require significant editing. W3C also distinguishes captions from translated subtitles and recommends a separate description of important visual information where appropriate. [9] [10]

Meta’s official safe-zone help page was not text-extractable in the research environment and redirected to a login/localized shell, so no precise Meta safe-zone measurement is asserted here. The platform matrix will therefore use a conservative rule: keep important proof, labels, hands, faces, tables, numbers, logos, controls, menus, and the primary action outside expected platform overlays, then validate against the actual destination preview at approval time. This is an uncertainty and a deliberate non-claim, not a platform guarantee.

### 4. Current seasonal and event context

Knitty’s current page is soliciting Winter 2026 submissions, describing cold-weather garments and accessories and giving a September 15, 2026 deadline for an issue intended to go live in December 2026. The timing makes package readiness and destination-specific submission requirements relevant to the audience, but it does **not** authorize urgency language, imply a relationship with Knitty, or establish that Stitch & Scale is submitting to Knitty. The film may use a calendar or envelope as an illustrative prop only, marked as non-product and non-destination UI. [1]

### 5. Product truth, rights, and human evidence boundaries

The refreshed repository still contains no current product-proof registry entry or approved non-private capture verifying an evidence-package surface. The available research cannot prove that Stitch & Scale currently assembles submission bundles, checks missing attachments, validates destination requirements, preserves rights records, tracks package completeness, or blocks handoff. Generated text and fictional cards cannot stand in for current UI truth.

The package concept must preserve the following as separate evidence gates: mathematical calculation, recorded design/business fact, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, test knitting, image/model context, rights/permission, and destination-specific submission rules. A package checklist cannot prove the math, a technical edit, a test-knit result, a chart’s readability, a schematic’s scale, a model’s fit, or publication acceptance. David remains a developer whose late mother knitted; he must not be framed as a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, technical editor, tester, or publication authority.

## Uncertainty and limits

The research is strongest on public submission guidance and adjacent export documentation. It does not establish the current product surface, the current campaign landing page, any private design records, customer or tester outcomes, legal advice, tax treatment, publication acceptance, or the requirements of an unspecified destination. The Knitty and CYC pages describe their own or general industry workflows and must not be presented as universal law. Vogue Knitting demonstrates a different submission format but does not prove a competitor weakness. The Meta safe-zone page could not be read beyond its login shell; precise safe-zone measurements remain unverified. Platform rules may change and must be rechecked at approval time.

No source proves that a complete-looking package is accepted, publishable, technically correct, accessible, test-knit, or commercially successful. No source supports revenue, adoption, accuracy, pricing, testimonials, credentials, urgency, customer outcomes, or Stitch & Scale capability claims.

## Decision changed by research

Research changed the next concept from a generic “missing attachment” idea into a narrower **destination-specific evidence-package-state** angle. The film should not say “the app knows your package is complete.” Instead, it should ask: **“Complete for whom, for which destination, and with which individual evidence?”**

The selected angle is `evidence-package-state`. Its visual metaphor is a sealed envelope or export bundle that opens into individually named artifacts—pattern text, image set, schematic, chart, gauge/material record, rights statement, invoice or agreement, and return/custody note—while one missing item remains explicitly unverified. The dramatic turn is from relief at a sealed bundle to respectful pause at the missing or destination-specific component. The proof surface is a current, readable product package or attachment-context surface only if supplied; otherwise every card remains illustrative and visibly labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

The angle is materially different from `evidence-status-state`: it does not ask whether a status has a scope and evidence window; it asks whether a named destination’s required artifacts, rights, and human gates are individually present and attributable. It is also distinct from `record-purpose-state`, `export-identity-state`, `publication-boundary-state`, `sample-return-state`, `permission-line`, `selective-disclosure-state`, `correction-trace`, `reader-handoff`, and the imported `false-completion` and `release-gate-roster` concepts. It does not reopen or consume either imported filename reservation.
