## Fresh question

**When a craft workflow record contains something someone saw, something they transcribed, something they calculated, or something they inferred, how can the record keep those states separate so an interpretation is not presented as a raw observation and a raw observation is not mistaken for technical validation?**

The question is distinct from pass 241 `inspection-scope-state`, which asks what exact subject, version, sample, conditions, question, finding, and outside-scope boundary were inspected. Pass 242 focuses on **the epistemic state of the statement inside that bounded inspection**: direct observation, transcription, calculation, reviewer interpretation, or unresolved inference.

## Evidence gathered

### 1. CYC distinguishes contemporaneous notes from later transcription and changes

The Craft Yarn Council’s current pattern-writing guidance tells designers to write down what they did after each row or round rather than rely on memory. It then separates transcription, in which notes are cleaned up after the completed sample, from later standards, terminology, sizing, technical editing, and testing. It also says changes made during design should be noted. [1]

This supports a state distinction: `OBSERVED IN NOTE`, `TRANSCRIBED`, `CALCULATED`, `INTERPRETED`, and `UNRESOLVED` are not interchangeable. A typed pattern line may be a transcription of a note, not a direct observation of the finished sample; a count may be calculated from a formula, not visually observed; a technical-editor comment is an interpretation or finding, not raw project data.

### 2. Pattern work combines written, visual, numerical, and tested evidence

CYC’s overview describes written patterns, video tutorials, charts, graphs, schematics, and combinations. It emphasizes the math involved in design and pattern writing and asks testers to follow a pattern literally and report whether directions, abbreviations, symbols, charts, materials, and finished appearance line up. [1]

The evidence state should therefore travel with the object and method: `OBSERVED / WHERE`, `TRANSCRIBED FROM`, `CALCULATED BY`, `INTERPRETATION BY`, or `OPEN QUESTION`. It must not label a calculated or interpreted value as a verified craft result.

### 3. Knitty separates designer work, technical editing, test knitting, and editorial decisions

Knitty’s current June 17, 2026 guidance expects a complete submission and states that its technical editors help solve math mistakes and improve fit where possible. It does not require or recommend test knitting before submission and says test knitting does not necessarily influence selection. It distinguishes the designer’s work, technical editing, optional test knitting, editorial selection, rights, and publication. [2]

This supports a human-gate boundary: a designer’s observation, a technical editor’s interpretation, a test knitter’s experience, and a publication editor’s decision are different evidence roles. The film must not collapse them into an automated verified state.

### 4. Accessibility reporting separates review process, findings, and interpretation

W3C’s current accessibility report template says a useful report identifies the review date, scope, URLs or samples, included and excluded material, reviewers, tools and versions, manual reviews, interpretative summary, detailed results, and recommended actions. [3] WCAG-EM 2.0 describes a process of defining scope, selecting representative samples, evaluating them, and reporting findings, and warns that a selected subset generally does not support a whole-product conformance claim. [4]

This supports an observation record that preserves the method and speaker: what was seen directly, what was transcribed, what was calculated, what was interpreted, what remains open, who reviewed it, and what sample or condition limits the statement. It does not support a claim that Stitch & Scale automatically classifies epistemic states or produces conformance conclusions.

### 5. Accessibility and mobile presentation

Use explicit text labels rather than color alone: `OBSERVED`, `TRANSCRIBED`, `CALCULATED`, `INTERPRETED`, `UNRESOLVED`, `SOURCE`, `METHOD`, `REVIEWER`, and `LIMIT`. Review caption-on and caption-free versions, one narrator family, original/cleared audio, and human-verified captions. Captions must not cover observation cards, numbers, tables, proof, hands, faces, logos, controls, menus, or primary actions.

The visual opportunity is a fictional field card: `GAUGE: 20 ST / 10 CM`. It first appears as `OBSERVED: SWATCH NOTE`, then a second card shows `CALCULATED: 20 ST ÷ 10 CM`, a third shows `INTERPRETED: MAY MATCH TARGET`, and a final card shows `UNRESOLVED: NEEDS HUMAN CHECK`. The film never turns any state into automatic accuracy, fit, or publication approval.

## Uncertainty and limits

CYC, Knitty, and W3C document craft workflow and evaluation reporting, not Stitch & Scale functionality. Epistemic labels are a planning model, not a product feature or a guarantee that an observation is accurate. A direct observation can be mistaken, a transcription can omit context, a calculation can use wrong inputs, and an interpretation can be disputed. Technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, test knitting, rights, accessibility review, and publication decision remain separate human gates.

No source supports claims of automatic observation capture, transcription fidelity, formula correctness, inference detection, reviewer tracking, technical editing, gauge, fit, chart readability, schematic scale, test-knit success, accessibility conformance, rights, acceptance, publication, or user outcomes.

No source supports claims about revenue, adoption, accuracy, customer outcomes, final pricing, credentials, testimonials, urgency, or unverified product capabilities. David is a developer whose late mother knitted; he must not be presented as a knitter, knitwear designer, pattern designer, technical editor, tester, publisher, accountant, lawyer, or tax adviser.

## Decision changed by research

Research changed the concept from a generic “evidence label” into the narrower **observation-state** angle. The film will show one fictional gauge field moving from `OBSERVED: SWATCH NOTE` to `CALCULATED`, `INTERPRETED`, and `UNRESOLVED`, with source, method, reviewer, and limit visible. The final state is `EVIDENCE STATE DECLARED / HUMAN REVIEW REQUIRED`.

The selected angle is materially different from pass 241: it is not about what a reviewer inspected or what sits outside the inspection scope. It is about **what kind of statement the bounded review contains—observation, transcription, calculation, interpretation, or unresolved inference**. It does not reopen the imported `false-completion` or `release-gate-roster` reservations.
