# Pass 244 Wide Research — Restore Boundary State

**Research date:** 2026-08-20  
**Director pass:** 244  
**Campaign assumption:** No active brief was present at refresh. This pass assumes the current campaign remains founding-tester recruitment through the free demo and early-access cohort. The audience is independent knitwear designers and pattern publishers. The proposed form is a 35–45 second organic 9:16 founder-proof short for Instagram Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. Tone is first-person, quiet, specific, dry, and craft-respectful. Brief tier is a standard single-original-video planning package, blocked pending proof.

## Fresh question

**What exactly does a saved backup or export prove, and what still requires a tested restore before a designer can trust continuity?**

The question is intentionally narrower than local-first ownership, generic backup, recovery-proof, archival-state, offline-continuity, and the recent evidence-state cluster. It asks for a boundary between a record that says something was saved and evidence that a usable, correctly scoped restoration was actually completed. It does not assume Stitch & Scale has a backup, export, restore, sync, or recovery surface.

## Evidence gathered

### 1. Backup scheduling is not restoration evidence

The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency’s small-business guidance says that regular backup scheduling is insufficient by itself. It specifically recommends testing partial and full restores, writing a restoration plan, and accounting for the possibility that recovery takes longer than expected. It also warns that backups can be incomplete or damaged.[1]

CISA’s Cybersecurity Performance Goals 2.0 frames recurring testing and independent validation as ways to reduce overconfidence in controls. Those recommendations are written for organizations and cybersecurity controls, not craft software, but the evidence boundary transfers: a backup record is not the same thing as a successful restore test.[2]

### 2. Local-first ownership includes responsibility, not a guarantee

Ink & Switch’s local-first essay describes local-first software as treating the copy on the user’s device as primary and emphasizes ownership, offline use, longevity, privacy, and user control. It also explicitly says that data ownership brings responsibility for maintaining backups and other protections against data loss.[3] This is a useful strategic explanation for why local-first matters to creative professionals, but it is not evidence that a particular product implements backup, restore, synchronization, encryption, portability, or long-term preservation.

### 3. Craft pattern work contains recoverable records and human gates

The Craft Yarn Council’s pattern-writing guidance describes patterns as maps for readers and makers, with written instructions, charts, graphs, schematics, and combinations. It tells designers to record actions after each row or round, clean up notes, check standards and charts, use technical editing, and ask testers whether directions, abbreviations, symbols, charts, materials, yarn amounts, and finished appearance align.[4] The evidence supports preserving a source note and a recoverable working record, but it does not make a backup a technical edit, a test knit, a print review, or a release approval.

Knitty’s June 17, 2026 submission guidance separates designer work, technical editing, optional test knitting, editorial selection, rights, and publication. A retained file or exported package can support custody or continuity questions, but it cannot silently stand in for those distinct human gates.[5]

### 4. Competitor documentation shows feature surfaces, not restore proof

EnvisioKnit publicly documents chart drawing, automatic written-instruction generation, pattern layout, multiple export formats, and print/share workflows.[6] knitCompanion publicly documents local projects and PDFs, iCloud/Dropbox/cloud sharing, offline availability after sync, backup options, and warnings about deletion, duplicate files, and the need to synchronize before going offline.[7] These pages show that export, local copies, sync, and backup are recognizable category surfaces, but neither public page proves Stitch & Scale’s product state or a completed restore of a Stitch & Scale project. The competitive opportunity is therefore not to claim that Stitch & Scale is safer or more capable; it is to ask the tester to identify what a trustworthy continuity record would need to show.

### 5. Accessibility, captions, and platform placement constrain the proof composition

W3C’s WCAG 2.1 understanding page says prerecorded synchronized media should provide captions, including dialogue, speaker identification, and meaningful non-speech audio. It also states that captions should not obscure relevant information.[8] YouTube’s current help page explains that creators can add subtitles and captions during or after upload, and that captions broaden access to deaf or hard-of-hearing viewers and viewers who speak another language.[9]

Meta’s current safe-zone guidance defines the safe zone as the area where text and logos will not be cropped or covered by the interface. For 9:16 Stories/Reels/Feed placements, it says to keep top, bottom, and side edges free of key creative elements; it warns that taller screens can crop outside-safe-zone areas.[10] TikTok’s June 2026 in-feed specification recommends 9:16 at 540×960 or higher, caps ad captions at four displayed lines, and says safe-zone size varies with dimensions and caption length.[11] These are ad specifications, not universal organic rules, so the package adopts conservative safe zones and marks the organic-placement assumption.

### 6. Cultural, seasonal, and event context is not strong enough to drive the concept

The cultural context is the everyday stewardship of handmade work: notes, charts, samples, printed instructions, business records, and the trust passed between designer and maker. That context supports a respectful continuity story, but it does not justify speaking for every knitting tradition, geography, or community. A current August 2026 fiber-event search surfaced regional festivals and yarn crawls, including events around 21–23 August, but did not establish one event that is clearly relevant to Stitch & Scale’s founding-tester audience, geography, distribution rights, or campaign calendar. No event hook is therefore used. The visual and copy system stays evergreen, avoiding invented urgency, cultural generalization, or event attendance claims.[12]

## Audience pain and visual opportunity

The audience pain is **false confidence in continuity**: a designer may have a file that appears saved, exported, synced, or backed up while not knowing whether the intended project version, size data, chart, schematic, notes, business records, or related assets can be restored and opened in a usable state. This is a hypothesis, not a prevalence claim. The visual opportunity is a paper “RESTORE?” card beside a clearly labeled fictional record with `SAVED COPY`, `RESTORE TARGET`, `VERSION`, `SCOPE`, `CHECKED`, and `RESULT: UNRESOLVED`; it then splits the claim from the evidence by showing `FILE EXISTS` versus `RESTORE TEST` as separate gates. Every card must read `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

The dramatic contradiction is: **a saved copy can be real and still not be a restored project**. The emotional turn is not fear or a promise of safety; it is relief through a smaller honest question: “Can I open the exact thing I meant to keep?”

## Product truth and rights reconciliation

The repository contains no current product-proof registry entry or approved non-private capture for a Stitch & Scale backup, export, restore, sync, archive, file-version, or recovery surface. Therefore the proof surface is a fictional prop only. No number, label, button, status, workflow, storage destination, encryption claim, cloud claim, sync claim, or recovery capability may be presented as current product UI.

The repository’s README says full-resolution videos remain outside Git and that the planned external archive is private Google Drive; a public Drive URL is not an upload credential.[13] No external media, product capture, tester data, or third-party imagery is authorized for this pass. ## Research-derived decision

Research changed the concept from a broad “local-first ownership” message to the narrower angle `restore-boundary-state`. The hook becomes **“A backup is not the same thing as getting your work back.”** The visual metaphor becomes a split restore card rather than a completed-looking checkbox or evidence-state ladder. The proof surface becomes the boundary between `FILE EXISTS` and `RESTORE TEST`, including scope, version, and result. The emotional turn becomes honest uncertainty followed by a tester invitation to define the smallest meaningful restore check. The final line becomes **“Keep the copy. Test the return.”**

The nearest recent prior is pass 243 `review-question-state`, which asks what a reviewer should check. Pass 244 instead asks what a continuity artifact proves after a save or export and what remains unproven until a restore test. It does not define a review question, classify an observation, or bound an inspection sample. It is also distinct from occupied historical slugs such as `backup-state`, `recovery-proof`, `offline-continuity`, `archival-state`, and `retention-state`: those concern the existence, recovery, offline, archival, or retention boundary generally; this angle isolates the evidence gap between a stored copy and a verified return to a usable, correctly scoped project.

## Uncertainty and limits

CISA guidance is general small-business cybersecurity advice, not evidence about independent knitwear designers or Stitch & Scale. Ink & Switch is a design essay, not a current product audit. CYC and Knitty describe craft workflow and submission distinctions but do not establish how common backup failures are. Competitor pages document their own stated features and do not establish comparative reliability. Meta and TikTok specifications cited here are primarily advertising guidance; organic placement can differ. No user interview, analytics, conversion evidence, restore test, product capture, approved asset, proof registry, or rights clearance was available in the repository. The pass must remain blocked.

## Decision and next action

**Decision:** create a planning-only blocked package for `restore-boundary-state`; reserve a unique filename but do not generate or upload.

**Next action:** Product/Archive owner must add one current, versioned, approved, non-private proof-registry entry or rights-cleared capture for an observable Stitch & Scale continuity surface that distinguishes saved copy from tested restore, including exact labels, version/date, scope, destination, capture owner, privacy treatment, and restore result. Then rerun Director claim, asset, rights, caption, audio, platform, and acceptance validation before any Generator authorization.
