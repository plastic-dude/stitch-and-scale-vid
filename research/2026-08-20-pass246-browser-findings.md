# Pass 246 Wide Research — Labor Visibility State

**Research date:** 2026-08-20  
**Director pass:** 246  
**Fresh question:** **What part of independent knitwear design labor disappears when a pattern’s visible output is treated as the whole job, and how can a designer record that labor without turning an estimate into a proven financial outcome?**

## Campaign assumption

No active brief was present at refresh. This pass assumes the standing campaign objective remains founding-tester recruitment through the free demo and early-access cohort. The audience is independent knitwear designers and pattern publishers managing design notes, grading, charts, schematics, samples, business records, technical editing, test knitting, publication preparation, and customer-facing follow-through. The proposed form is a 35–45 second organic 9:16 founder-proof short for Instagram Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. Tone is first-person, quiet, specific, dry, craft-respectful, and suspicious of false precision. Brief tier is a standard single-original-video package, blocked pending proof.

## Evidence gathered

### 1. The visible pattern is only one part of the work chain

The Craft Yarn Council’s current pattern-writing guidance describes a pattern as a roadmap and distinguishes design, math, contemporaneous notes after each row or round, transcription, standards, technical editing, charts, schematics, testing, and finished appearance.[1] It specifically advises recording what was done immediately rather than reconstructing it later and then cleaning those notes before publication. Its tester questions cover directions, abbreviations, symbols, charts, materials, yarn quantities, and finished appearance.[1]

The TKGA current Professional Knitwear Designer Certification page divides professional preparation into design foundations, gauge, grading, yardage, spreadsheets, shaping, charting, pattern writing, business management, technical editors, test knits, layout, pricing, publishers, resubmissions, accounting, contracts, rights, customer relations, and what to do when things go wrong.[2] This is a training curriculum, not a time study or market-rate survey. It is useful evidence that the workflow contains multiple labor categories that can be invisible when only the finished pattern is shown.

Knitty’s current June 17, 2026 submission guidance requires a complete submission with separate pattern text, images, charts where needed, schematics, originality and rights statements, and clear photography. It distinguishes the designer’s work from technical editing and says test knitting is optional and separate from publication selection.[3] The source also provides publication-specific terms and an upcoming Winter 2026 deadline, but those terms must not be generalized into Stitch & Scale pricing, revenue, or campaign urgency.

### 2. The honest audience pain is not “charge more”; it is “see the work before deciding”

The audience pain is a **visibility gap**: a designer can see the finished pattern or sample while undercounting the work required to reach it, including notes, grading, verification, layout, photography, accessibility preparation, admin, customer support, and rights handling. This is a workflow hypothesis, not a prevalence claim. The research does not establish that designers are universally underpaid, that any rate is fair, or that a particular product can solve the problem.

The safe creative question is not “What should this pattern cost?” or “How much revenue will it make?” It is: **“Which work happened before this file looked finished?”** That question can be illustrated without inventing a price, an hourly rate, a customer outcome, or a product capability.

### 3. Category tools foreground output, access, and export rather than a complete labor ledger

EnvisioKnit documents chart creation, generated written instructions, layout, preview, and export formats.[4] knitCompanion documents project/PDF access, annotations, local/cloud locations, offline availability after synchronization, backup options, deletion warnings, and conflict risks.[5] These official category pages make output, access, and project handling visible. They do not establish a complete labor ledger, a pricing calculation, a business record, or a Stitch & Scale surface. The competitive opportunity is therefore a transparent accounting question about work categories, not a superiority claim.

### 4. Decision-record practice supports recording evidence without pretending to calculate value

Microsoft’s current Architecture Decision Record guidance recommends preserving context, alternatives, outcome, tradeoffs, confidence, status, rationale, consequences, and links to superseding decisions.[6] This supports a useful separation: a record can make work and assumptions visible without pretending to produce a correct financial answer. The guidance is software-architecture guidance, not craft-business evidence, so it is used only as a documentation analogy.

### 5. Accessibility and platform constraints affect the labor story

W3C’s WCAG 2.1 guidance says prerecorded synchronized media should provide captions that include dialogue, speaker identification, and meaningful non-speech audio, and says captions should not obscure relevant information.[7] YouTube documents adding subtitles and captions during or after upload.[8] Meta’s current safe-zone guidance says key text and logos should stay away from interface-covered edges, while TikTok’s June 2026 in-feed specification recommends vertical 9:16 at 540×960 or higher and says safe-zone size varies with dimensions and caption length.[9] [10]

The planned labor sequence therefore uses one category at a time, one readable card per close shot, a conservative central safe zone, one narrator family, and caption-on plus caption-free review. No dense table will be shown as a single unreadable frame. These constraints improve the video’s proof readability but do not prove product accessibility or platform acceptance.

### 6. Cultural, seasonal, and event context is real but does not authorize borrowed urgency

The cultural context is the dignity of handmade work and the often-unseen care behind a pattern that another person can follow. The framing must avoid speaking for all knitters, all textile traditions, or all independent businesses. Knitty’s current page places Winter 2026 submissions near a September 15, 2026 deadline and a December 2026 issue.[3] That provides a current seasonal publication context, but Stitch & Scale has no verified connection to Knitty, no permission to use its deadline, and no basis for an event or urgency claim. No event hook is adopted. The concept remains evergreen and uses no borrowed publication branding, schedule, payment, or rights language.

## Audience pain and visual opportunity

The visual opportunity is a fictional receipt-like work card that begins with `PATTERN FILE: READY` and then unfolds into separate rows: `DESIGN NOTES`, `GRADING`, `CHARTS + SCHEMATIC`, `TECHNICAL EDITING`, `TEST KNITTING`, `LAYOUT`, `PHOTOS`, `ACCESSIBILITY`, `ADMIN + SUPPORT`. A final card reads `VALUE IS NOT A SINGLE NUMBER` and is labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

The dramatic contradiction is: **the cleaner the final file looks, the easier it is to forget the invisible work that made it usable**. The emotional turn is from self-doubt about “small” tasks to clarity that each task is a real human input, without assigning a price or claiming a business result.

## Product truth and rights reconciliation

The repository contains no current product-proof registry entry or approved non-private capture verifying a Stitch & Scale labor ledger, time/cost surface, hidden-work breakdown, pricing calculator, test-knit compensation record, technical-editing record, photography/admin record, or business-math surface. No generated card may be shown as current product UI. No claim may be made that Stitch & Scale automatically counts labor, assigns monetary value, recommends a price, calculates profitability, tracks customer support, or proves return on effort.

No external media, pattern files, customer records, private notes, invoices, charts, schematics, or third-party imagery are approved. Rights are unverified. Any future product capture must provide product version/date, exact visible labels, capture owner, privacy treatment, destination, and rights/credit data. Illustrative categories may be written on an original paper prop, but any number, value, price, or product control must come from current approved evidence.

## Research-derived decision

Research changed the concept from a broad business-math story to the narrower angle `labor-visibility-state`: **show the work categories before asking whether the output is worth it**. The hook becomes **“A pattern file can look finished while the work is still invisible.”** The visual metaphor becomes a clean file card unfolding into a human-labor receipt rather than the future-reader context card in pass 245. The proof surface becomes `OUTPUT / WORK CATEGORY / EVIDENCE / OWNER / STATUS / COST UNKNOWN / VALUE NOT CALCULATED`. The emotional turn becomes recognition rather than pricing advice. The final line becomes **“Name the work before you name the value.”**

The nearest prior is pass 245 `revisitability-state`. Pass 245 asked whether a recorded decision remains understandable and challengeable later. Pass 246 asks which human work categories are hidden behind a finished-looking pattern output. It does not claim a price, revenue, profitability, business outcome, labor total, or automated ledger. It is not a rename of `business-math`, `price-basis`, `cost-model`, `record-context`, `revisitability-state`, `restore-boundary-state`, or any prior evidence-state concept. The slug was checked against all 256 occupied angle rows and was unoccupied before reservation.

## Uncertainty and limits

CYC, TKGA, and Knitty describe workflow and publication boundaries; they do not establish typical hours, rates, prevalence, or profitability. TKGA is a training program and its scope is not a neutral industry census. Knitty’s payment, rights, deadline, and submission rules are publication-specific and cannot be generalized. Competitor pages state their own features and do not establish comparative weakness or Stitch & Scale behavior. Microsoft’s ADR model is from software architecture rather than craft business. Platform rules cited here are primarily advertising or help guidance, and organic behavior may differ. No user interviews, analytics, product capture, proof-registry entry, cost record, financial study, usability study, or rights clearance was available.

## Decision and next action

**Decision:** create a planning-only blocked package for `labor-visibility-state`; reserve the unique filename but do not generate or upload.

**Next action:** Product owner must provide one current, versioned, approved, non-private proof-registry entry or rights-cleared capture showing the exact Stitch & Scale work-category or labor-record surface, including visible labels, ownership/status fields, any displayed values, version/date, capture owner, privacy treatment, destination, and rights/credit. Then rerun Director claim, asset, rights, caption, audio, platform, and acceptance validation before any Generator authorization.
