# Pass 248 Wide Research — Size Coverage State

**Research date:** 2026-08-20  
**Director pass:** 248  
**Fresh question:** **What does representative multi-size coverage mean, and when can a central-size pass say nothing about the boundary sizes of a graded knitwear pattern?**

## Campaign assumption

No active brief was present at refresh. This pass assumes the standing campaign objective remains founding-tester recruitment through the free demo and early-access cohort. The audience is independent knitwear designers and pattern publishers managing size ranges, body measurements, ease, gauge, grading, stitch and row counts, yardage, charts, schematics, samples, technical editing, test knitting, and publication preparation. The proposed form is a 35–45 second organic 9:16 founder-proof short for Instagram Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. Tone is first-person, quiet, specific, dry, craft-respectful, and suspicious of false completeness. Brief tier is a standard single-original-video package, blocked pending proof.

## Evidence gathered

### 1. A middle-size pass is not automatically coverage of the range

The Craft Yarn Council’s current body-sizing guidance distinguishes body measurements from finished-garment measurements and ease. It says patterns often include schematics with measurements across different pattern sizes and advises reviewing all sizing information before beginning. It also says fit and length charts are guidelines and that changes may alter the appearance of a project.[1] This supports a concrete coverage question: **which supported sizes, representations, and measurements were actually reviewed?** It does not establish that a central size can stand in for every size or that a product validates a range automatically.

The Craft Yarn Council’s pattern-writing guidance describes math, contemporaneous notes, transcription, standards, charts, schematics, technical editing, testing, and checks that directions, abbreviations, symbols, charts, materials, yarn quantities, and finished appearance agree.[2] The guidance reinforces that a technically complete pattern requires more than one visible pass; it does not define a universal representative sample or prove a Stitch & Scale coverage workflow.

The TKGA current Professional Knitwear Designer Certification page explicitly groups gauge, grading, yardage, spreadsheets, shaping, allowances, formulas, charting, pattern writing, technical editors, test knits, and layout within the design workflow, and separately covers business, rights, publication, and what to do when things go wrong.[3] This is a training curriculum, not an industry census or error-rate study. It is useful evidence that size coverage crosses calculation, representation, editorial, and physical-test boundaries.

Knitty’s current June 17, 2026 submission guidance requires complete pattern text, charts where needed, schematics, sizing conventions, clear photography, and math and instructions that produce the intended design. It distinguishes technical editing from optional test knitting and publication selection.[4] These are publication-specific requirements, not evidence that Stitch & Scale checks every supported size.

### 2. Evaluation methodology warns against silently generalizing from a sample

W3C’s WCAG Evaluation Methodology 2.0, published July 23, 2026, describes a process to define scope, explore the target product, select a representative sample set, evaluate the selected sample, and report findings.[5] It explicitly says that conformance claims cannot be made for an entire website based only on a selected subset, because unidentified errors may remain. Its overview applies the same structured-sample principle to websites, mobile apps, and other digital products.[6]

This is an analogy about evidence boundaries, not a claim about knitwear or Stitch & Scale. The safe transfer is precise: a central-size review, one chart, one schematic, or one test-knit report must be labeled as the checked sample. It cannot silently become a claim about every supported size, every representation, or the whole pattern. Boundary-size coverage must be explicitly recorded, and untested sizes remain unresolved.

### 3. Competitor documentation foregrounds output and access, not representative coverage evidence

EnvisioKnit documents chart creation, generated written instructions, layout, preview, and export formats.[7] knitCompanion documents project/PDF access, annotations, local/cloud locations, offline availability after synchronization, backup options, deletion warnings, and conflict risks.[8] These official pages show category expectations around output and project handling. They do not document a Stitch & Scale multi-size sample registry, boundary-size evidence, coverage state, or test-knit roster. The competitive opportunity is therefore an evidence-scope question, not a superiority claim.

### 4. Accessibility and platform constraints affect a coverage proof sequence

W3C’s WCAG 2.1 guidance says prerecorded synchronized media should provide captions containing dialogue, speaker identification, and meaningful non-speech audio, and says captions should not obscure relevant information.[9] YouTube documents adding subtitles and captions during or after upload.[10] Meta’s current safe-zone guidance says key text and logos should remain away from interface-covered edges; TikTok’s June 2026 in-feed specification recommends 9:16 at 540×960 or higher and says safe-zone size varies with dimensions and caption length.[11] [12]

The planned sequence therefore shows one size or coverage state per close shot, uses a conservative central safe zone, declares one narrator family, and requires caption-on plus caption-free review. No dense multi-size table will be shown as a single unreadable frame. These constraints improve proof readability but do not prove product accessibility, grading correctness, or platform acceptance.

### 5. Cultural, seasonal, and event context is bounded

The cultural context is respect for the people who need a pattern to serve real bodies and for designers who must communicate fit across a range without treating a single sample as universal. The framing must not speak for all bodies, all knitters, or all textile traditions. Knitty’s current page places Winter 2026 submissions near a September 15, 2026 deadline and a December 2026 issue.[4] This is real seasonal publication context, but Stitch & Scale has no verified connection to Knitty and no permission to borrow its deadline, themes, payment, rights, or urgency. No event hook is adopted. The concept remains evergreen.

## Audience pain and visual opportunity

The audience pain is **false coverage confidence**: a designer may check a central size, a single representation, or one polished chart and still lack explicit evidence for the boundary sizes or alternate representations in the supported range. This is a workflow hypothesis, not a prevalence claim and not a claim that any user published an error.

The visual opportunity is a fictional size-range strip with three separated cards: `CENTER SIZE CHECKED`, `BOUNDARY SIZE A: UNRESOLVED`, and `BOUNDARY SIZE B: UNRESOLVED`. A second row reads `SAMPLE / SCOPE / RESULT / LIMIT`. The center card receives a green pencil tick, while the boundary cards remain neutral and carry `NOT REVIEWED`. A fixed footer reads `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

The dramatic contradiction is: **a passing middle card can make an entire range look finished even when its edges have not been checked**. The emotional turn is from false closure to a specific, smaller coverage question.

## Research-derived decision

Research changed the concept from a broad grading or QA story to the narrower angle `size-coverage-state`: **show the difference between a central-size pass and evidence about the boundary of the range**. The hook becomes **“The middle size passed. What about the edges?”** The visual metaphor becomes a three-card size strip with unresolved boundary cards rather than the stopped source/derived arrow in pass 247. The proof surface becomes `SIZE / REPRESENTATION / SAMPLE / SCOPE / RESULT / LIMIT / REVIEWER / STATUS`. The emotional turn is false closure replaced by a named coverage boundary. The final line becomes **“A sample is not the whole range.”**

The nearest prior is pass 247 `derived-freshness-state`. Pass 247 asked whether a downstream value is current after an upstream source changes. Pass 248 asks whether a checked sample legitimately represents the supported size range. It is not a rename of `grading`, `size-range`, `test-knit-coverage`, `release-gate-roster`, `review-question-state`, `derived-freshness-state`, `labor-visibility-state`, or any prior evidence-state concept. It is a sample-scope and boundary-coverage question, not a roster, automatic sampler, defect detector, or fit-certification claim. The slug was checked against all 258 occupied angle rows and was unoccupied before reservation.

## Uncertainty and limits

CYC and TKGA describe sizing, grading, gauge, math, and publication workflows but do not establish typical boundary-size error rates, sample-size rules, prevalence, or any Stitch & Scale coverage feature. Knitty’s requirements are publication-specific. W3C WCAG-EM is a digital-product evaluation methodology and is used only as an evidence-boundary analogy. Competitor pages state their own features and do not establish comparative weakness or Stitch & Scale behavior. Platform rules cited here are primarily advertising or help guidance, and organic behavior may differ. No user interviews, analytics, product capture, proof-registry entry, multi-size grading audit, boundary-size test, usability study, or rights clearance was available.

## Decision and next action

**Decision:** create a planning-only blocked package for `size-coverage-state`; reserve the unique filename but do not generate or upload.

**Next action:** Product owner must provide one current, versioned, approved, non-private proof-registry entry or rights-cleared capture showing the exact size-coverage surface, including supported-size list, selected sample, boundary-size status, representation, scope, result, limit, reviewer/owner, version/date, capture owner, privacy treatment, destination, and rights/credit. Then rerun Director claim, asset, rights, caption, audio, platform, and acceptance validation before any Generator authorization.
