# Pass 249 Wide Research — Observed Knit State

**Research date:** 2026-08-20  
**Director pass:** 249  
**Fresh question:** **What can one observed test knit actually prove, what remains outside that observation, and how should the record preserve the knitter’s report without turning it into a release roster or universal approval?**

## Campaign assumption

No active brief was present at refresh. This pass assumes the standing campaign objective remains founding-tester recruitment through the free demo and early-access cohort. The audience is independent knitwear designers and pattern publishers managing pattern directions, charts, schematics, sizing, grading, technical editing, test knitting, sample notes, and publication preparation. The proposed form is a 35–45 second organic 9:16 founder-proof short for Instagram Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. Tone is first-person, quiet, specific, dry, craft-respectful, and suspicious of false completion. Brief tier is a standard single-original-video package, blocked pending proof.

## Evidence gathered

### 1. A test knit is an observed human attempt, not a universal verdict

The Craft Yarn Council’s current pattern-writing guidance describes a pattern as a roadmap and says creators should record what they did, clean up the notes, follow standards, and ask testers to follow the pattern without assumptions or alterations. Its suggested tester questions include whether directions are clear and concise, abbreviations, symbols, and charts align, materials and yarn quantities are comprehensive, and the finished item looks as intended.[1] This supports a structured observation record: identify the exact pattern version, size, gauge, representation, steps followed, observed result, and feedback. It does not support calling the observation proof that every size, construction route, or knitter will have the same experience.

The TKGA current Professional Knitwear Designer Certification page separates gauge, grading, yardage, spreadsheets, shaping, formulas, charting, pattern writing, technical editors, running test knits, layout, publication work, and business records.[2] The program’s own structure makes test knitting one part of a larger workflow. It is a training curriculum, not a study of test-knit reliability or evidence that Stitch & Scale records this surface.

Knitty’s current June 17, 2026 guidance is especially explicit about boundaries. It requires the designer to submit a complete pattern and says the designer’s math and grading should be as good as possible. It says Knitty does not have patterns test knit and that a designer may test knit beforehand, but a test knit does not necessarily influence publication selection. It also separates the publication’s technical-editing work from the designer’s preparation and publication decision.[3] This supports the claim that a test-knit observation and a publication or release decision are different evidence layers.

### 2. The observation needs a scope, not just a green check

The useful record is not `TESTED: YES`. It is a scoped observation containing `PATTERN VERSION`, `SIZE`, `GAUGE`, `REPRESENTATION`, `STEPS`, `OBSERVATION`, `EVIDENCE`, `LIMIT`, `FOLLOW-UP`, and `STATUS`. A report that says “the test knit passed” without naming the version, size, gauge, or observed evidence is not strong enough to support a whole-range or publication claim. This is a workflow design principle derived from the sources and from the project’s deterministic QA boundaries, not a claim that current Stitch & Scale already stores these fields.

The observation should distinguish what happened from what a reviewer inferred. The human knitter’s report is a recorded observation; the designer or editor may then make a decision; a test knit does not replace technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, or additional testing. The same report should preserve unresolved feedback instead of converting every note into a pass.

### 3. The concept must not become the imported release roster

The imported `release-gate-roster` concept is a later, narrower successor candidate to `false-completion`; it concerns accountability and who checked a human gate. The present concept is different: it concerns **what one test knit observed and what the observation cannot generalize**. It has no roster, tester assignment queue, staffing grid, automatic routing, release-room state, or “who checked the size” hook. Generic human-gate proof does not authorize the imported roster. The observation record must remain evidence about a bounded event, not a release decision or owner queue.

### 4. Accessibility and platform constraints affect the observation proof

W3C’s WCAG 2.1 guidance says prerecorded synchronized media should provide captions containing dialogue, speaker identification, and meaningful non-speech audio, and says captions should not obscure relevant information.[4] YouTube documents adding subtitles and captions during or after upload.[5] Meta’s current safe-zone guidance says key text and logos should remain away from interface-covered edges; TikTok’s June 2026 in-feed specification recommends 9:16 at 540×960 or higher and says safe-zone size varies with dimensions and caption length.[6] [7]

The planned sequence therefore shows one observation record per close shot, uses a conservative central safe zone, declares one narrator family, and requires caption-on plus caption-free review. No dense feedback table will be shown as a single unreadable frame. These constraints improve proof readability but do not prove product accessibility, test-knit reliability, or platform acceptance.

### 5. Cultural, seasonal, and event context is bounded

The cultural context is respect for the person who follows a pattern and takes the time to report what happened. The framing must not romanticize unpaid labor, speak for all testers, or imply that one knitter represents all bodies, techniques, abilities, or yarn conditions. Knitty’s current page places Winter 2026 submissions near a September 15, 2026 deadline and a December 2026 issue.[3] That is publication-specific seasonal context, not a Stitch & Scale partnership, event, payment, rights, or urgency authorization. No event hook is adopted. The concept remains evergreen.

## Audience pain and visual opportunity

The audience pain is **false closure from an unscoped test result**: a designer or team can preserve a green check while losing the version, size, gauge, exact steps, observed evidence, or unresolved follow-up that made the check meaningful. This is a workflow hypothesis, not a prevalence claim and not a claim that any user made a defective publication.

The visual opportunity is a fictional observation card. The top reads `TEST KNIT OBSERVATION`; the fields appear one at a time: `PATTERN VERSION`, `SIZE`, `GAUGE`, `REPRESENTATION`, `STEPS`, `OBSERVATION`, `EVIDENCE`, `LIMIT`, `FOLLOW-UP`, `STATUS`. The final field remains `STATUS: OBSERVED / DECISION UNRESOLVED`. A second card reads `ONE OBSERVATION ≠ WHOLE RANGE`. Every card carries `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

The dramatic contradiction is: **a green check can be less honest than a bounded observation with a visible limit**. The emotional turn is from release pressure to evidence clarity.

## Research-derived decision

Research changed the concept from a broad test-knit coverage story to the narrower angle `observed-knit-state`: **preserve the bounded observation and its limit before anyone turns it into a release decision**. The hook becomes **“A test knit is an observation, not a universal verdict.”** The visual metaphor becomes a scoped observation card with a visible unresolved decision rather than the three-card size strip in pass 248. The proof surface becomes `PATTERN_VERSION / SIZE / GAUGE / REPRESENTATION / STEPS / OBSERVATION / EVIDENCE / LIMIT / FOLLOW_UP / STATUS`. The emotional turn is release pressure replaced by evidence clarity. The final line becomes **“Record what happened before you decide what it proves.”**

The nearest prior is pass 248 `size-coverage-state`. Pass 248 asked whether a checked sample represents the supported size range. Pass 249 asks what a single test-knit observation actually records and what it cannot generalize. It is not a rename of `test-knit-coverage`, `release-gate-roster`, `false-completion`, `size-coverage-state`, `derived-freshness-state`, `labor-visibility-state`, or any prior evidence-state concept. It has no roster, assignment, routing, or publication approval surface. The slug was checked against all 259 occupied angle rows and was unoccupied before reservation.

## Uncertainty and limits

CYC and TKGA describe workflow and tester questions but do not establish test-knit reliability, coverage percentages, tester compensation norms, error rates, or Stitch & Scale behavior. Knitty’s test-knit and technical-editing rules are publication-specific. The observation-versus-decision separation is an evidence discipline, not a claim about an existing product feature. Competitor pages were not used to establish test-knit superiority. Platform rules cited here are primarily advertising or help guidance, and organic behavior may differ. No user interviews, analytics, product capture, proof-registry entry, test-knit record, privacy clearance, usability study, or rights clearance was available.

## Decision and next action

**Decision:** create a planning-only blocked package for `observed-knit-state`; reserve the unique filename but do not generate or upload.

**Next action:** Product owner must provide one current, versioned, approved, non-private proof-registry entry or rights-cleared capture showing the exact observed-knit surface, including pattern version, size, gauge, representation, steps, observation, evidence, limit, follow-up, status, capture owner, privacy treatment, destination, and rights/credit. Then rerun Director claim, asset, rights, caption, audio, platform, and acceptance validation before any Generator authorization.
