# Pass 250 Wide Research — Ambiguity State

**Research date:** 2026-08-20  
**Director pass:** 250  
**Fresh question:** **When a pattern direction can be read two ways, what evidence distinguishes an unclear instruction from a maker’s legitimate choice, and what should remain unresolved until a technical editor or test knitter reviews it?**

## Campaign assumption

No active brief was present at refresh. This pass assumes the standing campaign objective remains founding-tester recruitment through the free demo and early-access cohort. The audience is independent knitwear designers and pattern publishers managing pattern directions, charts, schematics, sizing, grading, technical editing, test knitting, sample notes, and publication preparation. The proposed form is a 35–45 second organic 9:16 founder-proof short for Instagram Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. Tone is first-person, quiet, specific, dry, craft-respectful, and suspicious of false certainty. Brief tier is a standard single-original-video package, blocked pending proof.

## Evidence gathered

### 1. A pattern direction must act like a usable roadmap

The Craft Yarn Council’s current pattern-writing guidance describes a pattern as a roadmap that gives a reader directions and instructions to achieve a designed result. It describes written patterns, tutorials, charts, graphs, and schematics as different forms of that roadmap. It recommends recording work as it happens, cleaning the notes, following standards for terms and abbreviations, and checking that sizing, charts, and symbols align.[1] This supports a precise creative question: **can a reader identify the action, object, scope, and expected result of this instruction without having to guess?** It does not define a universal ambiguity score or prove that Stitch & Scale evaluates one.

The same guidance treats technical editing as a second set of eyes focused on industry and technical clarity, and describes test knitting as following the pattern without assumptions or alterations and then reporting whether directions are clear and concise, abbreviations, symbols, and charts line up, materials and yarn quantities are complete, and the finished look matches the intention.[1] The source therefore separates ambiguity exposure from automatic defect classification: a note that “I read this two ways” is evidence for review, not by itself a final verdict about the designer or pattern.

### 2. Technical editing and test knitting are different evidence gates

The TKGA current Professional Knitwear Designer Certification page places gauge, grading, yardage, spreadsheets, shaping, formulas, charting, pattern writing, technical editors, running test knits, layout, publication, business records, and rights in distinct parts of the professional workflow.[2] This supports keeping a direction’s clarity, mathematical correctness, physical experience, and publication decision separate. It is a training curriculum, not an error-rate study and not evidence of a Stitch & Scale ambiguity feature.

Knitty’s current submission guidance, last updated June 17, 2026, says designers submit complete patterns and should make their math and grading as good as possible. It says Knitty’s technical editors help solve math mistakes and improve fit, while Knitty does not test knit patterns; optional prior test knitting is the designer’s choice and does not necessarily affect publication selection.[3] This is a current publication-specific boundary: an unclear instruction may need editorial clarification, maker feedback, or both, but neither alone should be silently promoted to a universal publication verdict.

### 3. A useful ambiguity record names the competing readings

The useful record is not `CLEAR: NO`. It is a bounded ambiguity record containing `PATTERN_VERSION`, `LOCATION`, `ORIGINAL_TEXT`, `READING_A`, `READING_B`, `MISSING_CONTEXT`, `EXPECTED_RESULT`, `EVIDENCE`, `REVIEWER`, `NEXT_CHECK`, and `STATUS`. The two readings must be preserved as interpretations, not rewritten into a single invented correction. The record should also distinguish an instruction ambiguity from a legitimate choice of technique, gauge, ease, or construction method.

The record should preserve the exact source text, the point at which the two readings diverge, and the evidence needed to resolve it. If a chart, abbreviation, schematic, or preceding instruction changes the interpretation, that context must be named. If the record has only a maker’s question and no approved technical review, status remains `AMBIGUITY_REPORTED / DECISION_UNRESOLVED`. This is a workflow and evidence discipline, not a claim about existing Stitch & Scale behavior.

### 4. Accessibility and platform constraints affect an ambiguity proof

W3C’s WCAG 2.1 guidance says prerecorded synchronized media should provide captions containing dialogue, speaker identification, and meaningful non-speech audio, and says captions should not obscure relevant information.[4] YouTube documents adding subtitles and captions during or after upload.[5] Meta’s current safe-zone guidance says key text and logos should remain away from interface-covered edges; TikTok’s June 2026 in-feed specification recommends 9:16 at 540×960 or higher and says safe-zone size varies with dimensions and caption length.[6] [7]

The planned sequence therefore shows one instruction ambiguity per close shot, keeps the original text and two readings separated, uses a conservative central safe zone, declares one narrator family, and requires caption-on plus caption-free review. No dense side-by-side table will be used as the only proof. These constraints improve legibility but do not prove product accessibility, ambiguity detection, or platform acceptance.

### 5. Cultural, seasonal, and event context is bounded

The cultural context is respect for the maker who should not have to reverse-engineer a private design intention from an unclear line. The framing must not speak for every knitter, every language, every disability, or every textile tradition. Knitty’s current page places Winter 2026 submissions near a September 15, 2026 deadline and a December 2026 issue.[3] That is publication-specific seasonal context, not a Stitch & Scale partnership, event, payment, rights, or urgency authorization. No event hook is adopted. The concept remains evergreen.

## Audience pain and visual opportunity

The audience pain is **interpretation debt**: an instruction can look complete in the document while leaving two plausible actions for the person who must follow it. That ambiguity may be exposed through a tester question or technical-editing note, but a single report does not automatically establish a defect, a required correction, or a release block. This is a workflow hypothesis, not a prevalence claim and not a claim that any Stitch & Scale user experienced a failure.

The visual opportunity is a fictional ambiguity card. The top reads `INSTRUCTION REVIEW`; below it are `PATTERN VERSION`, `LOCATION`, and `ORIGINAL TEXT`. The text then splits into `READING A` and `READING B`. A lower strip reads `MISSING CONTEXT`, `EXPECTED RESULT`, `EVIDENCE`, `REVIEWER`, `NEXT CHECK`, and `STATUS: AMBIGUITY REPORTED / DECISION UNRESOLVED`. Every card carries `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

The dramatic contradiction is: **the neatest sentence on the page can be the one that forces the maker to invent the missing context**. The emotional turn is from pretending clarity to naming the two readings without inventing a verdict.

## Research-derived decision

Research changed the concept from the broad observation and test-knit territory to the narrower angle `ambiguity-state`: **preserve the exact instruction and the competing readings before a human review decides what is wrong, what is a choice, and what remains unresolved**. The hook becomes **“If two makers read the same line two ways, the line is not finished.”** The visual metaphor becomes a split instruction card with `READING A` and `READING B`, rather than the scoped observation card in pass 249. The proof surface becomes `PATTERN_VERSION / LOCATION / ORIGINAL_TEXT / READING_A / READING_B / MISSING_CONTEXT / EXPECTED_RESULT / EVIDENCE / REVIEWER / NEXT_CHECK / STATUS`. The emotional turn is false clarity replaced by an explicit ambiguity record. The final line becomes **“Name the two readings before you choose one.”**

The nearest prior is pass 249 `observed-knit-state`. Pass 249 asked what one observed test knit records and what it cannot generalize. Pass 250 asks how an unclear instruction should preserve competing interpretations before a technical or physical review decides. It is not a rename of `review-question-state`, `requirement-interpretation-state`, `interpretation-state`, `actionability-state`, `observed-knit-state`, `size-coverage-state`, `false-completion`, `release-gate-roster`, or any prior evidence-state concept. It has no test-knit roster, tester assignment, release approval, automatic ambiguity detection, or automatic correction surface. The slug was checked against all 260 occupied angle rows and was unoccupied before reservation.

## Uncertainty and limits

CYC and TKGA describe pattern-writing, clarity, technical editing, and testing practices but do not establish ambiguity rates, a universal ambiguity taxonomy, or Stitch & Scale behavior. Knitty’s rules are publication-specific. The two-reading record is a proposed evidence discipline, not a product capability. Competitor pages were not used to establish comparative weakness. Platform rules cited here are primarily advertising or help guidance, and organic behavior may differ. No user interviews, analytics, product capture, proof-registry entry, technical-editing review, test-knit report, privacy clearance, usability study, or rights clearance was available.

## Decision and next action

**Decision:** create a planning-only blocked package for `ambiguity-state`; reserve the unique filename but do not generate or upload.

**Next action:** Product owner must provide one current, versioned, approved, non-private proof-registry entry or rights-cleared capture showing the exact ambiguity surface, including pattern version, location, original text, competing readings, missing context, expected result, evidence, reviewer, next check, status, capture owner, privacy treatment, destination, and rights/credit. Then rerun Director claim, asset, rights, caption, audio, platform, and acceptance validation before any Generator authorization.
