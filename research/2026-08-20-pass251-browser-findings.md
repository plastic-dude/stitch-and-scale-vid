# Pass 251 Wide Research — Maker Entry State

**Research date:** 2026-08-20  
**Director pass:** 251  
**Fresh question:** **Before a maker starts a knitwear pattern, what minimum information must be visible and coherent—pattern identity, materials, gauge, notes, size, support files, and scope—and what remains a human choice rather than a product readiness verdict?**

## Campaign assumption

No active brief was present at refresh. This pass assumes the standing campaign objective remains founding-tester recruitment through the free demo and early-access cohort. The audience is independent knitwear designers and pattern publishers managing pattern identity, materials, gauge, sizing, notes, charts, schematics, technical editing, test knitting, sample notes, and publication preparation. The proposed form is a 35–45 second organic 9:16 founder-proof short for Instagram Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. Tone is first-person, quiet, specific, dry, craft-respectful, and suspicious of false readiness. Brief tier is a standard single-original-video package, blocked pending proof.

## Evidence gathered

### 1. The beginning of a pattern is a decision surface, not a decorative cover

Quince & Co.’s current pattern-reading guide says a knitter should check the materials list, gauge, notes, repeats, abbreviations, support materials, size, and the whole pattern before starting. It calls the pattern a roadmap and warns that small materials such as stitch markers, waste yarn, and buttons can affect the project’s flow.[1] Its gauge section says the maker should use the correct stitch pattern, yarn weight, and fiber type for the swatch, and pause to investigate a drastically different result rather than proceeding by assumption.[1]

The same source says size should be cross-checked against the pattern’s actual measurement guide, that body measurements and finished measurements should not be silently conflated, and that the selected size can affect yarn quantity.[1] This makes the maker-entry question concrete: **can the person identify the pattern version, intended size, required materials, gauge basis, notes, and support files before the first operation?** It does not establish a universal checklist score or prove a Stitch & Scale entry surface.

### 2. CYC separates body measurements, finished measurements, ease, and guidance limits

The Craft Yarn Council’s current body-sizing guidance says a maker should review all sizing information before beginning. It distinguishes body measurements from finished-garment measurements, explains that fit depends on body measurement plus ease, and says fit and length charts are guidelines; individual changes may alter the appearance of a garment.[2] This is important for the entry card: `SIZE SELECTED` is not the same thing as `FIT GUARANTEED`, and `MEASUREMENT REVIEWED` is not the same thing as `PUBLICATION READY`.

The CYC pattern-writing guidance also frames a pattern as a roadmap and recommends recording work as it happens, cleaning notes, following standards, and asking testers whether directions, abbreviations, symbols, charts, materials, yarn quantities, and finished appearance align.[3] A pre-start record can expose missing inputs; it cannot replace technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, or test knitting.

### 3. Publication practice expects complete inputs but keeps decisions separate

Knitty’s current submission guidance, last updated June 17, 2026, says submissions should contain a completed design, complete pattern text, appropriate sizing, materials, measurements, charts or schematics where required, and supporting images. It says designers should make their math and grading as good as possible; technical editors help solve math mistakes and improve fit; Knitty does not test knit patterns; and optional prior test knitting is the designer’s choice and does not necessarily affect publication selection.[4]

The TKGA current Professional Knitwear Designer Certification page separates inspiration and submissions, gauge, grading, yardage, spreadsheets, shaping, formulas, charting, technical editors, running test knits, layout, publication, business records, and rights.[5] Together, these sources support a narrow maker-entry concept: make prerequisites and scope visible before work begins, while preserving later human decisions as separate states.

### 4. Competitor context shows entry information, not Stitch & Scale proof

Quince & Co.’s guide is a content and workflow example from a yarn/pattern publisher: it exposes the categories a maker needs to inspect before beginning. It does not document a Stitch & Scale surface, automatic entry validation, readiness scoring, fit certification, or publication approval.[1] EnvisioKnit documents charting, written instructions, layout, preview, and export; knitCompanion documents project/PDF access, annotations, local/cloud locations, offline availability after synchronization, backup options, and deletion/conflict cautions. These competitor pages establish category context only; they do not prove comparative weakness or Stitch & Scale capability.

### 5. Accessibility and platform constraints affect the proof sequence

W3C’s WCAG 2.1 guidance says prerecorded synchronized media should provide captions containing dialogue, speaker identification, and meaningful non-speech audio, and says captions should not obscure relevant information.[6] YouTube documents adding subtitles and captions during or after upload.[7] Meta’s current safe-zone guidance says key text and logos should remain away from interface-covered edges; TikTok’s June 2026 in-feed specification recommends 9:16 at 540×960 or higher and notes that safe-zone size varies with dimensions and caption length.[8] [9]

The planned sequence therefore shows one entry field family per close shot—identity, materials, gauge, notes, size, support files, and scope—rather than a dense checklist. Captions remain outside the cards and hands. One narrator family is declared, and caption-on plus caption-free reviews remain mandatory. These practices improve legibility but do not prove accessibility conformance, current organic platform acceptance, or product readiness.

### 6. Cultural, seasonal, and event context is bounded

The cultural context is respect for the maker’s first five minutes with a pattern: the time when missing prerequisites become avoidable confusion. The concept must not imply that every maker uses the same sequence, measures the same way, or needs the same support. Knitty’s Winter 2026 schedule and September 15, 2026 submission deadline provide publication-specific seasonal context, not a Stitch & Scale partnership, event hook, deadline, payment, rights, or urgency authorization.[4] No event hook is adopted; the concept remains evergreen.

## Audience pain and visual opportunity

The audience pain is **entry-state blindness**: a document may appear available while the maker still lacks a clear identity, material basis, gauge basis, selected size, notes, support file, or stated scope. This is a workflow hypothesis grounded in the sources’ before-start guidance, not a prevalence claim and not a claim that Stitch & Scale users experienced a failure.

The visual opportunity is a fictional `BEFORE YOU START` card that unfolds into seven small fields: `PATTERN ID / VERSION`, `MATERIALS`, `GAUGE BASIS`, `NOTES`, `SIZE / MEASUREMENTS`, `SUPPORT FILES`, and `SCOPE / LIMIT`. A final strip reads `ENTRY REVIEWED / HUMAN CHOICE REMAINS`. Every card carries `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

The dramatic contradiction is: **the first useful pattern decision is often not “start knitting”; it is “what am I actually starting with?”** The emotional turn is from a false green light to a visible entry boundary.

## Product truth and rights reconciliation

The repository contains no current product-proof registry entry or approved non-private capture verifying a Stitch & Scale maker-entry surface, pattern identity/version field, materials field, gauge basis, notes, support-file field, size/measurement field, scope field, entry review state, or any readiness score. No generated card may be presented as current product UI. No claim may be made that Stitch & Scale automatically validates prerequisites, chooses a size, checks gauge, certifies fit, detects missing inputs, scores readiness, or approves publication.

No external media, real pattern file, customer record, private notes, tester identity, feedback screenshot, chart, schematic, or third-party imagery is approved. Rights and privacy are unverified. Any future product capture must provide product version/date, exact visible labels, capture owner, privacy treatment, destination, and rights/credit data. Illustrative cards may show category names but not real pattern text, counts, measurements, labels, controls, or user data.

## Research-derived decision

Research changed the concept from pass 250’s ambiguity territory to the narrower first-entry boundary `maker-entry-state`: **show what must be known before work begins, while keeping size choice, fit, gauge confirmation, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, test knitting, and publication decisions separate**. The hook becomes **“Before you start, what exactly do you have?”** The visual metaphor becomes a fictional entry card unfolding from identity to materials, gauge, notes, size, support files, and scope, rather than pass 250’s split-reading card. The proof surface becomes `PATTERN_ID / VERSION / MATERIALS / GAUGE_BASIS / NOTES / SIZE_MEASUREMENTS / SUPPORT_FILES / SCOPE_LIMIT / ENTRY_REVIEW / HUMAN_CHOICE_REMAINS`. The emotional turn is false readiness replaced by a named entry boundary. The final line becomes **“Start with the information, not the assumption.”**

The nearest prior is pass 250 `ambiguity-state`. Pass 250 asked how one unclear instruction preserves competing readings before human review. Pass 251 asks what minimum context must be visible before a maker begins. It is not a rename of `prerequisite-state`, `reader-handoff`, `readability-under-load`, `publication-boundary-state`, `audience-readiness-state`, `review-question-state`, `interpretation-state`, `actionability-state`, `observed-knit-state`, `size-coverage-state`, `false-completion`, or `release-gate-roster`. It has no tester roster, release routing, automatic readiness score, fit certification, or automatic prerequisite check. The slug was checked against all 261 occupied angle rows and was unoccupied before reservation.

## Uncertainty and limits

CYC and Quince & Co. describe practical pattern-entry information but do not establish a universal maker-entry schema, user-completion rate, or product requirement. Knitty and TKGA describe publication and professional workflow boundaries; they do not prove Stitch & Scale behavior. Competitor pages were used for category context, not comparative performance claims. Platform rules cited here are primarily help or advertising guidance, and organic behavior may differ. No user interviews, analytics, product capture, proof-registry entry, usability study, technical-editing review, test-knit report, privacy clearance, or rights clearance was available.

## Decision and next action

**Decision:** create a planning-only blocked package for `maker-entry-state`; reserve the unique filename but do not generate or upload.

**Next action:** Product owner must provide one current, versioned, approved, non-private proof-registry entry or rights-cleared capture showing the exact maker-entry surface, including pattern ID/version, materials, gauge basis, notes, size/measurements, support files, scope/limit, entry review state, human-choice boundary, capture owner, privacy treatment, destination, and rights/credit. Then rerun Director claim, asset, rights, caption, audio, platform, and acceptance validation before any Generator authorization.
