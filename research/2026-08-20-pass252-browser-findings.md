# Pass 252 Wide Research — Version Delta State

**Research date:** 2026-08-20  
**Director pass:** 252  
**Fresh question:** **When a pattern changes after a maker or tester has started, what exact delta must be disclosed so prior notes do not silently apply, and how should a change notice remain separate from a defect verdict, release approval, or automatic correction?**

## Campaign assumption

No active brief was present at refresh. This pass assumes the standing campaign objective remains founding-tester recruitment through the free demo and early-access cohort. The audience is independent knitwear designers and pattern publishers managing pattern versions, corrections, grading, charts, schematics, materials, technical editing, test knitting, samples, and publication preparation. The proposed form is a 35–45 second organic 9:16 founder-proof short for Instagram Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. Tone is first-person, quiet, specific, dry, craft-respectful, and suspicious of silent changes. Brief tier is a standard single-original-video package, blocked pending proof.

## Evidence gathered

### 1. Pattern changes need an explicit record, not a silent replacement

The Craft Yarn Council’s current pattern-writing guidance recommends writing down what was done after each row or round, then crossing out and adding a replacement when a change is made. It says the change should be noted and typed into the cleaned-up pattern, and it then separates standards, technical editing, and test knitting.[1] This supports a narrow change question: **what changed, where, from which version, why, and which sizes or representations does the delta affect?** It does not establish a universal versioning system or prove that Stitch & Scale automatically records deltas.

The same CYC guidance asks testers whether directions, abbreviations, symbols, and charts line up, whether materials and yarn quantities are complete, and whether the finished appearance matches the intended design.[1] A later correction therefore needs an affected-surface field and an evidence field; it should not silently invalidate the original tester report or rewrite the tester’s observation into a new conclusion.

### 2. A change notice should distinguish changed, fixed, deprecated, removed, and known issue

Keep a Changelog’s current 2.0.0 guidance describes a changelog as a curated chronological list of notable changes for each version, written for humans. It groups changes into Added, Changed, Deprecated, Removed, Fixed, and Security, says breaking changes should be marked clearly, and distinguishes a changelog from release notes: the changelog is the ongoing record while release notes are a curated announcement.[2]

The source also states that known issues are discovered rather than changed, and that a machine can draft a changelog but a human must curate what is notable and explain it plainly.[2] For this campaign concept, the useful proof surface is therefore not `UPDATE: DONE`. It is `FROM_VERSION / TO_VERSION / CHANGE_TYPE / AFFECTED_SURFACE / OLD_STATE / NEW_STATE / WHY / SIZES_OR_REPRESENTATIONS / ACTION_REQUIRED / EVIDENCE / OWNER / STATUS`. This is a proposed evidence discipline, not a current Stitch & Scale feature.

### 3. A version delta is not automatically a defect or a release decision

Knitty’s current submission guidance, last updated June 17, 2026, says designers submit complete patterns with math and grading as good as possible. It says technical editors help solve math mistakes and improve fit; Knitty does not test knit patterns; optional prior test knitting is the designer’s choice and does not necessarily influence publication selection; and publication selection is separate from the designer’s own preparation work.[3]

The TKGA current Professional Knitwear Designer Certification page separates gauge, grading, yardage, spreadsheets, formulas, charting, technical editors, running test knits, layout, publication, business records, and rights.[4] These sources support preserving the old observation, the changed artifact, and the human decision separately. A delta record can say `ACTION REQUIRED` without saying `DEFECT VERIFIED`, `ALL SIZES SAFE`, or `APPROVED FOR RELEASE`.

### 4. Accessibility favors user-controlled and perceivable change communication

W3C’s WCAG 2.2 guidance for Change on Request says major changes of context should be initiated by user request or have a mechanism to turn them off, because unexpected changes can disorient people with disabilities. It gives an “Update now” control rather than silently updating content as an example.[5] This is an accessibility analogy for explicit change communication, not a claim that a Stitch & Scale update surface conforms to WCAG.

W3C’s prerecorded-caption guidance says synchronized captions should contain dialogue, speaker identification, and meaningful non-speech audio, and should not obscure relevant information.[6] YouTube documents adding subtitles and captions during or after upload.[7] Meta’s current safe-zone guidance says key creative elements should remain away from interface-covered edges; TikTok’s June 2026 in-feed specification recommends 9:16 at 540×960 or higher and notes that safe-zone size varies with dimensions and caption length.[8] [9]

The planned short therefore uses a user-visible `FROM_VERSION` to `TO_VERSION` card, one change category per close shot, a clear `ACTION REQUIRED` strip, and no surprise flip or fake auto-refresh. Captions stay outside the proof. One narrator family is declared, and caption-on plus caption-free reviews remain mandatory.

### 5. Competitor and publication context do not prove Stitch & Scale behavior

Quince & Co.’s current pattern-reading guide says notes may call out construction information or common questions from test knitters and advises reading the whole pattern before starting.[10] EnvisioKnit documents charting, written instructions, layout, preview, and export; knitCompanion documents project/PDF access, annotations, local/cloud locations, offline availability after synchronization, backup options, deletion/conflict cautions. These pages show category expectations around pattern access and support, not Stitch & Scale version-delta records or automatic change alerts.

### 6. Cultural and seasonal context is bounded

The cultural context is respect for the social responsibility of changing shared instructions: a designer’s correction can affect people who have already invested time, materials, and trust, so the change should be named without blaming the earlier reader or pretending that every downstream consequence is known. This is a framing choice, not a claim about a particular community, prevalence, or user harm.

Knitty’s Winter 2026 schedule and September 15, 2026 deadline provide publication-specific seasonal context but do not authorize a Stitch & Scale partnership, event hook, deadline, payment, rights, or urgency claim.[3] No event hook is adopted; the concept remains evergreen.

## Audience pain and visual opportunity

The audience pain is **silent-delta debt**: a designer or tester may keep reading an earlier pattern state after a correction changes a row, chart, material, measurement, or supported representation. The key risk is not simply that a change exists; it is that the affected person cannot tell whether their prior notes, sample, or test result still apply. This is a workflow hypothesis grounded in the sources’ change-record and technical-review practices, not a prevalence claim and not a claim that a Stitch & Scale user experienced a failure.

The visual opportunity is a fictional `VERSION DELTA` card. The top reads `FROM: V001` and `TO: V002`. The middle shows one category at a time: `CHANGED / CHART C-01 / ROWS 1–12`, then `ACTION REQUIRED / RECHECK`, then `AFFECTED SIZES / REPRESENTATIONS`. The lower strip reads `WHY`, `EVIDENCE`, `OWNER`, and `STATUS: HUMAN REVIEW`. Every card carries `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

The dramatic contradiction is: **a correction can make the new pattern better while making the old notes unsafe to reuse unless the change is named.** The emotional turn is from silent replacement to an explicit delta with an action boundary.

## Research-derived decision

Research changed the concept from pass 251’s maker-entry boundary to `version-delta-state`: **show exactly what changed between two versions, what is affected, what action is required, and what remains a human review decision**. The hook becomes **“A correction is not complete until the people using the old version can see what changed.”** The visual metaphor becomes a from/to delta card with a visible affected-surface strip, rather than pass 251’s pre-start entry card. The proof surface becomes `FROM_VERSION / TO_VERSION / CHANGE_TYPE / AFFECTED_SURFACE / OLD_STATE / NEW_STATE / WHY / SIZES_OR_REPRESENTATIONS / ACTION_REQUIRED / EVIDENCE / OWNER / STATUS`. The emotional turn is silent replacement replaced by explicit delta communication. The final line becomes **“Name the change before you ask anyone to trust the new version.”**

The nearest prior is pass 251 `maker-entry-state`. Pass 251 asked what minimum context must be visible before a maker begins. Pass 252 asks how a later change should communicate its delta to people who may already be working from an earlier state. It is not a rename of `change-notice`, `change-communication-state`, `version-lock`, `drift-state`, `revisitability-state`, `derived-freshness-state`, `ambiguity-state`, `maker-entry-state`, `review-question-state`, `false-completion`, or `release-gate-roster`. It has no automatic version comparison, correction propagation, tester notification, release roster, or release approval surface. The slug was checked against all 262 occupied angle rows and was unoccupied before reservation.

## Uncertainty and limits

CYC describes recording changes while writing a pattern but does not define a universal change-log schema or prove product behavior. Keep a Changelog is software-oriented and is used here as a communication analogy, not as knitwear law. W3C Change on Request concerns web content and accessibility; it does not certify any Stitch & Scale update behavior. Knitty and TKGA describe publication and professional workflow boundaries, not a universal release-gate process. Competitor pages provide category context only. Platform rules cited here are primarily help or advertising guidance, and organic behavior may differ. No user interviews, analytics, product capture, proof-registry entry, technical-editing review, test-knit report, privacy clearance, or rights clearance was available.

## Decision and next action

**Decision:** create a planning-only blocked package for `version-delta-state`; reserve the unique filename but do not generate or upload.

**Next action:** Product owner must provide one current, versioned, approved, non-private proof-registry entry or rights-cleared capture showing the exact version-delta surface, including from/to versions, change type, affected surface, old/new state, why, affected sizes or representations, action required, evidence, owner, status, capture owner, privacy treatment, destination, and rights/credit. Then rerun Director claim, asset, rights, caption, audio, platform, and acceptance validation before any Generator authorization.
