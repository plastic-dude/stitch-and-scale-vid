# Pass 255 Wide Research — Attribution Boundary State

**Research date:** 2026-08-20
**Pass:** 255
**Question:** When a knitwear pattern or campaign uses an external chart, photograph, video, translation, schematic, or support resource, what must be recorded to distinguish credit from permission to reproduce, and how can that distinction become a truthful short-video story without claiming legal certainty or product capability?

## Decision summary

The research changes the concept from a generic “remember to credit sources” reminder to a sharper **attribution-versus-permission boundary**. The story will show that naming a creator or linking to an original source can help people find and acknowledge the work, but neither a credit line nor a link alone proves that the planned use is licensed, within scope, current, or allowed for redistribution. The planned proof surface is an illustrative `USE CHECK` card with separate fields for `WORK`, `RIGHTSHOLDER / REQUESTED CREDIT`, `SOURCE LINK`, `LICENSE OR PERMISSION`, `ALLOWED USE`, `CHANNEL / TERM`, `MODIFICATION`, and `HUMAN RIGHTS REVIEW`. It will remain explicitly labeled `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.

This is a planning and rights-discipline proposal, not legal advice. Copyright and license questions depend on jurisdiction, facts, contract language, and the particular work. The package will not state that a use is fair, legal, cleared, or approved without current rights evidence and appropriate human review.

## Audience and craft workflow interpretation

Independent designers and publishers work with multiple expressive objects in one pattern package: written instructions, charts, schematics, photographs, videos, translations, and external support links. Knitty’s current submission guide is useful evidence that these are operationally distinct files and relationships, while CYC and Quince & Co. material from earlier passes establish that charts, keys, schematics, and support resources carry different reader functions. The audience pain is not simply “forgetting a credit.” It is the false comfort of a tidy source line that leaves the reuse scope, permitted channel, modification, term, or reviewer unresolved.

The strongest human contradiction is: **the source can be named while the use is still unauthorized or unclear**. That contradiction is safe to dramatize as a workflow risk, not as a claim that Stitch & Scale users have experienced infringement or that the product currently prevents it.

## Platform, accessibility, audio, and visual decisions

The standing production lessons remain binding: captions must not cover the proof card, hands, numbers, logos, or controls; dense tables need close readable shots; one narrator family must be declared; caption-on and caption-free reviews are mandatory; and generated text cannot stand in for current UI truth. The pass-255 proof card therefore uses one field family per close shot rather than a dense rights table. The fixed footer `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI` remains on every readable card.

A 9:16 organic short is still the sensible default because the current campaign has no active brief and the repository objective is founding-tester recruitment. The concept will use a quiet tabletop, one narrator family, original paper props, and no third-party imagery, screenshots, music, logos, or pattern excerpts. The CTA remains an invitation to founding testers to identify what evidence a product should preserve; it does not promise a completed rights-management feature.

## Product-truth reconciliation

No current product-proof registry entry or approved non-private capture was found for an attribution, rights, permission, license, channel-scope, term, modification, or human-rights-review surface. Therefore the card is illustrative only and the brief is **blocked**. No generated UI, invented warning, license badge, automatic clearance, copyright verdict, or competitor screen may be presented as Stitch & Scale.

Technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, translation review, and test knitting remain distinct human evidence gates. They cannot be replaced by a rights card or by generated text.

## Research conclusion

The research supports a materially distinct angle named `attribution-boundary-state`: it is about the difference between **crediting a source** and **having permission for a specific use**, not about dependency naming, locale parity, version changes, or release gates. The evidence supports a cautious rights ledger and a human review stop. It does not authorize production, clearance, legal advice, or a product feature claim.
