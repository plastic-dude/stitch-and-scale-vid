The fresh research question was: **When a pattern or campaign uses another person’s chart, photograph, video, translation, or support resource, what distinguishes credit from permission for a particular use?** The answer changed the creative from a generic source note to a visible boundary between acknowledgement and use authorization.

## Nearest prior and material difference

The nearest prior is pass 254, `dependency-disclosure-state`. Pass 254 asks what a pattern resource is for and how its source, scope, version, access, and last-check relationship should be named. Pass 255 asks whether a source may be reused in a specific way after it has been named.

| Dimension | Pass 254 `dependency-disclosure-state` | Pass 255 `attribution-boundary-state` |
|---|---|---|
| Changed pain | A supporting resource can be attached without purpose, scope, version, access, or review context. | A source can be named and linked while permission, license, permitted use, channel, term, or modification remains unresolved. |
| Hook | “A file can be attached and still not tell you what it is for.” | “A credit line can be correct and still not give you permission to use the work.” |
| Visual metaphor | A dependency card naming a resource relationship. | A two-column `CREDIT` versus `USE CHECK` card where the credit side fills first and the rights side stays unresolved. |
| Proof surface | Pattern version, resource, purpose, source, scope, last check, link label, access, alternate version, human dependency review, status. | Work, rightsholder/requested credit, source link, license or permission, allowed use, channel/term, modification, human rights review, status. |
| Emotional turn | Attachment-looking-complete becomes an explicit dependency boundary. | A tidy source line becomes visibly insufficient for a particular reuse decision. |
| Final line | “Name what the pattern depends on before you trust the link.” | “Credit the source. Clear the use. Keep the human gate.” |

The candidate is also distinct from occupied `credit-line`, `source-note`, `quote-permission`, `provenance-chain`, `permission-line`, `consent-state`, `privacy-by-default`, `dependency-state`, `support-boundary`, `cross-reference-state`, `locale-alignment-state`, `version-delta-state`, `false-completion`, and `release-gate-roster`. Those concepts address source mention, quotation permission, general provenance, consent, privacy, resource relationships, cross-locale parity, version changes, false closure, or release accountability. This pass focuses on the **semantic gap between attribution and permission for a defined use scope**.
