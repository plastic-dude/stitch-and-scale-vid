## Selection result

`price-sensitivity-state` was selected after refreshing the published branch and inspecting the 267 occupied ledger rows. No row used this exact slug. It is reserved once in pass 257 and does not reuse or rename any prior filename.

The fresh question was: **When a projected price changes because one input is estimated, variable, or incomplete, what should an independent knitwear designer record before treating the number as a final business fact?** The research changed the concept from generic price or cost capture to a visible delta between a changed input and a moving derived result.

## Nearest prior and material difference

The nearest prior is `price-basis`, with `record-estimate-state`, `assumption-state`, `cost-allocation-state`, `honest-price`, `labor-visibility-state`, `quantity-basis`, and `sensitivity-state` as adjacent territory. Pass 257 is not a restatement of what a price is based on, whether a record is an estimate, which assumption exists, how a cost is allocated, whether labor is visible, or a generic sensitivity concept. It asks: **which named input changed, what derived output moved, and who must decide before the number is treated as final?**

| Dimension | Nearest prior territory | Pass 257 `price-sensitivity-state` |
|---|---|---|
| Changed pain | A price, estimate, assumption, cost, or labor record lacks context. | A derived price looks final while one input is estimated or changes independently. |
| Hook | Naming the basis or cost. | “A price can move before the label does.” |
| Visual metaphor | Basis card, estimate record, labor ledger, or generic sensitivity marker. | Split `OUTPUT` / `INPUT CHANGED` card; one input swaps and the output marker moves to `NOT FINAL — DECISION NEEDED`. |
| Proof surface | Basis, estimate, assumption, cost, labor, or generic result fields. | `OUTPUT / INPUT CHANGED / OLD VALUE / NEW VALUE / SOURCE / RECORDED-ESTIMATED-PLANNED / EFFECT ON RESULT / ASSUMPTION / DECISION OWNER`. |
| Emotional turn | A number gains context. | A precise-looking total refuses false finality when its most influential input moves. |
| Final line | Context-specific prior line. | “Before you call it a price, show me what moved.” |

The candidate is distinct from occupied `price-basis`, `record-estimate-state`, `assumption-state`, `assumption-register`, `cost-allocation-state`, `honest-price`, `labor-visibility-state`, `quantity-basis`, `sensitivity-state`, `input-state`, `decision-basis-state`, `comparison-basis`, and `result-boundary-state`. It is also separate from pass 256 `size-exception-state`, which concerns a grading breakpoint and affected construction pieces rather than business math.

## Dramatic engine choice

**Subject:** A fictional `PRICE SENSITIVITY` card with one output marker and one changing input.
**Desire:** Know whether a projected outcome is stable enough to discuss or still needs a human decision.
**Obstacle:** Material, labor, overhead, fee, volume, or sales inputs can be estimated or move independently while the total retains a false aura of precision.
**Stakes:** A designer may treat a derived estimate as a recorded business fact. The film must not claim that this occurred to a Stitch & Scale user.
**Change:** Move from one polished total to a visible input delta, source/state label, affected output, and decision owner.
**Contradiction:** A more precise-looking number can be less honest if its most important input is hidden.
**Escalation:** Stable output card; one input changes; output marker moves; source and state appear; `NOT FINAL — DECISION NEEDED` replaces any approval cue.
**Final image:** `INPUT CHANGED → OUTPUT MOVED → HUMAN DECISION`, with `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.
