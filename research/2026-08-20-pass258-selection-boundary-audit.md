## Selection result

`device-exit-state` was selected after refreshing the published branch and inspecting the 268 occupied ledger rows. No row used this exact slug. It is reserved once in pass 258 and does not reuse or rename any prior filename.

The fresh question was: **When an independent designer leaves, replaces, loses, or stops trusting a device, what must be verified so local-first records remain owned, readable, exportable, and recoverable without implying cloud sync or guaranteed backup?** The research changed the concept from generic local ownership to a visible boundary between possessing a local copy and having evidence that the record can leave the device and still be interpreted.

## Nearest prior and material difference

The nearest prior is `restore-boundary-state`, with adjacent `backup-state`, `recovery-proof`, `offline-continuity`, `portability-state`, `export-portability`, `export-identity-state`, `custody-state`, and `request-ownership-state`. Those concepts concern restore evidence, backup, recovery, offline continuity, general portability, export identity, custody, or ownership requests. Pass 258 is not a restore proof or an export feature claim. It asks what an owner should verify **before leaving a device**: local copy, export, format/version, fixity, destination, readability, backup state, and human confirmation.

| Dimension | Nearest prior territory | Pass 258 `device-exit-state` |
|---|---|---|
| Changed pain | A backup, restore, export, recovery, or ownership state lacks context. | Local possession is mistaken for a trustworthy exit from a device. |
| Hook | Prove the backup, restore, export, or ownership state. | “Having the file is not the same as knowing you can leave the device.” |
| Visual metaphor | Recovery boundary, portability record, custody card, or backup state. | An original `DEVICE EXIT` card moves from `LOCAL COPY` to `EXPORT`, then asks `CAN YOU OPEN IT?` and `DID YOU TEST THE RESTORE?`. |

| Emotional turn | A state gains recovery or ownership context. | Possession becomes a testable exit boundary without promising durability. |

The candidate is distinct from occupied `backup-state`, `recovery-proof`, `restore-boundary-state`, `offline-continuity`, `portability-state`, `export-portability`, `export-identity-state`, `custody-state`, `request-ownership-state`, `local-state`, `data-loss`, and `device-state`. It is also distinct from pass 257 `price-sensitivity-state`, which concerns changing business inputs and derived outputs rather than data custody or device migration.

## Dramatic engine choice

**Subject:** A fictional `DEVICE EXIT` card, blank removable-drive-shaped prop, and neutral device silhouette.
**Desire:** Leave a device while retaining evidence that the record remains owned, readable, and inspectable.
**Obstacle:** A local copy, export button, or file presence does not by itself prove format readability, fixity, destination, backup, or restoration.
**Stakes:** A designer may believe the work is safe because it is present on one device. The film must not claim this happened to a Stitch & Scale user.
**Change:** Move from possession to a named exit checklist and human confirmation.
**Contradiction:** Local-first ownership increases agency but also makes responsibility visible.
**Escalation:** Local copy; export; format/version; checksum/fixity; readability test; backup state; restore test remains separate; human confirmation.
**Final image:** `LOCAL COPY → EXPORT → READABILITY TEST → HUMAN CONFIRMATION`, with `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI`.
