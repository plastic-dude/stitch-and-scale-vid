# Pass 264 Wide Research — Outsider Position State

**Research date:** 2026-08-20
**Pass:** 264
**Question:** How can a developer transparently name limited lived craft experience, a family connection, and a product boundary without borrowing maker identity or turning personal history into authority?

## Decision summary

The research changes the concept from generic founder disclosure to an **outsider-position boundary**. David can explain that his late mother knitted and that this is part of why the problem matters, while also stating that he is a developer, does not knit, and is not a knitwear designer, pattern designer, technical editor, or test knitter. The story must turn toward the designers and makers who can correct, shape, and review the product rather than use the family connection as borrowed authority.

The planned illustrative proof surface is an `OUTSIDER POSITION` card with `ROLE: DEVELOPER`, `MOTIVATION: FAMILY CONNECTION`, `WHAT I KNOW`, `WHAT I DO NOT KNOW`, `WHO CHECKS`, `WHAT I AM ASKING`, and `STATUS`. This is not a current Stitch & Scale product UI or community-ownership feature. It will carry `ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT PRODUCT UI` and remain blocked.

## Audience and campaign interpretation

The audience pain is not that a founder has an unusual origin story. It is the trust question that follows: who is speaking, what do they actually know, whose work is being represented, who gets to correct the product, and what is being asked of the community? A family connection can explain motivation; it cannot substitute for lived craft practice, technical editing, test knitting, community ownership, or current product proof.

The standing campaign objective remains recruiting founding testers through the free demo and early-access cohort. No event, seasonal deadline, competitor comparison, testimonial, urgency hook, adoption statement, customer outcome, capability, or authority claim is adopted. The invitation is to designers and makers who can identify what the product gets wrong or omits; it does not promise that their input will ship, be paid, or produce a particular outcome.

## Product-truth reconciliation

No current Stitch & Scale product-proof registry entry or approved non-private capture verifies an outsider-position record, role field, community-review relationship, contribution ownership, or product surface that operationalizes this story. Therefore the package is **blocked**. The paper card is not current product UI and must not be presented as a community co-design engine, tester roster, consent workflow, compensation system, or product capability.

The concept must not claim that David represents knitters, speaks for designers, has lived craft expertise, has tested patterns, performs technical editing, owns community stories, guarantees co-design, or can promise that feedback will be implemented. Technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, localization review, and test knitting remain distinct human evidence gates.

## Rights, visual opportunity, and asset decisions

Use an original paper `OUTSIDER POSITION` card, one blank “what I do not know” strip, one blank “who checks” strip, and one neutral pencil with no real maker’s photo, family photo, private message, customer story, craft artifact, community quote, product capture, or third-party media. Do not use the late mother’s name, image, handwriting, private materials, or personal records without explicit clearance. If current product capture or tester/co-design evidence is ever supplied, verify version, destination, privacy, consent/rights, ownership, exact labels, and whether the story belongs to the people represented before use.

The visual opportunity is a neat founder card that initially reads `FOUNDER STORY`; the hand crosses it out and replaces it with `POSITION`. The card fills `ROLE: DEVELOPER`, `MOTIVATION: FAMILY CONNECTION`, `WHAT I KNOW: THE PROBLEM MATTERS`, `WHAT I DO NOT KNOW: THE MAKER’S WORK`, `WHO CHECKS: DESIGNERS / MAKERS`, and `WHAT I AM ASKING: CORRECT ME`. The emotional turn is from origin-story credibility to accountable participation.

## Platform, caption, audio, and acceptance decisions

Use a mobile-native 9:16 master under 45 seconds with one founder narrator, one microphone family, one room tone, and one pencil-on-paper sound motif. Captions may not cover the role, limitation, reviewer, ask, hands, or CTA. Review caption-on and caption-free versions independently. The distinction “I am a developer; I do not knit” and the invitation “correct me” must remain understandable with audio muted and captions disabled. Reject mixed voices, doubled words, overlaps, room-tone jumps, stale UI, identity claims, community-outcome claims, unlicensed audio, and dense overlays.

## Research conclusion

The evidence supports the materially distinct angle `outsider-position-state`: show a developer making his role and limits explicit, refusing borrowed maker identity, and handing product authority back to the designers and makers who can review it. It does not authorize a co-design engine, tester roster, community-ownership workflow, consent feature, compensation promise, or product capability claim. The package remains blocked until current product proof, rights/consent, platform, caption, audio, and acceptance evidence exist.
