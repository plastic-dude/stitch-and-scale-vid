## Caption map and accessibility

Captions contain the complete spoken dialogue and are reviewed against the handoff card. Meaningful non-speech cues may be labeled as `[file moved]`, `[context missing]`, and `[review pending]` only when they carry information. Captions remain in a declared lane that never covers `HANDOFF`, `SENDER`, `RECIPIENT`, `CONTEXT`, `VERSION`, `REQUIRED_ACTION`, `OPEN_ITEMS`, `RIGHTS`, `ACCESS_NEEDS`, `RECEIPT`, `REVIEWED`, or `DISPOSITION`, tables, numbers, labels, buttons, menus, hands, or primary actions. Produce and review caption-on and caption-free masters. The sources inform information-design and production-review boundaries only; this script does not claim a product handoff or accessibility implementation [894] [895] [896].

## Audio plan

One narrator family, one voice, no mixed speakers. Use a clear dry recording with stable room tone. The file movement is original or cleared and quiet. Hold silence at `CONTEXT: MISSING`; use a neutral review tone, not a delivered chime, notification ping, urgency cue, access-denied cue, or approval sound. Reject mixed voices, doubled words, overlaps, room-tone jumps, unlicensed music, or audio that implies product capability.

## Alternatives

If motion reduces legibility, use a static sender-to-recipient diptych with recipient, context, version, required action, open items, rights, access needs, receipt, review, and disposition centered. If a real product capture is later supplied, do not insert it automatically; first record exact labels, version/date, rights, and changed claims, then rerun Director review. If the example uses a pattern handoff, preserve calculation, technical editing, physical print review, chart readability, schematic scale, and test knitting as distinct human evidence gates; a handoff does not replace any of them.
