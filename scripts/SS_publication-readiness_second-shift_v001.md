## Voice direction

One narrator family. First person. Close, dry, unhurried, and specific. The voice is a developer speaking with craft respect, not a craftsperson performing authority. No motivational swell. Let the first line arrive after the fabric sound. Leave a small breath before the final invitation.

## Caption text

Use the narration verbatim. Do not add feature labels that are not spoken. For non-speech audio, use only `[paper folds]` or `[fabric rustle]` when needed for comprehension. Keep captions to two lines maximum where practical. Use lower safe zone only when no proof, hands, buttons, numbers, or table occupy that area; otherwise use the upper safe zone or cut to a clean frame.

## 30-second vertical adaptation

Keep the contradiction and the human gate. Remove the operational-record line and compress the proof sequence:

> “You finished the pattern. Then the pattern asks what ‘finished’ means. Every size, every warning, every note that still needs a person. Stitch & Scale gives those questions somewhere visible to live. And when the work needs a human check, it can say that too. Try the demo. Tell us where the work still breaks.”

The 9:16 edit must use true vertical screen captures and a separate close proof shot. It must not be a crop of the 16:9 master.

## 15-second square adaptation

Use only if the proof remains readable:

> “A finished pattern can still have unfinished questions. Inspect the work, record the evidence, and keep the human checks visible. Try the Stitch & Scale demo.”

If the square cut requires captions over proof or reduces UI text below legibility, do not ship it.

## Forbidden substitutions

Do not replace “can say that too” with “automatically catches everything,” “ready to publish,” “error-free,” “guaranteed fit,” “saves hours,” or any claim of adoption, revenue, accuracy, or final pricing. Do not describe David as a knitter, knitwear designer, or technical editor.
